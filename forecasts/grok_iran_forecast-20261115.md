**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of all the following for at least 30 consecutive days (or the equivalent continuous condition at longer horizons):

- **Direct military activity**: No significant kinetic exchanges (missile, drone, or air strikes on territory, bases, or major assets) between US/Israeli forces and Iranian forces. No large-scale operations. Limited deniable incidents do not count as normality.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthi, Iraqi militia, and other proxy attacks on shipping or regional targets reduced to pre-February 2026 sporadic levels (near zero monthly incidents of significance).
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and nearby waters)**: Confirmed commercial-vessel attacks or mine strikes averaging <1 per month; effective freedom of navigation without mandatory restricted routes, AIS-off requirements, or routine naval escorts for ordinary commercial traffic.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily AIS-visible and confirmed commercial transits through Hormuz recovered to ≥80% of the pre-conflict baseline (roughly 70–100+ vessels per day versus current levels of ~6–35 depending on source and AIS status).
- **War-risk insurance**: Additional premiums for standard Hormuz/Gulf voyages reduced to ≤1% of hull value (from recent 7–10% range), with broad market availability and no widespread cancellations.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Aggregate Gulf oil (and condensate/products) flows equivalent to ≥90% of pre-war Hormuz throughput (~15–20+ mb/d scale), with Qatari and other LNG under normal (non-force-majeure) conditions and no major port or terminal shutdowns.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent and LNG pricing reflecting non-crisis fundamentals without sustained large risk premia attributable to Hormuz; volatility indices consistent with ordinary geopolitical noise.
- **Force posture**: US/Coalition naval and air presence reduced from peak crisis levels; Iranian IRGC naval harassment and mining activity ceased or at negligible levels; Gulf state alerts and shelter orders rare.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No new territorial strikes, nuclear advances that restart cycles, or large proxy activations.

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price decline, or battlefield pause alone does not satisfy the definition. Commercial and insurance normalization are gating.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of ~15 November 2026)**  
The conflict began 28 February 2026 with US-Israeli strikes. A June 2026 memorandum of understanding for a 60-day ceasefire lapsed in August without a broader deal. The US maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports while facilitating some commercial transits.  

UKMTO and related reporting indicate Hormuz transits remain ~65% below pre-conflict levels. Kpler and similar trackers have shown oil flows varying from roughly 48% to higher partial recoveries of pre-war volumes (~8–14 mb/d range in recent periods versus ~17 mb/d baseline). War-risk premiums are quoted in the 7–9% (and up to 10%) of hull-value range versus ~0.25% pre-war. Brent has traded in the low-to-mid $90s recently after higher peaks.  

Confirmed and claimed activity continues at low intensity: IRGC drone claims/attacks toward Bahrain (US CENTCOM reports shoot-downs with no damage), unverified Iranian mine-strike claims on tankers, occasional US strikes on Iranian targets (e.g., Qeshm area), and Houthi activity. Diplomacy via Qatar and others continues but positions remain far apart (Iran’s conditions for full Hormuz reopening versus US emphasis on economic pressure and nuclear restrictions). No major combat operations are currently underway; US officials have described economic pressure as the primary tool. Evidence is drawn from UKMTO, UN Security Council reporting, Kpler/Windward/UANI tracking, insurer commentary, and major news organizations. Iranian claims of mine strikes and radar hits are frequently unverified or denied; US assessments of mine clearance and transit facilitation are weighted more heavily where they conflict.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  

- **1980s Tanker War (1984–1988)**: Systematic shipping attacks in the Gulf; oil continued to flow with escorts and higher costs; full stabilization followed the 1988 ceasefire. Relevant for prolonged harassment under mutual interest in pressure. Breaks down: different primary attackers (Iraq), pre-modern drone/mine scale, and eventual formal end to the broader war. Suggests multi-month to multi-year timelines if low-intensity attrition continues; partial commercial flow possible earlier.  
- **Houthi Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb crisis (2023–2026)**: Attacks caused sustained diversions; even after multi-month pauses and declarations, Suez/Bab traffic remained 50–60%+ below normal for quarters. Highly relevant for insurance and owner behavior lag. Breaks down: alternative Cape route exists (none for Hormuz). Suggests quarters to a year or more for full commercial normalization after kinetic pause.  
- **2019 Gulf tanker attacks and Soleimani period**: Brief risk spikes, insurance elevation for weeks to months, rapid de-escalation without sustained closure. Relevant for short-cycle risk. Breaks down: no blockade or multi-month campaign. Suggests weeks to a few months for insurance and transit recovery if attacks stop cleanly.  
- **Post-1991 Gulf War / short high-intensity chokepoint events**: Rapid shipping recovery once kinetic phase ended. Relevant as upper-bound speed. Breaks down: decisive military outcome and no residual proxy/mining campaign.  
- **Insurance-market post-crisis behavior (general and Red Sea/Hormuz 2026 itself)**: Premiums rise fast and fall slowly; “quick up, slow down” is a repeated pattern. Capacity returns only with sustained incident-free periods.  

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment)**: 1 week ~1%; 1 month ~5%; 3 months ~15%; 6 months ~30%; 1 year ~50%; 18 months ~65%. These reflect historical lags in commercial trust, insurance, and residual harassment even after major kinetic phases end.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- US preference for economic pressure over resumed major combat (recent official statements).  
- Partial oil-flow recovery already achieved under facilitation.  
- Active (if stalled) diplomacy and mutual interest in avoiding full-scale re-escalation.  
- Gulf states’ preference for de-escalation and revenue continuity.  
- Historical precedent that oil continues to move under elevated risk when incentives align.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Persistent Iranian harassment capability and incentive (leverage via Hormuz).  
- High and sticky war-risk premiums and owner caution (Red Sea precedent).  
- Unresolved political conditions (Iran’s demands, nuclear file, blockade).  
- Proxy residual capacity (Houthis and others).  
- Mine and restricted-route uncertainty; AIS-off traffic complicates assessment.  
- Insurance and commercial trust lag even after kinetic pauses.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

Synthesis treats insurance normalization and sustained safe high-volume transit as primary gating factors. Partial military pauses or oil-price moves do not unlock the joint outcome. Final figures are judgmentally combined (not arithmetic averages), with heavier weight on the slowest commercial/insurance components and residual political risk.

**• 1 Week**  
- Probability: 1%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~1%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly higher near-term kinetic pause probability offset by ongoing incidents and insurance levels.  
- Core rationale: Current low-level harassment, high premiums, and transit shortfalls make simultaneous satisfaction of the operational definition extremely unlikely within days.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance and transit volume.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Immediate verified ceasefire on shipping plus insurer announcements of major premium cuts.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Confirmed large mine strikes or new territorial exchanges.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**• 1 Month**  
- Probability: 4%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward for sticky insurance and unresolved conditions.  
- Core rationale: Even a clean kinetic pause would leave insurance and owner behavior lagging; current incident rate and blockade make the full definition improbable.  
- Main gating factor: War-risk insurance and commercial transit recovery.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Sustained 30-day incident-free period plus clear diplomatic sequencing that reduces premiums.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumption of significant strikes or new proxy campaign.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-High  

**• 3 Months**  
- Probability: 12%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~15%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest downward due to unresolved political conditions and historical commercial lag.  
- Core rationale: Possible further kinetic de-escalation and partial flow recovery are plausible, but full insurance normalization and high-volume unrestricted transit remain unlikely without a broader arrangement.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization and proxy/shipping security.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Formal understanding that ends harassment and blockade elements, triggering rapid premium decline.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Continued or rising incidents, or Israeli/US decision for renewed major operations.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**• 6 Months**  
- Probability: 28%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~30%

- Core rationale: Window for a limited deal or sustained pressure-induced restraint opens; Red Sea-style lag still constrains full commercial/insurance recovery.  
- Main gating factor: Sustained safe transit volume plus insurance.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Verified multi-month low-incident period and premium drop below 2%.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: New escalation cycle or Iranian reconstitution of effective denial capability.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium

- Core rationale: Historical pattern of eventual commercial adaptation or settlement becomes more relevant; cumulative probability of either a deal or effective attrition-induced restraint rises. Full definition still requires all domains.  
- Main gating factor: Absence of new major escalation triggers combined with insurance/transit.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Durable political arrangement or demonstrated Iranian inability to sustain harassment.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Protracted low-level campaign or nuclear-related re-escalation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-Low  

**• 18 Months**  
- Probability: 62%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~65%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest downward for structural residual risks (proxies, insurance memory, unresolved issues).  
- Core rationale: Longer horizon allows more paths (deal, attrition success, mutual exhaustion). Commercial systems adapt even under residual risk; full simultaneous normality remains non-trivial.  
- Main gating factor: Durable reduction in harassment capability or incentives.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Formal end to hostilities with verified mine clearance and force reductions.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Entrenched low-intensity conflict or new regional war trigger.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Low-Medium  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-intensity attrition through late 2026 into 2027: limited US economic pressure and selective facilitation, Iranian deniable harassment, partial oil flows at elevated cost, high but gradually moderating insurance, and intermittent diplomacy without rapid breakthrough. Full functional normality most probable in the 12–18 month window if no major re-escalation occurs.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
A sequenced diplomatic understanding (Qatar-mediated or similar) that trades limited nuclear/sanctions concessions for verified end to harassment and partial blockade relief. This could compress insurance and transit recovery into 3–6 months, raising the 6-month probability substantially. Requires both sides accepting incomplete wins and no Israeli unilateral action.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iranian reconstitution of denial capacity, Israeli pressure for resumed strikes, or a nuclear/IAEA flashpoint restarts higher-intensity exchanges. Commercial traffic and insurance remain suppressed for 18+ months; oil flows stay structurally lower with persistent premia. Historical Tanker War duration and Red Sea lag support this tail.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that the forecast overestimates normalization: residual Iranian incentives to retain Hormuz leverage, insurance market stickiness (documented “slow down” pattern), and proxy autonomy could keep the full definition unmet even at 18 months. Strongest case that it underestimates: US economic pressure may degrade Iranian capability faster than assumed, and partial commercial adaptation (already visible in some flow recoveries) could meet a slightly looser operational standard sooner. Load-bearing assumptions are (1) no major Israeli-driven re-escalation and (2) insurance responding to incident-free periods within historical ranges. Evidence that would prove the view wrong includes either a sudden formal deal with rapid premium collapse or a clear multi-month rise in confirmed high-impact shipping attacks.
