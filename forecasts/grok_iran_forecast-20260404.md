**Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
“Return to functional normality” is defined operationally and observably across domains as:  
- **Direct military activity**: Zero confirmed US/Israeli strikes on Iranian targets or Iranian missile/drone/naval attacks on Gulf shipping/assets for ≥30 consecutive days.  
- **Proxy activity**: Hezbollah/Houthi/militia attacks on Gulf or related targets at ≤5 incidents per month (verified via UKMTO/CENTCOM reporting).  
- **Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Sustained transit with <1 confirmed attack per month and US/coalition naval escort presence at pre-war levels.  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Average daily vessel crossings ≥100 (≥80% of pre-war baseline of ~135–138), sustained for ≥30 days (via AIS/tanker-tracker data).  
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums ≤0.5% of hull value (renewable without special US backstop), full P&I coverage available without force majeure clauses.  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (Saudi, Qatar, UAE, Kuwait, Iraq) at ≥90% of pre-war volumes (IEA/Kpler data); no force majeure declarations; Iranian exports secondary.  
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent crude volatility index ≤ historical peacetime average (~15–20%); no sustained supply shock premiums.  
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Israeli/Gulf forces at DEFCON-equivalent 4 or below with partial drawdowns; no heightened alerts or mine-laying.  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No strikes on Kharg Island oil facilities, Ras Tanura, Ras Laffan, or equivalent for ≥60 days.  

A temporary ceasefire or battlefield pause does not qualify. Lower oil prices alone do not qualify. Commercial normalization must lag military pauses by weeks due to insurer and buyer risk aversion.

**Current Situation Snapshot**  
As of 4 April 2026 (~Day 37 since onset), the Strait of Hormuz remains under Iran’s selective/permission-based transit regime (routed via Larak Island). Open commercial transits averaged 2–5 vessels per day in the latest JMIC/Windward aggregates, with ghost-fleet Iranian oil to China as the dominant flow; limited southern Omani-water routing observed. The Malta-flagged French-owned CMA CGM container ship Kribi successfully transited on 2 April—the first French-owned vessel since hostilities began—signaling selective tolerance for certain non-hostile flags. No verified large-scale coalition escorted convoys have executed. War-risk premiums have stabilized near 1% of hull value in active syndicates (with Chubb/US-backed reinsurance now available), yet safety-driven crew refusals and P&I restrictions continue to suppress volumes. Iran and Oman are actively drafting a joint protocol to “monitor transit” through the Strait (IRNA, 2 April). President Trump reiterated on 1 April that any ceasefire requires the Strait to be “open, free, and clear,” with the conditional reprieve set to expire 6 April; he referenced a request from Iran’s new regime president. Houthi Bab el-Mandeb readiness statements remain unchanged. Ras Laffan damage (17% Qatar LNG capacity loss) and force majeure declarations are unchanged, with 3–5 year repairs projected.

Recovery: commercial volumes and insurance terms returned to baseline within 1–3 months after de-escalation. Breaks down due to far lower intensity and absence of state-on-state missile exchanges. Partial normalization: 6–12 months before premiums fell and schedules stabilized. Breaks down because scale was smaller and no direct Iranian state involvement. Suggests commercial recovery trails military calm by months.  

4. **1991 Gulf War**: Relevant for rapid kinetic phase followed by coalition naval clearance. Timeline: 1–3 months to restored transit and insurance norms. Breaks down because current conflict lacks decisive ground component or immediate regime outcome. Suggests faster recovery only under clear military resolution.  

5. **2003 Iraq War opening phase**: Relevant for Gulf-area port and export interruptions during regime-change fighting. Recovery: shipping and volumes normalized in 2–6 months once major combat subsided. Breaks down because Hormuz itself was never closed.
