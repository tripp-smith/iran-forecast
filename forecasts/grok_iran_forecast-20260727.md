**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War, by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (as of 27 July 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of all of the following observable conditions for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot):

- Direct military activity: No sustained US/Iran kinetic exchanges (airstrikes, missile/drone attacks on territory or major bases) beyond isolated, contained incidents; no major new campaign phases.
- Proxy activity: No significant Houthi or other Iranian-aligned attacks on commercial shipping or energy infrastructure in the Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb or Gulf that disrupt flows at scale.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial vessels transit freely without requirement for Iranian “permits,” Iranian-designated routes only, or routine IRGC interdiction/warning shots/mines claims; confirmed absence of active mining threats that deter traffic.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits through Hormuz recover to ≥80% of pre-war baseline (~110+ of the ~130–140 vessels/day), including substantial non-Iranian/Chinese-linked tanker and LNG traffic, sustained over consecutive weeks.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war-risk premiums fall to ≤2× pre-war levels (pre-war ~0.25% of hull value; current multiples ~8× or higher with voyage costs up to ~$2.5M for VLCCs) and coverage is widely available without mass club withdrawals or voyage-by-voyage restrictions that price out ordinary commercial operators.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf exporters (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Iraq) restore loadings and loadings exit the Gulf at volumes approaching pre-war levels without large-scale rerouting via Sumed, Red Sea alternatives, or prolonged backlog; Iranian exports normalized under any prevailing sanctions regime without blockade enforcement dominating.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent crude risk premium attributable to Hormuz/Gulf security falls such that prices stabilize without repeated $10+/week spikes driven by transit fears; timespreads and freight rates normalize.
- Force posture of major actors: US force levels and operational tempo in the Gulf return toward pre-escalation steady-state (no continuous multi-week strike campaigns); Iranian IRGC Navy posture shifts from active control enforcement to routine presence.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No successful major attacks on energy infrastructure, mass casualty events on shipping, nuclear-related incidents, or Gulf-state territory strikes that restart the cycle.

A temporary ceasefire, partial traffic rebound under military escort, lower oil prices alone, or battlefield pause without commercial/insurance recovery does **not** meet the standard.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (with breakdowns):

- 1980–88 Tanker War (Iran-Iraq): Repeated attacks on shipping in Gulf/Hormuz approaches; traffic adapted with escorts/rerouting but never fully normalized until war end. Partial commercial recovery lagged military pauses by months; full normalization only after 1988 ceasefire + demining. Suggests 6–18+ months post-major hostilities for functional commercial recovery.
- 2019–2020 Iran tanker attacks / Gulf tensions (and 2019 Saudi infrastructure attack): Limited kinetic window; insurance spiked, traffic slowed but recovered in weeks–months once attacks ceased and no sustained control claim. Breaks down because no multi-month full closure or dual blockade + proxy expansion.
- Red Sea/Houthi campaign 2023–2025: Sustained proxy attacks on commercial shipping; major carriers avoided route for months–>1 year; insurance elevated; partial recovery only with military pressure + de-escalation. Normalization incomplete even after peaks. Suggests multi-month lag for insurance/transit confidence.
- 1990–91 Gulf War / post-invasion mine clearance and shipping restoration: Post-kinetic demining and confidence restoration took weeks to months for full commercial return despite military victory.
- Broader chokepoint disruptions (Suez 2021, historical mining): Physical clearance faster than commercial/insurance confidence; 1–6 months typical for volume recovery after clear security signal.

Base-rate prior (pre-current evidence, pure reference-class average for demanding multi-domain normality after sustained Gulf maritime war with control contest):  
1 week ~1–3%; 1 month ~5–10%; 3 months ~15–25%; 6 months ~30–45%; 1 year ~50–65%; 18 months ~60–75%. Heavy discount for cases involving contested sovereignty claims over the strait itself.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Durable security guarantee that removes Iranian ability/incentive to enforce exclusive control (US military degradation of IRGC Navy assets + diplomatic face-saver).  
2. Insurance market response to sustained incident-free transit (rapid premium compression once volume returns).  
3. Gulf-state and major-buyer (Asia) pressure for volume restoration + alternative infrastructure acceleration as backstop.  
4. Mutual exhaustion / political incentive for Trump administration and Iranian leadership to lock in a workable Hormuz formula.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Iranian strategic view of Hormuz control as core regime leverage and deterrent (load-bearing; repeatedly stated).  
2. Fragility of any pause: rapid return to tit-for-tat once one side perceives advantage or domestic pressure.  
3. Insurance and charterer risk aversion: commercial thresholds higher than pure military pause.  
4. Proxy expansion (Houthis) and secondary fronts raising multi-domain costs.  
5. Domestic Iranian politics post-leadership changes and US political calendar constraints on sustained high-tempo ops.

- What would lower: Any new vessel strike or US strike resumption.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Intermittent pauses and limited diplomatic tracks (Oman-mediated) produce episodic traffic rebounds under residual risk, but full multi-domain normality remains elusive through 2026. Insurance and charterers stay cautious. Gradual degradation of Iranian maritime capabilities + Gulf-state pressure yields a workable (not ideal) transit regime by mid-to-late 2027, with elevated baseline risk premiums persisting.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Decisive US military pressure degrades IRGC Navy sufficiently that Iran accepts a non-exclusive, internationally monitored transit regime in exchange for sanctions relief/economic package. 4–8 weeks of zero incidents trigger rapid insurance compression and volume recovery. Probability of this path materializing within 3–6 months: ~15–20%.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran treats any arrangement short of recognized management role as existential; low-level attacks, mining claims, and proxy actions continue indefinitely. Traffic remains suppressed or highly managed; insurance stays elevated; energy markets retain structural risk premium. This is the stronger near-term risk.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Overestimation risk: June rebound showed commercial actors can move quickly when a security signal appears; current pause + Oman talks could be the start of a durable formula faster than reference classes suggest. Underestimation risk: Iranian leadership views Hormuz control as non-negotiable residual deterrent after leadership losses and military degradation; commercial thresholds (insurance + crew willingness) are higher than pure kinetic pause implies. Load-bearing assumption is that a political/military equilibrium short of Iranian exclusive control is achievable and sustainable. If that is false, probabilities at 6+ months are too high.
