**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following to hold simultaneously and sustainably (not just a pause or partial recovery):

- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes, drone/missile exchanges, or naval clashes between Iran, US, Israel, or proxies in/near the Gulf for at least 30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Iran-backed groups (e.g., Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, PMF) with no major attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or Israel/US assets.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe, unhindered commercial transit without routine IRGC interventions, warnings, boardings, or attacks; US/Iranian blockades effectively lifted.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily vessel transits approaching pre-war norms (~50-60+ commercial vessels/day); no significant stranding of tankers or rerouting dominance.<grok:render card_id="d6d842" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">76</argument></grok:render>
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit percentages or pre-crisis baselines; full private market coverage widely available without sovereign backstops or exclusions for the Persian Gulf.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (via Hormuz) recover to ~80%+ of pre-war volumes (~17-21 mb/d oil equivalent pre-crisis); minimal reliance on bypass pipelines or dark fleet tactics for routine operations.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained spikes or contango from chokepoint risk); no major supply shocks or inventory draws attributable to Gulf disruptions.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval presence de-escalates to routine patrols; Iranian IRGC Navy returns to pre-war harassment levels or lower; no heightened alerts or blockades.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No leadership crises, nuclear breakthroughs, proxy flare-ups, or failed talks that credibly threaten the above within the horizon.

A temporary ceasefire or reduced (but ongoing) skirmishes does **not** qualify. Lower oil prices from offsets (US shale, rerouting) do not qualify.
