**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of these observable, multi-domain conditions for a sustained period (minimum 30 consecutive days without major reversal):  
- **Direct military activity**: Sustained cessation of kinetic US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory/assets and Iranian strikes or attempts on US assets or Gulf shipping/energy infrastructure. Limited defensive intercepts do not count as normality.  
- **Proxy activity**: No major Houthi, Iraqi militia, or other proxy attacks disrupting Gulf or adjacent (Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb) commercial or energy flows.  
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and nearby waters)**: No active mining, projectile attacks, or seizures on commercial vessels; transit does not require routine US naval escorts or dark (AIS-off) sailing as the dominant mode.  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Confirmed (AIS or dark-tracked) commercial vessel transits through Hormuz at ≥80% of pre-war baseline (~85 AIS-visible daily average pre-crisis), sustained.  
- **War-risk insurance**: Premiums ≤1% of hull value (roughly 4× peacetime ~0.25%) with broad, non-voyage-by-voyage availability from commercial markets (US DFC facility optional but not required).  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf (non-Iranian) oil/petroleum liquids and LNG loadings/exports at ≥90% of pre-war average, with continuous operations at major terminals (Ras Tanura, Ras Laffan, etc.) and no routine ship-to-ship workarounds or bypass dependence as primary path.  
- **Energy market stress**: Brent risk premium largely dissipated (prices driven by fundamentals rather than war disruption; historically consistent with sub-$90–95 range under stable supply).  
- **Force posture**: US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted; no multi-carrier dedicated Hormuz escort/blockade mission; Iranian IRGC naval activity returned to pre-war routine (no active denial).  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No confirmed Iranian nuclear breakout steps, large-scale attacks on Gulf infrastructure, or new regional fronts that re-trigger the above disruptions.  

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price dip, or escorted partial recovery does not qualify. Military pauses without commercial and insurance normalization fail the definition.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, 1981–88)**: Relevant for sustained maritime attacks, mining, and escort operations while oil continued to flow. Traffic never fully stopped; US escorts (Earnest Will) enabled continuity. Full commercial normality (no escorts, low risk) followed the August 1988 ceasefire within months. Suggests partial functional transit under protection in 3–6 months of reduced intensity; full normality 6–18 months after hostilities end. Breaks down: lower modern missile/drone density and no nuclear overlay.  
- **2019–2020 Hormuz/Saudi tanker attacks**: Short, discrete incidents; flows recovered within weeks of de-escalation. Suggests 1–3 months for partial recovery once attacks stop. Breaks down: far smaller scale than 2026 sustained campaign.  
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Prolonged insurance spikes, diversions, partial escort/reroute adaptation over 12–24 months without full closure. Suggests 6–18 months for insurance and routing normalization after intensity drops. Breaks down: bypass (Cape) available; Hormuz has none of comparable scale.  
- **1990–91 Gulf War and 2003 Iraq invasion**: Brief major disruptions followed by rapid (weeks–months) commercial recovery once kinetic phase ended. Suggests 1–3 months post-hostilities for oil flows. Breaks down: decisive conventional outcomes and no residual Iranian denial capability.  
- **2025 Twelve-Day War**: Minimal lasting shipping impact; rapid return. Suggests short horizons if intensity collapses. Breaks down: no Hormuz closure or sustained blockade.  

**Base-rate prior** (pre-current-evidence adjustment, weighted toward Tanker War + Red Sea for residual risk, Gulf Wars for post-kinetic recovery):  
1 week: 2%; 1 month: 8%; 3 months: 25%; 6 months: 40%; 1 year: 55%; 18 months: 65%.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Active diplomatic channel and mutual interest in de-escalation (Iran economic pressure; US midterm/energy cost sensitivity).  
2. Demonstrated ability to restore substantial oil flows under escort (proves technical feasibility).  
3. Iranian conventional naval degradation (US claims of sunk assets) and mine-clearance success.  
4. Insurance market and US DFC facility keeping cover available (expensive but not zero).  
5. Shared interest in avoiding wider regional war among Gulf states.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Residual Iranian denial capacity (mines, drones, missiles, dark-fleet targeting) and political incentive to retain Hormuz leverage.  
2. Unresolved nuclear stockpile and inspection issues as escalation trigger.  
3. High US force posture and blockade costs creating open-ended mission.  
4. Infrastructure damage requiring multi-year repair independent of ceasefire.  
5. Proxy activity (Houthis) and domestic Iranian pressures that can re-trigger strikes.  
6. Insurance and commercial risk aversion lagging military pauses by months.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued limited kinetic exchanges and escorted/dark partial flows for weeks to months, with intermittent diplomatic progress. Gradual insurance easing and traffic increase if a durable pause holds after any post-midterm window. Full multi-domain normality more likely in the 6–18 month range than sooner, with residual elevated risk and occasional incidents.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Rapid mutual stand-down post-talks, verified mine clearance, blockade lift, and Iranian restraint on denial. Insurance markets reprice quickly on 30–60 days of calm. Oil flows already prove feasible. Probability mass shifts earlier by 15–20 points if observed.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Nuclear leverage or domestic Iranian pressures trigger renewed intensity; Houthi/proxy fronts expand; US maintains open-ended blockade/escorts for cost or policy reasons; infrastructure damage and risk aversion keep insurance and dark sailing dominant. Commercial normality delayed beyond 18 months.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case for faster: Markets and flows have already shown resilience; both sides have incentives to claim victory and reduce costs; history of rapid post-kinetic recovery in Gulf cases. I may underweight this. Strongest case for slower: Hormuz has no easy bypass, residual asymmetric capabilities are cheap to maintain, nuclear stockpile remains a trigger, and insurance lags are structural. I may overestimate the speed at which escorted flows convert to true normality. Load-bearing assumptions: that a pause will hold long enough for commercial confidence, and that nuclear issues do not re-escalate. Evidence of new nuclear activity or sustained vessel attacks would lower forecasts materially; a signed, implemented agreement with traffic data would raise them.
