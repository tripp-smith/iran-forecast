**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality means a stable, low-risk operating environment where commercial actors treat the Gulf as a routine transit and export zone, not a high-threat area. Observable thresholds (all must be substantially met for ~30+ consecutive days):

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, interceptions, or naval clashes involving US/Israel/Iran or proxies in Gulf waters or airspace.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi/Hezbollah/PMF attacks on shipping, infrastructure, or Gulf states linked to the conflict.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Free transit without Iranian/IRGC "permission," tolls, selective blocking, or credible threats; US/Iranian naval forces de-escalated.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic (tankers, LNG, containers) at ≥70-80% of pre-war baseline (~100-135 vessels/day through Hormuz).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums back to low single-digit multiples of pre-war (e.g., <0.5-1% of hull value for Gulf transits); broad availability without special reinsurance or government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exporters (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Iraq, Iran) operating at near-capacity with stable flows; no major forced rerouting or stockpiling issues.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalized (e.g., <30-day realized vol near historical Gulf baselines); no sustained premiums from chokepoint risk.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/coalition drawdown from heightened alert; Iranian naval/IRGC forces not postured for disruption; Gulf states not in emergency import/export mode.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No active negotiations collapsing into threats; no major proxy reactivation or leadership crises triggering renewed brinkmanship.

A temporary ceasefire or partial traffic resumption does not qualify. Lower oil prices alone do not qualify if driven by demand destruction rather than restored flows.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Mutual exhaustion + economic pain (Gulf states, global markets) pushing diplomacy.  
2. US naval escorts/reinsurance potential to bootstrap traffic.  
3. Iran's weakened conventional posture post-strikes.  
4. Third-party mediation (Pakistan, China interests in flows).  
5. Seasonal/Asian buyer pressure for oil.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. **Hormuz governance dispute** (Iran wants leverage/control; US rejects) — gating.  
2. Lingering proxy/escalation risks (Hezbollah, Houthis).  
3. Insurance market memory (slow to reprice down).  
4. Regime survival politics in Iran (new leadership under Mojtaba/IRGC hardline).  
5. Destroyed infrastructure + trust deficit.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate by horizon)  
- Major reduction in direct strikes: High by 1-3 months (~70-90%).  
- Sustained safe commercial transit: Low short-term (~10-40% by 3mo), medium by 6-12mo.  
- Insurance normalization: Lags traffic; ~20-50% by 6mo.  
- Oil/LNG export recovery: Faster if partial safe passage; gated by insurance/transit.  
- Proxy restraint: Medium (ongoing Lebanon).  
- Force drawdown/no new triggers: Improves with deal but fragile.

Gating: Hormuz transit + insurance + political settlement.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Fragile ceasefire holds with intermittent negotiations; partial/slow traffic resumption by 3-6 months under escorted or coordinated passages; insurance eases gradually; full commercial normalization by 9-15 months if no major breach. Persistent low-level risks.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Strong US/GCC pressure + Iranian concessions on Hormuz (e.g., for sanctions relief) + successful third-party guarantees. Traffic rebounds quickly; premiums fall. Prob ~20-25% for 6-month normality.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks collapse; Iran maintains leverage via proxies/selective disruptions; insurance stays punitive; new escalation trigger (e.g., Lebanon or Iranian internal crisis). Normalization delayed to 2+ years. Prob ~30-35% for <50% at 1 year.
