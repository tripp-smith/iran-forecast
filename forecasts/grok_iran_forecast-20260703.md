**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following to hold simultaneously and sustainably (not just a pause or temporary dip in disruption):

- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes (missiles, drones, naval engagements) between US/Israel/Gulf states and Iran or its direct forces in/near the Gulf for at least 30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant attacks or credible threats by Iran-aligned groups (e.g., Houthis, Iraqi militias, Hezbollah remnants) targeting shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US/Gulf forces.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No attacks on commercial vessels; effective de-mining (or confirmed low mine risk); freedom of navigation without forced routing, tolls/fees imposed by Iran, or coercive IRGC escorts; US/coalition presence reduced to pre-war deterrence levels without active blockade.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily transits through Hormuz at or above ~80-90% of pre-war baseline (~60-100 vessels/day average, with tanker traffic dominant), sustained for 30+ days; backlog of stranded vessels cleared.<grok:render card_id="76b5e0" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">40</argument></grok:render>
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit multiples of pre-war levels (e.g., <1-2% of hull value per transit vs. current elevated 5-10x+); Joint War Committee removes or sharply downgrades the Gulf/Hormuz as a high-risk/war zone; standard commercial coverage widely available without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (including Iran) at ~85%+ of pre-war volumes; no major force majeure declarations; stable production/refinery/port operations.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalized (e.g., no sustained spikes from Gulf-specific risk); forward curves not in backwardation driven by Hormuz fears; Asian/European buyers resuming normal contracting without premiums for alternative routing.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval/air assets draw down toward pre-Feb 2026 levels; Iranian IRGC Navy returns to routine (non-coercive) patrols; no dual blockades.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No unresolved nuclear breakthroughs, major proxy flare-ups (e.g., Lebanon), or infrastructure attacks that could rapidly reverse gains. Temporary ceasefires or lower oil prices alone do **not** count.<grok:render card_id="6d20d4" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render>

This definition is deliberately demanding—it requires commercial and risk-market confidence to match military de-escalation, reflecting how insurance, shipping firms, and buyers behave conservatively post-disruption.
