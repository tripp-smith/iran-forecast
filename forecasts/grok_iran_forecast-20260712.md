**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following to hold simultaneously and sustainably (not a temporary lull):

- **Direct military activity**: No US/Israeli strikes on Iranian targets or Iranian missile/drone attacks on Gulf states/US assets for at least 30 consecutive days; no active naval engagements.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Iran-backed groups (e.g., no major Houthi or other attacks disrupting shipping or Gulf infrastructure).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No verified attacks on commercial vessels; freedom of navigation without Iranian-imposed routing/permits/tolls enforced by force; US/Gulf naval presence supports but does not dominate routine transit.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily transits return to ≥70-80% of pre-war norms (~60-100+ vessels/day sustained); stranded vessels cleared.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums normalize to low single-digit multiples of pre-war levels (e.g., <<1% of hull value per transit); broad private market availability without government backstops dominating.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (including via Hormuz) recover to ≥80% of pre-war volumes; no major force majeure declarations or storage-driven curtailments.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent and regional benchmarks show low volatility (e.g., no repeated >$10-15/bbl swings tied to Gulf events); no sustained premiums from supply fears.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Coalition drawdown from heightened alert; Iranian IRGC maritime forces de-escalated; Gulf states reduce defensive mobilizations.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No significant incidents (e.g., high-casualty attacks, mine-laying, nuclear-related developments) for the period.

A ceasefire or pause alone does **not** suffice if insurance remains prohibitive, transits stay depressed, or low-level threats persist.
