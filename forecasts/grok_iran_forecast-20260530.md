**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality means observable, sustained multi-domain recovery to pre-war (pre-Feb 28, 2026) operational baselines, not a temporary pause. It requires all of the following simultaneously for at least 30 consecutive days:  
- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, mine-laying, or harassment by state actors (US, Iran, proxies) in the Gulf/Hormuz.  
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi/Hezbollah/IRGC-QF attacks on shipping, ports, or energy infrastructure.  
- **Shipping security**: Safe commercial transit through Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters without escorts, attacks, or coercion; daily transits ≥80% of pre-war average (~100-140 vessels/day).  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Volume and diversity of traffic (tankers, bulk, containers) restored; no widespread rerouting or avoidance.  
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit basis points (e.g., ~0.15-0.5% of hull value); standard private coverage widely available without government backstops.  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Persian Gulf exports at ≥85% pre-war levels; no forced storage cuts or major buyer avoidance.  
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Oil prices and volatility near pre-war norms (absent other global shocks); no sustained premia from Gulf risk.  
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Iran/Gulf states de-escalated; no heightened alerts, blockades, or forward deployments beyond routine.  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No active disputes over nuclear stockpiles, strait "control"/tolls, or reconstitution that risk rapid reversion.<grok:render card_id="b3c29c" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">31</argument></grok:render>

A ceasefire or lower oil prices alone does not qualify. Temporary lulls or selective Iranian "escorts" do not count.

Breaks down: modern precision weapons, US direct involvement, global supply chains more sensitive. - **1991 Gulf War**: Coalition victory; shipping normalized in weeks-months as mines cleared and threats removed. Relevant for US-led ops; less relevant due to decisive defeat vs. current stalemate.  
- **Red Sea/Houthi disruptions (2023-)**: Persistent low-level attacks delayed full normalization 12+ months despite naval responses.  
- **Post-major chokepoint crises (e.g., Suez 1967-75, other conflicts)**: Commercial/insurance recovery lags military pauses by 3-12 months.  
- **Recent Iran-US/Israel flare-ups**: Quick de-escalation but lingering caution.  

**Base-rate prior** (before case evidence): Very low at short horizons (~5-15% by 1-3 months), rising slowly (30-50% by 6-12 months, 60-75% by 18 months) due to historical persistence of maritime risk premiums and proxy dynamics.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Diplomatic momentum (60-day MoU, nuclear talks).  
2. Mutual economic pain (Iran storage/export issues; global prices).  
3. US/Gulf military superiority for de-mining/escorts.  
4. Alternative routing/pipeline capacity growth.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked by importance)  
1. **Core political gap on Strait "control"** — Iran seeks institutionalized leverage; US rejects (gating).  
2. **Trust/ceasefire violations** — Recent strikes amid talks.  
3. **Reconstitution & proxies** — Iran rebuilding missiles; proxy activation risk.  
4. **Insurance/market hysteresis** — Slow return even after security improves.  
5. **Nuclear/HEU disputes**.
