**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means the Persian Gulf operates as a reliable, low-friction commercial energy chokepoint with minimal disruption risk, comparable to pre-2026 baseline (pre-Feb 2026 levels adjusted for secular trends). It requires **all** of the following sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal:

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes by US/Israel/Iran or their forces on each other or Gulf infrastructure/ships in/near Hormuz.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Hezbollah/Houthi/PMF attacks attributable to Iranian direction targeting Gulf shipping, energy, or US/ally assets.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Open transit without routine Iranian permission/registration requirements, tolls/fees beyond standard international norms, or credible threats of mines/attacks. US naval blockade effectively lifted. AIS data shows routine transits.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily vessel transits (tankers, LNG, containers) return to ≥80% of pre-war average (~140-180 vessels/day baseline). No widespread rerouting around Africa.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Private market premiums return to near pre-war levels (≤0.5% of hull value per transit for most vessels; no widespread cancellations or government backstops required as primary coverage). Joint War Committee removes or sharply downgrades Gulf risk rating.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Persian Gulf exporters (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, Iran, Kuwait) achieve combined exports ≥85% of pre-war volumes without force majeure or sustained curtailments. No major stranded cargoes.
- **Energy market stress/volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility (e.g., 30-day implied vol) returns to normal pre-war ranges; no sustained risk premium (> $5-10/bbl geopolitical adder); Asian/European buyers resume normal liftings without rationing/shortages.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Iranian naval/air forces in/near Gulf de-escalate to routine patrols (no heightened alerts, blockades, or massed assets). No Iranian "permission regime" normalized.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No unresolved flashpoints (e.g., nuclear breakout, major proxy flare-up, asset freeze disputes) likely to reignite within 3-6 months. Durable MOU or ceasefire framework in place.

A temporary pause, selective transits, or lower oil prices alone do not qualify. Full commercial normalization (insurance + volumes + confidence) lags military pauses.
