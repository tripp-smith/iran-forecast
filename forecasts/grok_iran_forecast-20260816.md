**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from ~16 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires concurrent, observable conditions across domains (not a temporary ceasefire, price drop, or battlefield pause):

- Direct military activity: Sustained absence (≥30 consecutive days) of reciprocal US/Iran (or major proxy) kinetic strikes on military/energy infrastructure or commercial vessels in the Gulf/Hormuz approaches.
- Proxy activity: No significant new activation or major attacks by Iran-aligned groups (Houthis, Iraqi militias, Hezbollah) targeting Gulf energy/shipping or US forces that materially disrupt operations.
- Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Threat assessments (JMIC or equivalent) at moderate or lower for ≥30 days; no successful attacks on commercial vessels; clearance of major mine hazards in primary transit lanes.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits recovering to ≥70% of pre-war baseline (~120–130/day, i.e., sustained ≥85–90) for ≥14 consecutive days, with broad flag participation (not limited to select/shadow fleets).
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war-risk premiums ≤1% of vessel value (vs. current multi-percent/elevated multiples) for standard Hormuz transit, with capacity readily available without special consortia or heavy deductibles.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude/product/LNG loadings and Hormuz-related flows recovering to ≥80% of pre-war volumes on a sustained basis (accounting for pipeline alternatives), without major shut-ins driven by security.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent volatility normalized (no persistent war-premium spikes >$10–15 attributable primarily to Hormuz risk); no recurring supply-shock pricing.
- Force posture of major actors: Meaningful reduction in heightened combat posture (US blockade lifted or suspended; Iranian coastal strike assets not actively enforcing control; no large-scale new deployments signaling imminent major escalation).
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No unresolved core disputes (e.g., Iranian claims of administrative control/tolls vs. free navigation) producing repeated breakdowns; no new large-scale attacks on energy infrastructure.

All major elements must hold simultaneously for the definition to be met. Partial recovery in one domain (e.g., some escorted transits) does not qualify.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant historical cases (Gulf/chokepoint/maritime disruption/regional wars):

1. **Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, 1984–88)**: Prolonged attacks on shipping; traffic reduced but never fully ceased; insurance spiked; full commercial normalization lagged formal ceasefire by months to >1 year due to residual risk and posture. Suggests multi-month to 1+ year lag post-major de-escalation for functional normality.
2. **2019 Gulf tanker attacks / Abqaiq**: Short, sharp disruptions; insurance rose sharply; recovery of confidence and volumes in weeks to low months once attacks stopped and deterrence held. Breaks down because scale was far smaller and no full war/blockade.
3. **Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing elements)**: Persistent asymmetric attacks; major diversions (Cape); insurance elevated; partial recovery only with sustained escort/pressure and reduced intensity—full normality not achieved even after years of lower activity. Relevant for proxy persistence and insurance lag.
4. **2024–25 Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb peaks**: Similar insurance and routing effects; recovery incomplete without durable political settlement.
5. **Post-1991 Gulf War / Kuwait liberation and earlier Iran-Iraq maritime phases**: Infrastructure and confidence recovery measured in months to years; residual mining and force posture delayed commercial return.
6. **Suez crises / other chokepoint closures (historical)**: Even temporary closures produce multi-month insurance and routing stickiness.

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment, reflecting that commercial/insurance normalization systematically lags military pauses, and large wars with unresolved sovereignty/control issues take longer):  
- 1 week: ~1–3%  
- 1 month: ~5–10%  
- 3 months: ~15–25%  
- 6 months: ~30–45%  
- 1 year: ~50–65%  
- 18 months: ~65–80%  

These are conservative anchors given the demanding multi-domain definition.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Successful Iran-Oman technical deal + US willingness to suspend blockade in exchange for verifiable free navigation (highest leverage).  
2. Sustained mutual restraint on vessel/infrastructure attacks creating insurance confidence.  
3. Economic pressure on Iran (oil revenue, sanctions) and Gulf states/Asia buyers pushing for practical arrangements.  
4. US force presence enabling escorted corridors that gradually expand.  
5. Fatigue and domestic costs after ~6 months of conflict.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Iranian core objective of recognized administrative control/leverage over Hormuz (IRGC hardline influence).  
2. Unresolved free-navigation vs. Iranian management/toll claims producing repeated breakdowns.  
3. Insurance market stickiness and operator risk aversion (even after kinetic pause).  
4. Proxy activation risk and residual mining/coastal strike capability.  
5. Domestic political incentives on both sides against rapid full concessions.  
6. Second-order effects (Houthi Red Sea linkage, energy infrastructure vulnerability).

- What would lower: Further vessel/infrastructure strikes.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued “no war, no peace” with intermittent low-intensity clashes, Oman-mediated technical arrangements producing limited/managed corridors, gradual partial recovery in volumes via escorted/dark/pipeline routes, but insurance and operator confidence lagging. Functional normality (per definition) most likely in the 9–18 month window if economic pressure and mutual restraint align; significant risk of prolonged partial disruption.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Iran prioritizes sanctions relief/oil revenue; Oman deal expands into practical free-ish transit; US suspends blockade for verifiable compliance; 4–6 weeks of clean operations allow insurance to ease and volumes to surge. Could push 3–6 month probabilities materially higher.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
IRGC hardliners dominate; repeated attacks to enforce control claims; US maintains/ tightens blockade; proxies activate more broadly; insurance remains punitive; volumes stay suppressed into 2027. Keeps even 18-month probability below 50%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible overestimation of normalization: Underweights Iranian strategic valuation of Hormuz leverage and insurance market memory/stickiness; June collapse shows how quickly gains reverse. Possible underestimation: Underweights cumulative economic pain on Iran and Gulf producers + US ability to expand escorted capacity. Load-bearing assumptions include continued Iranian capability to disrupt at low cost and that free navigation remains a non-negotiable US red line. Evidence of sustained high-volume clean transit + premium collapse would falsify the more pessimistic tilt.
