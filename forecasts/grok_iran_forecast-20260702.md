**Step 1: Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality in the Persian Gulf means a stable, low-risk operational environment where commercial and energy activities resume at levels consistent with pre-war patterns (pre-Feb 28, 2026), without reliance on temporary pauses or exceptional measures. Observable criteria include:

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, interceptions, or exchanges between US/Israel/allies and Iran/proxies in the Gulf or immediate environs for sustained periods.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Iranian-backed groups (e.g., Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, Iraqi militias) with no major attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US assets.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Safe transit without credible threats, attacks, mining, or coercive enforcement; freedom of navigation without permission fees or selective blockades.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily transits approaching or exceeding ~80-100+ vessels (pre-war norms ~60-100+ depending on metrics), with full participation by major operators (Maersk, etc.) and no significant stranded/backlog vessels.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Standard commercial coverage widely available at pre-war or modestly elevated premiums (e.g., <0.5% of hull value per transit); no widespread cancellations, government backstops, or "warlike operations" designations by Joint War Committee/Lloyd's.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Full resumption of ~15-20 mb/d combined flows through Hormuz from Gulf producers (including Iran), with stable production/export volumes, no storage crises, and diversified routing without major disruptions.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Oil/LNG prices and implied volatility returning to pre-war ranges (e.g., Brent ~$60-90/bbl stable, low contango/backwardation extremes); no sustained supply shocks or buyer hedging panic.
- **Force posture of major actors**: Drawdown of US naval/air assets to routine levels; no dual blockades or heightened Iranian coastal missile/drone readiness; Gulf states resuming normal operations.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear site incidents, leadership assassinations, major proxy flare-ups (e.g., Lebanon), or unresolved nuclear talks derailing the process.

Temporary ceasefires, partial traffic recovery, or lower prices alone do not qualify.
