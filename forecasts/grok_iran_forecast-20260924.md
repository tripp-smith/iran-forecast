**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (begun ~28 February 2026), by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from 24 September 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of these observable, multi-domain thresholds for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No sustained US/Iran or major-party kinetic exchanges (strikes on territory, naval assets, or commercial shipping) beyond isolated incidents that are rapidly contained and do not trigger retaliation cycles. No active mining or systematic interdiction.
- Proxy activity: No significant activation or sustained attacks by Iranian-aligned proxies (Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, Iraqi militias, etc.) targeting Gulf energy infrastructure, shipping, or US/partner forces that materially disrupt operations.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial vessels transit freely on internationally recognized routes without mandatory Iranian coordination/permits, without routine projectile/drone/mine threats, and without routine US Navy escort or redirection requirements. AIS visibility and normal navigational profiles resume.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily transits of commercial vessels recover to ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (~100–130 vessels/day range; sources vary on exact pre-war average but consistently indicate severe current collapse to single digits or low teens). Sustained multi-week averages, not spikes.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war-risk premiums for Hormuz transit fall to ≤0.5–1% of hull value (vs. current elevated levels of ~5% or higher in many reports; pre-war ~0.15–0.25%). Cover widely available without special facilities or prohibitive deductibles; cargo war risk normalizes.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude/LNG exports via Hormuz recover to ≥80% of pre-war volumes (~20 mbpd oil equivalent range pre-war; current flows highly constrained, with Iranian exports restricted by blockade and others using escorts/STS/bypass where possible). Continuous terminal operations without major backlog or dark-fleet dominance as the primary mode.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent crude and related products show no persistent war-risk premium exceeding $10–15/bbl above non-Gulf fundamentals; realized volatility returns toward pre-war norms without repeated $10+ daily swings driven by Hormuz incidents.
- Force posture of major actors: Meaningful de-escalation in US/partner forward presence dedicated to Hormuz escort/blockade and Iranian coastal force posture (IRGCN small boats, anti-ship missiles, radar). No large-scale ongoing blockade or counter-blockade.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No unresolved territorial claims over strait management, no active sanctions re-imposition cycles tied to maritime control, and no credible threats of closure or toll regimes that deter commercial operators.

A temporary ceasefire, lower oil prices from demand weakness, or a battlefield pause alone does **not** meet the standard. Commercial and insurance normalization lag military pauses and are gating.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (Gulf/chokepoint/maritime disruption/regional war):

- 1980–88 Tanker War (Iran-Iraq): Shipping continued under risk with escorts/convoys; full commercial/insurance normalization lagged formal ceasefire by many months to years. Partial functional recovery of volumes took 6–18+ months post-ceasefire amid residual mining/risk. Breaks down: bilateral conventional war, less great-power direct involvement, no modern drone/missile density.
- 2019 Gulf tanker attacks / Abqaiq-Khurais: Temporary spike in risk and premiums; shipping largely continued; normalization of insurance/risk perception within weeks to a few months after de-escalation. Breaks down: limited duration/scope, no full-scale war or blockade.
- Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing into 2026 context): Partial diversion and elevated premiums persist for extended periods even after intensity drops; full commercial return incomplete for 12–24+ months in high-threat phases. Breaks down: asymmetric proxy, different geography (Bab el-Mandeb), less direct state-on-state naval contest.
- Post-1991 Gulf War / post-2003 Iraq maritime recovery: Commercial shipping recovered relatively quickly once major combat ended and mines cleared, but residual risk and force posture lingered. Breaks down: coalition victory and regime change dynamics differ.
- Broader chokepoint disruptions (Suez 1956/1967/2021; historical Hormuz threats): Full volume recovery typically 1–6 months after clear security restoration; insurance lags physical security by weeks to months.

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment, weighted toward longer maritime/insurance lags and persistent Iranian leverage incentives):  
1 week: ~2–5%  
1 month: ~8–12%  
3 months: ~20–30%  
6 months: ~35–45%  
1 year: ~50–60%  
18 months: ~60–70%  

These reflect that physical passage often resumes before commercial confidence, insurance, and export continuity fully normalize. Current case is more severe and politicized than 2019 but less total-war than 1980s.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Durable political settlement removing Iranian incentive/ability to contest Hormuz control and US incentive for blockade.  
2. Sustained absence of attacks for multiple weeks, enabling insurer and operator confidence.  
3. Clearance of residual mines/threats + verified free transit routes without permits.  
4. Economic pressure on Iran (and oil-importing states) creating mutual interest in volume recovery.  
5. Third-party mediation (Oman, Qatar, others) producing enforceable corridor rules.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Iranian strategic view of Hormuz control as core post-war leverage and deterrent; resistance to unconditional free transit.  
2. Mutual escalation cycles (attacks on shipping ↔ retaliatory strikes on assets/tankers).  
3. High war-risk insurance and operator risk aversion that persist even after kinetic pauses.  
4. US blockade and sanctions linkage to maritime behavior.  
5. Proxy residual capacity and Gulf state security dilemmas.  
6. Political timelines (US midterms, Iranian internal consolidation) that reward posturing over settlement.

- Major reduction in direct strikes: 1w 10%; 1m 25%; 3m 45%; 6m 60%; 1y 70%; 18m 75%.  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (no routine threats/escorts required): 1w 5%; 1m 15%; 3m 35%; 6m 50%; 1y 65%; 18m 70%. (Gating for most others.)

Synthesis is not arithmetic average. Safe transit + insurance + export continuity are the primary commercial gating factors; military pause is necessary but insufficient. Political settlement is the highest-leverage upstream driver.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~2–5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slight downward (active recent attacks, blockade, low transits).  
- Core rationale: No pathway exists for multi-domain normalization in days. Even a sudden ceasefire would leave insurance, operator behavior, and residual threats unresolved.  
- Main gating factor: Ongoing kinetic risk and operator/insurer confidence.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Immediate verified mutual stand-down + public joint transit protocol + insurer rate cuts.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Further attacks or formal re-declaration of closure.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~8–12%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Mild downward.  
- Core rationale: Possible for kinetic pause and limited transit uptick, but insurance lag, Iranian control claims, and blockade make full functional recovery improbable. Historical chokepoint recoveries rarely compress this tightly after multi-month war.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance and commercial confidence.  
- What would raise: Durable MOU revival with enforceable free-transit language and verified demining/escort drawdown.  
- What would lower: Renewed tanker/port strikes or proxy activation.  
- Confidence: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 22%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~20–30%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Mild downward (persistent Iranian strategic interest + fragile prior MOU).  
- Core rationale: Window for mediated corridor agreement and partial volume recovery exists if both sides prioritize economics over leverage. Still requires overcoming insurance inertia and residual force posture.  
- Main gating factor: Political settlement on strait management.  
- What would raise: Successful third-party mediation producing operational free-transit rules accepted by operators/insurers.  
- What would lower: Escalation cycle or formal Iranian toll/permit regime.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 38%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~35–45%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral to slight downward.  
- Core rationale: Time allows for fatigue, economic pressure, and technical demining/arrangements. Reference classes (Tanker War lag, Red Sea persistence) support meaningful chance of partial-to-functional recovery, but Iranian leverage incentives and US political calendar create drag.  
- Main gating factor: Whether a settlement removes the control contest.  
- What would raise: Clear mutual economic incentives + verified sustained low-incident period feeding insurer competition.  
- What would lower: Prolonged low-intensity contest or new sanctions/blockade tightening.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 52%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~50–60%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Mild downward for political stickiness.  
- Core rationale: Longer horizon favors eventual volume and insurance recovery once major combat utility declines, consistent with historical maritime disruption patterns. Full multi-domain normality still requires political resolution of Hormuz status; otherwise a managed, higher-risk equilibrium can persist.  
- Main gating factor: Enduring political settlement vs. managed instability.  
- What would raise: Regime or leadership incentives shifting decisively toward economic reintegration.  
- What would lower: Entrenched Iranian control narrative + sustained US enforcement posture.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 62%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~60–70%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Mild downward.  
- Core rationale: Base rates and economic gravity support eventual functional recovery in most comparable cases, but the operational definition remains demanding (insurance, free transit without political conditionality, export continuity). Residual risk of prolonged contest keeps the number below higher certainty.  
- Main gating factor: Whether the strategic contest over the strait is resolved or merely managed.  
- What would raise: Comprehensive deal covering nuclear, sanctions, and navigation with verification.  
- What would lower: Permanent shift to dual-corridor/permission-based regime accepted as new normal.  
- Confidence: Medium (long-horizon uncertainty rises)  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Intermittent low-to-moderate intensity contest continues for months: limited escorted/coordinated transits, elevated but not prohibitive insurance for some operators, partial oil flows via STS/bypass/escorts, occasional attacks, and diplomatic cycles that produce temporary pauses without durable free-transit rules. Gradual volume recovery toward 40–60% of pre-war by 6–12 months if major escalation is avoided; full multi-domain normality only if a settlement removes the control incentive. Oil markets remain intermittently stressed.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
A new mediated agreement (Oman/Qatar/China facilitation) produces enforceable free-transit rules, rapid demining, insurer rate compression via competition/special facilities, and mutual blockade/sanctions relief. Kinetic incidents drop to near-zero for 4–6 weeks. Probability mass shifts upward most at 3–6 month horizons (could reach 40–50% at 3 months under this path).

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran prioritizes permanent management rights; US maintains blockade and retaliatory tanker strikes; proxies remain active; insurance stays elevated; operators largely avoid or demand heavy escorts. New normal of low-double-digit daily transits and persistent risk premium. Long-horizon probabilities compress (1-year ~30–40%).

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible overestimation of normalization: Underweights Iranian regime survival incentive to retain Hormuz as permanent deterrent/leverage after wartime losses; underweights how insurance markets and charterers reprice permanently after multi-month disruption and $2bn+ claims. Possible underestimation: Underweights pure economic gravity (Gulf exporters + Asian importers + tanker owners) and historical pattern that chokepoints eventually clear once combat utility falls. Load-bearing assumptions include continued Iranian capacity to contest and continued US willingness to enforce. Strongest falsifier for current view would be a verified multi-week attack-free period accompanied by insurer rate cuts and volume surge without political settlement.
