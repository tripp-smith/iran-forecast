**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (began 28 February 2026) by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from ~6 September 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
All of the following must hold simultaneously for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No sustained direct US/Israeli-Iran kinetic exchanges (airstrikes, missile/drone salvos on territory or bases) beyond isolated, contained incidents that do not cascade.  
- Proxy activity: Meaningful restraint by Iran-aligned groups (e.g., residual cells, Houthis, Iraqi militias) with no major multi-state attacks on Gulf infrastructure or shipping.  
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters): Free, unescorted commercial transit possible without systematic boarding, mining, drone/missile attacks, or IRGC “permission/toll” enforcement that deters major operators. Daily AIS-visible transits consistently ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (~100–120+ vessels/day).  
- Commercial transit levels: Sustained recovery of oil/LNG/container volumes through Hormuz to ≥70–80% of pre-war levels without large stranded fleets or chronic diversions.  
- War-risk insurance: Hull war-risk premiums returned to ≤2–3× peacetime baseline (pre-crisis ~0.1–0.25% of hull value per voyage); Joint War Committee listed-area status materially eased or removed for routine commercial traffic.  
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Persian Gulf seaborne oil exports and Qatar LNG flows restored to ≥70–80% of pre-war norms without force majeure declarations or chronic shortfalls.  
- Energy market stress/volatility: Brent crude no longer carrying a persistent large geopolitical premium driven by Hormuz risk (prices and volatility consistent with broader supply/demand rather than chokepoint closure fears); no repeated spikes >$10–15 on single incidents.  
- Force posture of major actors: No sustained high-tempo US naval escort operations or Iranian swarm/mine posture that signals contested control; drawdown from peak wartime readiness toward more routine presence.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new high-casualty incidents, leadership targeting, or territorial claims that reset the cycle of retaliation.

A temporary ceasefire, lower oil prices alone, or battlefield pause does **not** qualify. Commercial and insurance normalization are gating factors that lag kinetic pauses.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of ~6 September 2026)**  
War day ~190. Initial US/Israeli strikes (Operation Epic Fury) killed Supreme Leader Khamenei; Iran responded with widespread missile/drone attacks on Israel, US bases, and GCC states, and effectively closed Hormuz via warnings, boardings, attacks, and mining. Pre-war Hormuz carried ~20–25% of seaborne oil and significant LNG.  

A June 2026 Islamabad MOU (60-day cessation, Hormuz reopening language) produced partial traffic recovery (tens of vessels/day at peaks) that collapsed amid renewed tit-for-tat strikes, ship attacks, and US “mow the lawn”/tanker-for-tanker actions. **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  

Relevant classes (with breakdowns):  
1. **1980–88 Iran-Iraq War / Tanker War**: Prolonged Gulf shipping attacks, mining, escorts. Traffic adapted under risk but never fully normalized until ceasefire + postwar diplomacy; commercial recovery multi-year. Breaks down: lower intensity, different great-power involvement, no full Hormuz closure. Suggests 12–36+ months for functional commercial normality after major kinetic phase ends.  
2. **2019 Gulf tanker attacks / IRGC seizure period**: Short spikes in risk, insurance hikes, US/UK escorts. Normalized in months once attacks stopped and diplomacy stabilized. Breaks down: far lower intensity/scale, no regime-leadership decapitation or sustained multi-month closure. Suggests 1–6 months after clear de-escalation.  
3. **Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Chokepoint disruption, insurance spikes, diversions, intermittent attacks. Partial adaptation but not full pre-crisis normality years later; requires sustained restraint + capacity degradation. Breaks down: different geography/actors, lower oil share. Suggests multi-year for full commercial/insurance recovery absent decisive political settlement.  
4. **1990–91 Gulf War aftermath**: Rapid military victory, then residual risk (mines, sanctions, no-fly). Shipping recovered faster under coalition control, but political/insurance caution lingered. Breaks down: clearer conventional defeat of one side. Suggests 3–12 months for commercial flows under security umbrella.  
5. **Other chokepoint disruptions (Suez 1956/1967/2021; occasional Hormuz threats)**: Temporary closures or threats resolve in weeks–months if political will exists; prolonged if sovereignty claims persist.  

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence, averaged/weighted toward longer tanker-war and Red Sea patterns given intensity and contested sovereignty):**  
- 1 week: <5%  
- 1 month: 5–10%  
- 3 months: 15–25%  
- 6 months: 30–40%  
- 1 year: 45–55%  
- 18 months: 55–65%

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Mutual economic pain (oil revenue loss for Iran/producers; inflation/political costs for US/consumers) creating incentives for durable deal.  
2. Degraded Iranian conventional/proxy capacity reducing ability to sustain high-tempo disruption.  
3. Third-party mediation (Qatar/Pakistan/Oman) channels that previously produced MOUs.  
4. Gulf states’ interest in de-escalation and energy revenue recovery.  
5. Potential US political calendar pressure (midterms).

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Contested sovereignty/control of Hormuz (Iran seeking tolls/permission regime vs free navigation).  
2. Retaliatory cycles and “mow the lawn” logic that reset after every incident.  
3. Insurance market structural caution and claims experience (~$2B+ already) that lag kinetic pauses by months.  
4. Unresolved nuclear/IAEA and leadership legitimacy issues.  
5. Residual asymmetric capacity (mines, drones, small boats) that sustains low-cost disruption.  
6. Eroded US-Gulf trust increasing hedging behavior.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 2%  
- Base-rate anchor: <5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Down (active exchanges, single-digit traffic, extreme premiums)  
- Core rationale: Active kinetic cycle and commercial freeze; no mechanism for instantaneous multi-domain reset.  
- Main gating factor: End of current strike cycle + immediate traffic surge (impossible in days).  
- What would raise: Sudden comprehensive ceasefire + verified demining + insurer announcements (extremely low probability).  
- What would lower: Further ship attacks or major base strikes.  
- Confidence: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: 5–10%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly down (pattern of MOU fragility)  
- Core rationale: Possible temporary pause, but insurance and operator confidence cannot normalize in weeks; prior partial reopenings collapsed.  
- Main gating factor: Sustained safe transit without IRGC enforcement.  
- What would raise: Verified durable MOU implementation + 2–3 weeks zero incidents + premium drop.  
- What would lower: Any new ship attack or nuclear-related trigger.  
- Confidence: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 22%  
- Base-rate anchor: 15–25%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral-to-slight down  
- Core rationale: Window for mediated pause and partial traffic recovery exists, but commercial/insurance lag and sovereignty dispute make full definition hard to meet.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance + sustained ≥70% transit without escorts.  
- What would raise: Binding settlement language on free navigation + sustained zero major incidents + export rebound.  
- What would lower: Renewed mining or multi-state proxy attacks.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 38%  
- Base-rate anchor: 30–40%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral  
- Core rationale: Historical chokepoint recoveries often require half-year of stability; economic incentives strengthen, but structural Iranian leverage claim persists.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization + force posture shift.  
- What would raise: Formal free-navigation agreement + IAEA progress + premium collapse.  
- What would lower: Leadership instability or major US domestic political shift hardening positions.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 55%  
- Base-rate anchor: 45–55%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral  
- Core rationale: Longer horizon allows capacity attrition, adaptation, and deal-making; still well below certainty because contested control and residual asymmetric tools can sustain elevated risk.  
- Main gating factor: Durable political settlement removing Hormuz as bargaining chip.  
- What would raise: Comprehensive deal including nuclear limits + verified demilitarization of strait threats.  
- What would lower: Regime survival strategy centered on permanent chokepoint leverage.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 65%  
- Base-rate anchor: 55–65%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral-to-slight up (attrition effect)  
- Core rationale: Reference classes (Tanker War, Red Sea) show multi-year paths are common; full definition remains demanding. Overconfidence risk if assuming inevitable “new equilibrium” without commercial buy-in.  
- Main gating factor: Market confidence (insurance + charterer behavior) + absence of new triggers.  
- What would raise: Clear Iranian strategic shift away from Hormuz weaponization + sustained low premiums.  
- What would lower: Protracted low-intensity contest or new regional war front.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Intermittent kinetic pauses and partial traffic recoveries interspersed with resets, producing gradual but incomplete commercial adaptation under elevated risk. Insurance and mainstream operators remain cautious. Full multi-domain normality requires a political settlement that neither side has yet demonstrated willingness or ability to lock in; gradual attrition + economic pressure raises odds over 12–18 months without guaranteeing the tight definition.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Rapid mutual recognition of unsustainable costs + successful third-party mediation produces a binding free-navigation + limited nuclear understanding within 1–3 months. Verified demining, zero incidents for 4–6 weeks, insurer capacity return, and Gulf state buy-in allow traffic and premiums to normalize by 6 months. Requires Iranian leadership to deprioritize sovereignty signaling and US to accept constrained verification.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran treats Hormuz as permanent strategic equalizer; residual asymmetric tools + occasional high-profile attacks keep insurance elevated and operators diverted or escorted indefinitely. US maintains “mow the lawn” posture. Commercial flows recover only partially via dark/Chinese-linked routes; full definition fails even at 18 months. Proxy or intra-Gulf incidents act as recurring triggers.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible overestimation of normalization: Treats economic pain as decisive when Iranian regime survival and narrative of resistance have repeatedly outweighed short-term revenue; underweights how insurance markets permanently reprice after large claims and how “new equilibrium” can mean permanent elevated risk rather than pre-war baseline.  
Possible underestimation: Overweights current kinetic intensity and underweights capacity degradation + midterm political incentives for a deal that unlocks oil flows. Base rates from Tanker War may be too pessimistic given modern tracking, precision strike, and market adaptation speed. Load-bearing assumption is that free navigation (not Iranian-managed corridor) is required for the definition—softening that would raise longer-horizon probabilities.
