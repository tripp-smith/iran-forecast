**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain; not a temporary pause or price dip):

- **Direct military activity**: No sustained strikes, drone/missile attacks, or naval engagements by Iran, US/Israel, or proxies in/near the Gulf for at least 30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint (e.g., no major Houthi/Hezbollah/PMF attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US bases tied to the conflict).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe, routine transit without routine threats, mines, or attacks; no active dual blockades.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel transits recover to ≥70-80% of pre-war baseline (~80-100+ daily); backlog clears substantially; no widespread rerouting via Cape of Good Hope.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit multiples of pre-war (e.g., <1% hull value for short policies, not 1-5%+ or unavailable); major insurers restore standard coverage without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Near-normal volumes through Hormuz from Gulf producers (Iran, Saudi, UAE, etc.); no major storage crises or forced pipeline/reroute reliance.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Oil prices and volatility stabilize near pre-crisis ranges adjusted for fundamentals (no sustained shock premiums); no emergency IEA releases needed.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/coalition drawdown from heightened alert; Iran reduces IRGC naval/anti-shipping posture; no ongoing blockades.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No major incidents (e.g., ship attacks, leadership strikes, nuclear escalations) that reignite brinkmanship for the period.

Normality requires sustained commercial confidence and behavior, not just a signed paper or short ceasefire.
