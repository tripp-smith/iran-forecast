**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War, by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (as of 15 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires *all* of the following to hold concurrently and sustainably (not a single-day snapshot or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No sustained reciprocal strikes between major state actors (US/coalition and Iran) on Gulf-related targets; no systematic Iranian attacks on commercial shipping or Gulf infrastructure.
- Proxy activity: No material Houthi or other Axis-linked attacks disrupting Gulf/Red Sea energy shipping or major Gulf state infrastructure at scale.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial vessels transit without routine kinetic risk, mining hazards, or forced diversion; effective freedom of navigation under international norms (not solely Iranian-controlled corridors or US-escorted only).
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits through Hormuz sustainably ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (~100–130/day range, versus current ~7% / single-digit to low-teens averages).
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war-risk premiums for Hormuz transit return to ≤0.5–1% of hull value (versus pre-war ~0.25% and current multi-percent / 10–30× multiples), with broad underwriter availability and no widespread policy cancellations or JWC-listed prohibitive pricing.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf oil exports (ex-Iran under sanctions if applicable) and Qatari/UAE LNG loadings/transits recover to ≥80–90% of pre-war levels without chronic force majeure, massive STS workarounds, or storage constraints; Iranian exports constrained only by formal sanctions, not physical blockade of the waterway itself.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent/WTI no longer embed a large, persistent Hormuz risk premium; volatility normalized relative to broader macro conditions (not sustained $85–100+ solely from chokepoint fear).
- Force posture of major actors: Material drawdown or de-escalatory repositioning from peak wartime levels (US naval/air presence, Iranian coastal/missile/drone readiness); no active US naval blockade of Iranian ports or Iranian assertion of exclusive control requiring force.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No unresolved issues (nuclear reconstitution threats, leadership succession crises, major proxy activations, or territorial/administrative claims over the strait) that create high near-term probability of renewed major kinetic exchange.

A ceasefire, lower oil prices from demand weakness or SPR releases, partial escorted traffic, or battlefield pause alone does **not** meet the standard. Commercial normalization and multi-domain stability are required.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (adjusted for scale, stakes, and multipolar involvement):

- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Multi-year attacks on shipping (~450 vessels); strait never fully closed; flows continued under risk with eventual US reflagging/escorts (Earnest Will). Normalization of commercial risk took years post-ceasefire; high baseline for prolonged elevated insurance/risk. Breaks down: bilateral war without direct US-Iran regime-change stakes or nuclear overlay.
- **2019 Gulf of Oman tanker attacks + subsequent seizures**: Brief spikes; traffic largely continued; insurance rose temporarily then eased. Suggests rapid partial recovery if limited and contained. Breaks down: far smaller scale, no full war or leadership decapitation.
- **Red Sea/Houthi disruptions (2023–ongoing)**: Sustained attacks led to major rerouting (Cape of Good Hope); partial recovery only with sustained naval presence and incomplete deterrence. Insurance and behavior lagged kinetic pauses. Suggests multi-month to multi-year commercial lag even after reduced attacks.
- **Suez closures (1967–75 full; 2021 Ever Given brief)**: Full physical closure produces immediate traffic zero; reopening can be relatively fast once physical barrier removed, but confidence rebuild takes weeks–months. Breaks down: not kinetic/asymmetric war risk.
- **Black Sea grain corridor (2022–23)**: Negotiated corridors under fire risk; partial, fragile, and repeatedly disrupted. Suggests that negotiated “safe” routes under contested control rarely deliver full commercial normality quickly.
- **Broader post-major Middle East wars (1991 Gulf War aftermath; shorter 2025 Israel-Iran episode)**: Military pauses often precede months of residual risk, elevated insurance, and cautious commercial behavior.

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment) for *full functional normality* under this demanding definition:  
1 week ~1–3%; 1 month ~5–10%; 3 months ~15–25%; 6 months ~30–45%; 1 year ~50–65%; 18 months ~60–75%. Prolonged elevated risk and commercial lag are the historical norm after sustained chokepoint kinetic campaigns involving major powers.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Mutual exhaustion + munitions/stock constraints + economic pain (oil/LNG revenue, global spillover).  
2. Mediation channels (Oman, Pakistan, Qatar) and Gulf state pressure for stability.  
3. Precedent of June partial rebound shows technical capacity for rapid traffic recovery if kinetic risk drops.  
4. US interest in avoiding indefinite high-tempo operations; Iranian interest in sanctions relief/revenue.  
5. Bypass pipelines and alternative supply (US LNG, etc.) reducing some leverage of full closure.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Core dispute over control/administration of Hormuz and Iranian conditions (force posture, reparations, broader regional issues).  
2. Trust deficit after MOU collapse; repeated cycles of attack–retaliation.  
3. Proxy autonomy (Houthis) and residual Iranian coastal/missile/drone capability.  
4. Commercial lag: insurers, charterers, and crews require sustained safe passage evidence, not just statements.  
5. Domestic politics/leadership incentives on both sides; risk of miscalculation or spoiler actions.  
6. Physical residual hazards (mines, damage) and elevated force postures.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate probabilities of meaningful progress by horizon; not independent)

- Major reduction in direct strikes: 1w 15%; 1m 35%; 3m 55%; 6m 70%; 1y 80%; 18m 85%.  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (≥70% baseline without routine risk): 1w <5%; 1m 10%; 3m 25%; 6m 40%; 1y 55%; 18m 65%.  
- Meaningful insurance normalization (≤1% range, broad availability): lags transit; 1w <5%; 1m 5–10%; 3m 15–20%; 6m 30%; 1y 45%; 18m 55%.  
- Oil/LNG export recovery to high % of pre-war: partial oil possible faster via workarounds; full LNG slower. 1w low; rises with transit.  
- Proxy restraint: 1w 20%; 1m 40%; 3m 50%; higher later if main conflict settles.  
- Force drawdown + no new major triggers: slowest; remains gating even after kinetic pause.

Gating factors are sustained safe transit + insurance confidence + absence of new triggers. Kinetic pause alone is insufficient.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-to-moderate intensity risk with intermittent mediation breakthroughs and partial traffic recoveries that repeatedly stall. Eventual mediated arrangement (possibly Oman-centric corridors, phased demining, limited sanctions relief) produces kinetic pause by 3–6 months, followed by gradual commercial recovery over subsequent quarters. Full definition met later rather than earlier; insurance and LNG lag oil somewhat. Residual higher risk premium persists relative to 2025.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Mutual recognition of costs + effective mediation produces a durable, monitored arrangement within weeks to 1–2 months. Rapid demining, US facilitation then drawdown, Iranian restraint, and insurer confidence rebuild allow traffic and premiums to normalize by 3–6 months. Probability of this path is low but non-zero given June precedent and economic pressure.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Hard Iranian conditions + US insistence on freedom of navigation + proxy actions produce repeated cycles of attack and retaliation. Traffic remains suppressed, insurance elevated, and exports constrained for 12+ months. Managed instability becomes the equilibrium. This path is plausible given leadership incentives and trust deficit.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible overestimation of normalization: Underweights Iranian domestic politics/leadership consolidation incentives to maintain Hormuz leverage; underweights commercial stickiness (insurers and charterers may price residual risk for years); underweights munitions constraints or domestic US politics limiting sustained pressure.  
Possible underestimation: Overweights current hard positions; underweights economic pain on both sides and Gulf state agency in forcing a pragmatic deal; underweights technical capacity for rapid traffic rebound once kinetic risk drops (seen in June). Long-horizon probabilities may still be too high given the demanding multi-domain definition and historical lags.
