**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of the following observable thresholds (not a temporary ceasefire, price drop, or battlefield pause alone):

- **Direct military activity**: No significant kinetic exchanges (US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory or major Iranian missile/drone launches against US/coalition or Gulf targets) for ≥30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthi and other Iranian-aligned proxy attacks on Gulf shipping, energy infrastructure, or territory reduced to pre-February 2026 baseline (near-zero sustained incidents).
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and nearby waters)**: No confirmed attacks, mine incidents, or IRGC enforcement actions for ≥14 consecutive days; UKMTO/JMIC threat assessments lowered from severe/substantial; navigation possible without mandatory IRGC coordination or default heavy US escorts as the primary mode.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Sustained AIS-visible + estimated total commercial vessel transits through Hormuz ≥70% of pre-war daily average (~60–100 ships/day, baseline ~85–140 depending on source) for ≥14 days. Dark/AIS-off traffic counted only with corroborated volume evidence.
- **War-risk insurance**: Additional premiums widely available and ≤2–3× pre-war levels (roughly ≤0.5–0.75% of hull value per relevant period vs. prior 0.15–0.25%), with no broad cancellation notices.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Hormuz oil flows ≥80% of pre-war (~15+ mb/d through the strait on consistent tracking); total non-Iranian Gulf oil/LNG exports continuous near capacity without major interruptions; Iranian flows partially restored only if blockade/sanctions conditions change.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent premium to pre-war norms (~$70–75) reduced to <20–30% sustained, with no active emergency stock releases or extreme volatility spikes tied to Gulf risk.
- **Force posture**: US/coalition naval and air assets in the region reduced from wartime peaks; Iranian IRGC naval posture returned to defensive/normal rather than active interdiction.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No new territorial seizures, nuclear breakout steps, mass proxy activations, or formal blockade expansions.

All domains must hold concurrently for the period ending at the horizon. Partial recovery (e.g., oil volumes via bypasses) does not count.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, 1984–88)**: Relevant as sustained maritime interdiction in the same waters with mines, missiles, and speedboats; US escorts enabled continued (reduced) traffic. Breakdown: Iran was not the primary US target, technology was less asymmetric (fewer cheap drones/mines), and it ended only with the land-war ceasefire after years. Suggests 6–24+ months for commercial stabilization after hostilities end; partial traffic persisted throughout.  
- **1990–91 Gulf War**: Relevant for rapid US-led resolution of a Gulf conflict with shipping implications. Breakdown: Much shorter, decisive conventional outcome, limited prolonged maritime campaign. Suggests weeks to a few months for full normalization once combat ended.  
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–2025)**: Relevant for prolonged proxy/asymmetric shipping disruption, high insurance, and rerouting even after intensity declined. Breakdown: Lower strategic stakes for great powers, different chokepoint. Suggests 6–18 months for meaningful commercial recovery after peak threat reduction.  
- **2019–2020 US-Iran tanker/Gulf tensions**: Relevant for short spikes in attacks and insurance without full war. Breakdown: Did not involve sustained blockade or multi-domain war. Suggests weeks to 1–2 months for return after de-escalation.  
- **Chokepoint physical closures (e.g., Suez 2021)**: Relevant for pure transit recovery mechanics. Breakdown: No ongoing kinetic/insurance/political residual risk. Suggests days to weeks once barrier removed.  

**Base-rate prior** (probability of full operational normality by horizon, before case-specific evidence):  
- 1 week: 2–5% (almost never in active maritime wars).  
- 1 month: 5–10%.  
- 3 months: 15–25%.  
- 6 months: 30–45%.  
- 1 year: 50–65%.  
- 18 months: 60–75%.  

These reflect that commercial normalization lags military pauses due to mines, insurer caution, residual proxies, and force postures.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Ongoing Qatari/other mediation and periodic Iranian signals of willingness for short implementation plans (highest near-term weight if post-midterm US restraint holds).  
2. Demonstrated ability of Gulf producers to restore total oil volumes via pipelines and workarounds (reduces economic pressure for escalation).  
3. Historical pattern that maritime campaigns end when broader political settlement or exhaustion occurs.  
4. Potential post-election US focus shift or deal incentives.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Active IRGC maritime campaign (attacks, mines) and US blockade creating persistent residual risk (gating for insurance and free transit).  
2. Elevated force postures and explicit US signals of possible post-midterm strikes.  
3. Houthi/proxy activity continuing against Saudi targets.  
4. Insurance market and shipowner caution (lags physical safety by weeks–months; past cancellations).  
5. Lack of durable political settlement on nuclear, sanctions, or Hormuz control.  
6. Mines and asymmetric threats that are hard to clear fully and quickly.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Partial military de-escalation within 1–3 months after midterms (possible limited strikes then pause or deal), with oil volumes remaining resilient via workarounds. Commercial transit and insurance lag by 3–9 months due to residual mines/threats and underwriter caution. Full multi-domain normality most probable in the 6–18 month window if a political settlement holds; otherwise a prolonged “restricted but flowing” equilibrium.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm deal with rapid Hormuz reopening implementation, verified mine clearance, proxy restraint, and insurer rate collapse within weeks. Probability weight ~15–20% for 3-month horizon under optimistic assumptions.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Renewed major strikes, persistent IRGC maritime harassment, Houthi activation, and no settlement—leading to chronic high insurance and restricted transit even if total oil volumes hold via bypasses. Probability weight ~25–35% for normality still absent at 12–18 months.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case against this forecast: Overestimates near-term probabilities by underweighting asymmetric residual capacity (mines, drones, IRGC small boats) that can re-spike risk cheaply, and insurer behavioral lag (historical examples show premiums stay elevated months after incidents drop). Strongest case for higher probabilities: Underestimates speed of commercial adaptation and political incentives once election cycle ends; bypass infrastructure and volume recovery may force faster risk-pricing normalization than pure military reference classes suggest. Load-bearing assumptions: Mediation produces a durable pause rather than temporary; US post-midterm restraint holds; mine threat is clearable without years of effort.
