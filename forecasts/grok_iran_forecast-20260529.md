**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following sustained for at least 14 consecutive days without major reversal:
- **Direct military activity**: No strikes by US/Israel/Iran or major allies on each other’s territory/forces/assets in the Gulf region.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant activation/escalation by Iran-backed groups (e.g., Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, PMF) targeting Gulf shipping, infrastructure, or US/GCC assets.
- **Shipping security**: No attacks, mining, or credible threats forcing rerouting; freedom of navigation restored without mandatory Iranian "coordination," tolls, or escorts.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic through Strait of Hormuz returns to ≥70% of pre-war baseline (~100-130 vessels/day average); no sustained backlog of stranded tankers.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Private market premiums normalize to low single-digit add-ons (<<1% of hull value per voyage); no widespread government backstopping or policy withdrawals required for viability.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian and GCC exports flow at near pre-war volumes without major disruptions; no effective blockade.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility returns to normal ranges; no sustained >$20-30/bbl risk premium attributable to Gulf risks; Asian/European buyers resume normal contracting.
- **Force posture**: Major actors (US, Iran, GCC) show verifiable drawdown of high-alert postures, mine countermeasures complete, excess naval/air assets redeployed.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakout signals, leadership decapitation risks, or proxy surges that could rapidly reverse commercial confidence.

A temporary ceasefire or lower spot prices alone does **not** qualify.
