**Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from 13 October 2026).

**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
All of the following must hold simultaneously and be observable/sustained (not a temporary pause or price dip):

- Direct military activity: No sustained direct kinetic exchanges between US/Israeli forces and Iranian territory or forces (excluding isolated enforcement of any residual blockade). No Iranian ballistic/drone strikes on Gulf host-state targets.
- Proxy activity: Sustained restraint by major Iran-aligned groups (especially Houthis) — no major attacks on Gulf state infrastructure, airports, or shipping for multiple weeks.
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters): Threat level assessed “low” or equivalent by JMIC/UKMTO/CENTCOM; no attacks on commercial vessels for ≥2–4 weeks; free commercial transit without requiring special US naval coordination, Iranian permits, or dark/AIS-off operations as the norm.
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible (plus dark-adjusted) commercial transits at ≥70–80% of pre-crisis baseline (~85–125+ vessels/day), including tankers, LNG, and containers, on routine routes.
- War-risk insurance: Premiums returned to near-pre-crisis multiples (low single-digit % of hull or less for standard transits); broad availability of cover without single-voyage negotiation, exclusions, or 10–30× loadings as the market standard.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf (non-Iranian) crude, products, and LNG exports near pre-war volumes and reliability without force majeure, shuttle-only, or heavy pipeline-bypass dependence as the primary mode. Iranian exports unblocked if part of any settlement.
- Energy market stress: Brent (and LNG benchmarks) not persistently elevated solely by Hormuz risk premium; volatility normalized; no structural supply shortfall driven by the conflict.
- Force posture: Material drawdown of elevated US naval/air assets dedicated to Hormuz escorts, blockade enforcement, and surge presence; regional actors not maintaining wartime alert postures.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No fresh nuclear, proxy, or territorial incidents that re-close the strait or re-spike insurance/shipping.

A temporary ceasefire, lower spot oil prices alone, or battlefield pause without commercial/insurance recovery does **not** meet the definition.

**Current Situation Snapshot** (as of 13 October 2026, ~Day 228 of Hormuz crisis; war began 28 February 2026)  
Strait of Hormuz is severely restricted. AIS-visible commercial traffic is ~10% of pre-crisis (~8–14 vessels/day vs ~85–138). Many vessels run dark under US coordination or southern-corridor shuttles; Iran claims control and requires permits via its Strait Authority. Recent weeks saw the highest attack rates on shipping since the war began (dozens of tankers/LNG/containers hit or threatened in early–mid October, including outside the strait near Qatar/Sohar/Fujairah). US naval blockade of Iranian ports remains “ironclad”; CENTCOM reports redirecting 130+ vessels, disabling several, and destroying IRGC-linked tankers — no Iranian crude has crossed the blockade line for months. Gulf non-Iranian oil exports show partial recovery (Kpler and others cite ~60–80% of some pre-war metrics in September via mix of dark/escorted transits + bypass pipelines), but LNG remains >75% below pre-war, products lag, and stranded cargo value is high (~$20bn cited). Brent ~$105–107. War-risk insurance is extreme: single-voyage only, high multiples (reports of 30×), widespread restrictions/cancellations by clubs, rendering normal transit uneconomic for most operators. Direct US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory have been paused (Trump: none before 3 November midterms); Iranian ballistic/drone attacks on host states are limited recently, but shipping attacks continue. Proxies are active: Houthis have escalated against Saudi Arabia (multiple strikes on Riyadh and other airports, dozens killed, Saudi low on interceptors). Diplomacy is stalled (June Islamabad MOU collapsed; Iran’s conditions unmet). Elevated US force posture (carriers, blockade) persists. Sources include hormuz.now, Windward, Kpler, JINSA updates, UKMTO, CENTCOM, Al Jazeera, CNN, Reuters-linked reporting.

**Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (3–6):

- **1984–88 Tanker War (Iran–Iraq War maritime phase)**: Closest analog — sustained anti-shipping campaign in the Gulf/Hormuz for years. Oil flows reduced but continued (with hazard pay and later US escorts in Operation Earnest Will). Full commercial normalization only after the broader ceasefire. Suggests multi-year partial disruption possible even with escorts. Breaks down: No modern insurance market freeze of this severity; lower absolute volumes then.
- **2019 Gulf of Oman/Hormuz attacks**: Short-lived kinetic incidents produced sharp insurance spikes (×10) but traffic largely continued and recovered within weeks–months after de-escalation. Suggests rapid recovery possible after clear stop to attacks. Breaks down: Far lower intensity/duration than 2026.
- **2023–present Red Sea/Houthi disruption**: Sustained threat → large traffic drop, rerouting, elevated insurance for years; partial recovery with escorts but not full normality. Suggests 12–24+ months for commercial confidence even after partial pauses. Breaks down: Alternative routes exist (Cape); Hormuz has none.
- **Temporary chokepoint closures (Ever Given 2021, COVID port shocks)**: Physical short blocks recover in weeks, but commercial/insurance/positioning lags months. Suggests hysteresis. Breaks down: Not war-driven.
- **Broader regional wars with energy chokepoints**: Normalization tracks political settlement of the land war more than pure maritime events.

**Base-rate prior** (pre-current-evidence adjustment, conditional on a major sustained Gulf maritime war of this type already 7+ months old): 1 week ~2–5%; 1 month ~5–10%; 3 months ~10–20%; 6 months ~20–35%; 1 year ~35–50%; 18 months ~45–60%. Full functional normality (especially insurance + unrestricted transit) is slow; military pauses alone are insufficient.

**Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. US political calendar (Trump pause pre-midterms; possible post-election deal incentives).  
2. Partial oil-flow adaptation (escorts, dark ships, pipelines) showing resilience.  
3. Economic pain on all sides (Iran blockade, high prices, Gulf export losses).  
4. Historical pattern that sustained attacks eventually produce negotiation once leverage is tested.

**Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Persistent Iranian capability and will to attack shipping as primary leverage (current surge).  
2. Insurance market hysteresis — underwriters require months of clean security before broad, cheap cover returns.  
3. Proxy escalation (Houthis) creating second front and escalation risk.  
4. Stalled diplomacy and high force postures with no clear settlement path.  
5. Mine/threat residual risk and commercial inertia (contracts, force majeure, vessel positioning).  
6. US blockade + Iranian claims of control create dual vetoes over free transit.

**Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~2–5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly lower (ongoing attacks, insurance freeze, traffic 10%)  
- Core rationale: Active shipping attacks and extreme insurance make full normality impossible in days.  
- Main gating factor: Immediate halt to attacks + insurance signal  
- What would raise: Sudden comprehensive ceasefire with verified no-attack period  
- What would lower: Major new attack or Houthi escalation  
- Confidence: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 6%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5–10%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Lower (attack surge, stalled talks, proxy activity)  
- Core rationale: Even a pause would not clear insurance, traffic, or residual threat in 30 days.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance market response  
- What would raise: Sustained 3–4 week clean period + diplomatic breakthrough  
- What would lower: Continued weekly attacks or post-midterm US strikes  
- Confidence: High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 12%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~10–20%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Lower (hysteresis in insurance/commercial + current intensity)  
- Core rationale: Requires multi-week clean security, mine clearance if needed, and underwriter confidence — historically slow.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization + free transit without escorts  
- What would raise: Durable ceasefire + verified traffic recovery >50%  
- What would lower: Renewed major strikes or Houthi expansion  
- Confidence: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 22%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~20–35%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Lower (already 7.5 months in with no clear path; proxy front)  
- Core rationale: Possible if post-midterm diplomacy produces a lasting deal, but commercial lag remains.  
- Main gating factor: Political settlement sufficient for insurance and force drawdown  
- What would raise: Formal agreement reopening strait with enforcement mechanisms  
- What would lower: Escalation after US elections or prolonged stalemate  
- Confidence: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 38%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~35–50%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modestly lower (proxy risks, insurance memory, dual control claims)  
- Core rationale: Higher chance of negotiated outcome once costs accumulate, but full commercial normality still lags military pause.  
- Main gating factor: Durable political settlement + underwriter confidence  
- What would raise: Clear regime or agreement signals of permanent restraint  
- What would lower: Resumption of high-intensity war or new nuclear/proxy trigger  
- Confidence: Medium  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 48%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~45–60%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly lower (hysteresis + multiple fronts)  
- Core rationale: Even with settlement, residual insurance premia, vessel repositioning, and pipeline/bypass habits persist; full return to pre-war baseline is not automatic.  
- Main gating factor: Absence of residual threat that keeps insurance elevated  
- What would raise: Multi-month clean security + normalized premiums and traffic  
- What would lower: Low-level harassment continuing or new regional crisis  
- Confidence: Low–Medium  

**Most Likely Path**  
Stalemate continues through US midterms. Limited post-election diplomacy produces a fragile, conditional arrangement (escorted or monitored transit, partial Iranian restraint, US blockade relaxation). Traffic recovers partially (40–60%) with elevated insurance for many months. Full functional normality (unrestricted, cheap insurance, normal force posture) is unlikely before late 2027. Proxy activity (Houthis) remains a wild card that can re-escalate.

**Faster Normalization Case**  
Decisive post-midterm US-Iran deal that ends the blockade, guarantees no attacks, and includes verification. Insurance markets respond within 4–8 weeks of clean transit. Probability of normality by 6–12 months rises substantially (to ~40–60% range). Requires Iranian leadership calculating that further leverage has diminishing returns.

**Prolonged Instability Case**  
Attacks continue at low–medium intensity; Houthis keep pressure on Saudi Arabia; no durable deal. Insurance stays prohibitive; traffic remains suppressed or shuttle-dependent. Full normality remains <30% even at 18 months. Triggered by post-election US strikes or Iranian refusal to accept verification.

**Red-Team Critique**  
Overestimation risk: Assuming economic pain forces a deal faster than Iranian domestic politics or ideological commitment allow; underweighting residual mine/threat risk and underwriter conservatism. Underestimation risk: Underweighting the demonstrated partial oil-flow recovery via adaptation and the possibility that a US political decision (post-midterms) ends the blockade and escorts unilaterally, forcing Iranian restraint. Load-bearing assumptions: Iranian ability and will to sustain shipping attacks; insurance market requiring months of clean data; no sudden regime-level change in Tehran.

**Critical Signals to Monitor**  
- Weekly attack count (UKMTO/JMIC) and damage severity.  
- AIS + dark-adjusted transit volumes and % under escort vs free.  
- War-risk premium quotes and club notices.  
- Kpler/Vortexa oil and LNG export numbers.  
- Houthi activity vs Saudi and any Saudi interceptor shortages.  
- US force movements and post-3 November statements.  
- Iranian statements on permits/Strait Authority and any verification proposals.
