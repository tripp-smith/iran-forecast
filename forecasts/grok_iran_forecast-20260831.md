**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each of these horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

“Return to functional normality” requires simultaneous, observable satisfaction of all of the following multi-domain criteria for a sustained period (minimum 14 consecutive days, to distinguish from pauses):

- **Direct military activity**: No confirmed US, Israeli, or Iranian strikes on the other’s territory, bases, or forces in the Gulf theater; no new mine-laying or deliberate interdiction operations in the Strait of Hormuz or approaches.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant attacks by Iran-aligned groups (Houthis, Iraqi militias, Hezbollah remnants) on commercial shipping, energy infrastructure, or US/partner forces that disrupt Gulf operations.
- **Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Commercial vessels transit the TSS or agreed corridors without military escort as the default; UKMTO/JMIC or equivalent reports no “attacks,” “projectiles,” or “mine threats” requiring deviation; dark AIS rates return near pre-war norms.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily vessel crossings through Hormuz average ≥70% of the 2025 baseline (~100+ vessels/day), sustained.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Hull war-risk premiums ≤2× pre-war baseline (pre-war ~0.1–0.25% of hull value per voyage); JWC listed area status relaxed or premiums no longer prohibitive for mainstream operators.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf crude + products exports ≥90% of pre-war levels (~20–22 mb/d range) via normal commercial channels (not primarily dark/STS workarounds); major LNG facilities (e.g., Qatar) operating without force majeure linked to the strait.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent front-month volatility and risk premia consistent with pre-war ranges (no sustained >$20–25/bbl conflict premium relative to non-Gulf supply balances); no repeated force-majeure declarations by major Gulf producers.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US and coalition naval/air assets in the Gulf theater reduced from wartime surge levels toward pre-February 2026 steady-state; Iranian IRGC Navy posture no longer in active interdiction mode.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No new leadership assassinations, nuclear-site strikes, or base attacks that restart the kinetic cycle.

A temporary ceasefire, partial traffic recovery under escort, lower oil prices alone, or battlefield pause do **not** qualify. All domains must align.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant classes (with breakdowns):

1. **Iran-Iraq Tanker War (1984–88)**: Systematic attacks on shipping in Gulf/Hormuz approaches (~450+ attacks). Strait never fully closed; traffic continued under risk and later US escorts (Earnest Will). Full commercial normalization lagged the 1988 ceasefire by months to >1 year as insurance, routing, and residual risk adjusted. Suggests 6–18+ months post-major de-escalation for functional normality. Breaks down: no direct great-power vs. coastal-state war over control of the strait itself; Iran was weaker and fighting for survival.
2. **Suez Canal closures (1956, 1967–75)**: Physical total closures. 1956 reopened relatively quickly after political resolution; 1967–75 lasted 8 years with permanent routing shifts. Suggests rapid recovery only with clear political settlement; otherwise multi-year. Breaks down: canal is artificial and controllable by one state; Hormuz is international strait with dual coastal states.
3. **2019 Gulf of Oman tanker attacks + Iran threats**: Brief, limited attacks and threats. Traffic and insurance normalized within weeks–months after de-escalation and no sustained interdiction. Suggests weeks–3 months for low-intensity episodes. Breaks down: no full war, no leadership decapitation, no sustained closure attempt.
4. **Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Sustained proxy interdiction. Major carriers largely avoided; premiums elevated; partial traffic recovery only with escorts and persistent risk. Normalization incomplete after >2 years. Suggests multi-year for proxy-sustained disruption. Breaks down: secondary theater, not core chokepoint for Gulf exports, different geography.
5. **1990–91 Gulf War oil disruptions**: Kuwait production collapse then recovery over 1–2 years post-liberation; shipping security restored faster with coalition dominance. Suggests 6–12 months post-decisive military outcome. Breaks down: clear victor, occupation, and reconstruction; current war lacks decisive end-state.
6. **Post-ceasefire maritime recoveries after limited wars (e.g., various post-Cold War)**: Insurance and commercial confidence often lag kinetic pauses by 1–6 months even after agreements.

**Base-rate prior (pre-evidence adjustment, approximate probability of functional normality by horizon, averaging weighted classes with higher weight on Tanker War and sustained chokepoint cases):**  
1 week: <5%; 1 month: 5–10%; 3 months: 15–25%; 6 months: 30–45%; 1 year: 50–65%; 18 months: 65–80%. These are anchors only; current case is more severe (direct war, sustained effective closure claim, sovereignty contest).

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)

1. Mutual economic exhaustion and domestic pressure (Iran inflation/livelihoods; US midterms/political costs).  
2. Adaptive commercial workarounds (dark/STS, pipelines) reducing political urgency for full reopening.  
3. Mediation channels (Oman, Qatar, Pakistan) remain open.  
4. Proxy limitations observed so far.  
5. Possible eventual US posture adjustment reducing Iranian threat perception of permanent presence.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)

1. Core sovereignty/control contest over Hormuz (Iran conditions vs. US free navigation).  
2. Absence of durable, verified political settlement; repeated collapse of interim arrangements.  
3. Residual military capability and willingness for limited strikes/mine threats.  
4. Insurance and commercial risk aversion lag kinetic pauses by months.  
5. Force posture and domestic political incentives for continued pressure.  
6. Second-order effects (stranded crews, damaged confidence, Asian buyer diversification).

**10. Most Likely Path**

Prolonged low-intensity contest: intermittent limited strikes or threats, partial dark/STS/escorted traffic, elevated but slowly declining premiums, oil flows recovering via workarounds to 80%+ but not full commercial normality, stalled or fragile diplomacy. Functional normality most likely only after 12–18+ months if economic pressure and mediation produce a durable framework; otherwise a “new abnormal” with residual risk premium persists.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Mutual recognition that continued disruption harms both (Iran economy, US political costs, Asian buyers). Oman/Qatar-brokered interim corridor + verified demining + temporary fee/control compromise + US limited posture adjustment. Traffic and premiums respond within 1–3 months; full multi-domain criteria by 6–9 months. Probability of this path: ~20–25%.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Sovereignty dispute hardens; Iran uses residual mine/strike capability for leverage; US maintains pressure/blockade; proxies activate episodically; commercial confidence permanently impaired (buyers diversify, premiums structural). Functional normality delayed beyond 18 months or never fully restored under current definition. Probability of this path: ~30–35%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Possible over-estimation of normalization: under-weights path-dependence of insurance markets and commercial memory after 6+ months of disruption; under-weights Iranian domestic consolidation under new leadership; assumes economic incentives dominate ideology/sovereignty more than history supports. Possible under-estimation: adaptive capacity of Gulf exporters and dark fleet already greater than expected; US political calendar (midterms) could force faster de-escalation; second-order global demand destruction reduces urgency. Load-bearing assumptions: no decisive military breakthrough or regime collapse; no major new external trigger (e.g., wider proxy war). Strongest evidence that would falsify current view: sustained 14+ day multi-domain calm with traffic/premium data confirming recovery.
