**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

“Return to functional normality” means a sustained, observable state where:

- **Direct military activity**: No significant kinetic strikes (missiles, drones, naval engagements) between Iran/US/Israel and proxies in the Gulf for at least 30 days; no active US or Iranian naval blockades or mining.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, PMF, etc., with no major attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US/Israeli assets attributable to Iranian direction.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Open international transit without mandatory Iranian permits/tolls, threats, or harassment; US Navy escorts optional rather than essential; no routine mine threats.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Tanker and commercial vessel traffic through Hormuz returns to at least 70-80% of pre-war baseline (~20 million bpd oil equivalent); no widespread rerouting or stranding.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Standard commercial policies available at premiums no more than 2-3x pre-war levels; no broad Lloyd’s Joint War Committee high-risk designations forcing government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, Kuwait, Iran) exporting near pre-war volumes without major force majeure or physical constraints; no sustained global shortages/rationing.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., <30% annualized implied vol); no persistent risk premium >$10-15/bbl attributable to Gulf risk.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Iranian/coalition forces de-escalate to pre-crisis levels; no heightened alerts or major deployments.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No leadership assassinations, major proxy flare-ups, nuclear breakthroughs, or direct attacks on critical infrastructure.

A temporary pause or partial transit does not qualify.
