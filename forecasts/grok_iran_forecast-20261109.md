**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (as of 9 November 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires a sustained multi-domain condition (minimum 2–4 consecutive weeks of observed metrics meeting thresholds, with continuity expected thereafter), not a temporary pause or price dip. Thresholds are operational and observable:

- **Direct military activity**: No significant kinetic exchanges (missile, drone, or naval strikes) between US/Israeli forces and Iranian forces in the Gulf theater. Rare, isolated defensive actions do not disqualify if they do not disrupt commercial patterns.
- **Proxy activity**: No major Iranian-linked proxy attacks (Houthis, Iraqi militias) on shipping or Gulf infrastructure.
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and approaches)**: Strait open to unrestricted commercial transit without special permits, mandatory escorts, dark-sailing requirements, or active mine/harassment threats. UKMTO/IMO threat levels at baseline.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Total transits (AIS + estimated dark) recover to ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (~80–100+ vessels/day). Oil/LNG flows through Hormuz ≥80% of pre-war levels (~15–20 mb/d for crude).
- **War-risk insurance**: Additional premiums ≤1–2% of hull value (from current 6–10%), with restored market capacity and short cancellation clauses.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Regional Gulf exports (Hormuz + bypasses) near pre-war volumes without sustained disruption; Iranian export recovery is secondary unless a deal explicitly ties it to normality.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Gulf risk premium largely extinguished; Brent volatility low and price closer to fundamentals (pre-war ~$70–80 adjusted for other factors).
- **Force posture**: Meaningful drawdown of US surge naval/air assets from high-alert blockade/escort posture; no major IRGC naval surge or mine-laying.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakout moves, large-scale ground involvement, or collapse of talks into renewed full-scale war.

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price drop, or battlefield pause does **not** equal normality. Commercial and insurance normalization lag military pauses.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Domestic US political pressure (midterm losses, fuel prices, Vance/Trump acknowledgments) favoring a deal.  
2. Active Qatar mediation and Iranian offers of short-horizon reopening (conditional).  
3. Partial oil-flow recovery already achieved via workarounds, reducing immediate market panic.  
4. Mutual economic pain (Iranian exports near-zero; elevated shipping costs).  

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Unresolved core issues (nuclear program, sanctions, blockade sequencing).  
2. Continued low-level kinetic activity and mine/harassment claims.  
3. Insurance market memory and capacity constraints (claims already large).  
4. IRGC incentive to retain Hormuz leverage; US force posture still surge-oriented.  
5. History of collapsed ceasefires and Trump volatility.  

Ranked: political/diplomatic sequencing and insurance/shipping inertia are the strongest near-term gates; force drawdown and absence of escalation triggers dominate longer horizons.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued intermittent low-level maritime friction and elevated insurance through early 2027, with partial oil flows via workarounds. A limited political framework is more likely than full war resumption by mid-2027, but full commercial normality (open transit + normal premiums) lags any military pause by several months.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm political incentives produce a sequenced deal within 1–2 months that includes verified mine clearance, blockade adjustment, and sanctions relief. Insurance markets reprice quickly on verified incident-free transit; AIS recovers above 70% within 3 months. Probability of this path: ~15–20%.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks fail; low-level strikes and mine/harassment continue or intensify. Insurance stays elevated; open transit never recovers. Frozen conflict with elevated risk premium persists beyond 18 months. Probability of this path: ~25–30%.
