**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (initiated ~28 February 2026 by US-Israeli strikes) by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months from 12 August 2026.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of all of the following observable criteria for a continuous period of at least 14 days (to exclude temporary pauses):

- Direct military activity: No US/Israeli or Iranian strikes on each other’s territory, bases, or forces in the Gulf theater; no sustained coastal or island bombardment campaigns.
- Proxy activity: No significant Houthi, Hezbollah, or other Iranian-aligned attacks on commercial shipping, energy infrastructure, or Gulf-state targets linked to the Iran conflict.
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and approaches): No kinetic attacks, mining, or seizures of commercial vessels; risk assessments by JMIC/UKMTO/CENTCOM at “moderate” or lower with free transit on standard traffic-separation schemes (not solely Iranian-approved or escorted corridors).
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits through Hormuz averaging ≥100 (pre-war baseline ~130–140), including substantial non-Iranian/Chinese and energy tanker volumes in both directions.
- War-risk insurance: Hull war-risk additional premiums ≤1% of vessel value (pre-war ~0.25%; peak conflict levels 3–10%), with broad market capacity available without special consortia or government backstops as the dominant mode.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude/condensate flows through Hormuz recovering to ≥80% of February 2026 baseline (~15–17+ mb/d range historically) and Qatari/other LNG loadings near pre-war rates without chronic diversion or prolonged terminal outages.
- Energy market stress: Brent volatility and risk premium consistent with non-crisis periods (not sustained >$10–15/bbl geopolitical premium or repeated 5%+ daily spikes tied to Hormuz news); no structural shortage pricing driven by Gulf disruptions.
- Force posture: US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted or suspended; Iranian forces not asserting exclusive administrative control requiring permits/fees as a precondition for transit; major-power force levels not in active high-intensity interdiction posture.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new large-scale strikes, closed-strait declarations, or proxy campaigns that reimpose the above disruptions.

A temporary ceasefire, partial traffic recovery under Iranian oversight, lower oil prices alone, or a battlefield pause without commercial/insurance normalization does **not** meet the standard.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (adjusted for scale, energy centrality, and multipolar actors):

1. **Tanker War phase of Iran-Iraq War (1980s, especially 1984–88)**: Prolonged attacks on shipping; traffic disrupted but never fully halted; insurance spiked then partially normalized under escort regimes; full commercial confidence lagged years after major combat declined. Partial stabilization of flows often 6–18 months after peak intensity; full insurance/traffic normality longer.
2. **2019 Gulf tanker attacks / Iranian seizures + Saudi oil facility strikes**: Limited kinetic episodes produced multi-month elevated insurance and routing caution; recovery of “normal” risk pricing and unrestricted transit took ~3–9 months once kinetic activity ceased and deterrence stabilized.
3. **Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Sustained proxy disruption of a major chokepoint; traffic collapse on primary route, permanent diversion for many operators, insurance persistently elevated; no full functional normality after 2+ years despite periods of reduced intensity.
4. **Suez closures (1956, 1967–75)**: Full closures produced multi-year diversions and structural shifts; partial reopenings did not restore pre-crisis patterns quickly.
5. **Post-1991 and post-2003 Gulf force-posture normalizations**: Military pauses did not immediately restore commercial confidence; insurance and routing lagged.
6. **2024–25 limited Iran-Israel exchanges**: Short kinetic windows with rapid partial market recovery but residual elevated risk premiums.

Base-rate prior (probability of meeting the full multi-domain definition, before case-specific evidence):  
- 1 week: <5%  
- 1 month: 8–12%  
- 3 months: 20–30%  
- 6 months: 35–45%  
- 1 year: 50–60%  
- 18 months: 60–70%  

These reflect that commercial/insurance normalization systematically lags kinetic pauses, especially when control of a critical chokepoint remains contested.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Mutual economic pain (oil prices, Iranian revenue loss under blockade, global/Asian buyer pressure, US domestic fuel politics).  
2. Existence of Oman-mediated technical channel and prior partial reopening precedent.  
3. US preference for managed de-escalation over indefinite high-cost campaign.  
4. Iranian factional pragmatists seeking sanctions relief.  
5. Gulf-state interest in restored export continuity.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Contested sovereignty/control of Hormuz as core Iranian strategic leverage and domestic hardliner red line (IRGC).  
2. Trust deficit after MOU collapse; verification and enforcement problems.  
3. US political constraints on compensation/sanctions relief that look like “defeat.”  
4. Proxy activation risk and multi-front linkage (Lebanon, Yemen, etc.).  
5. Insurance and commercial risk aversion: operators and underwriters require sustained evidence, not announcements.  
6. Residual mining, coastal anti-ship capabilities, and force posture inertia.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: <5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slight downward (traffic still collapsing, active conditions demands, recent hostilities).  
- Core rationale: Multi-domain criteria cannot be met in days; even a sudden deal announcement would leave traffic, insurance, and verification lags.  
- Main gating factor: Physical and commercial restart time.  
- What would raise: Immediate joint US-Iran-Oman announcement + visible unrestricted transits + blockade suspension.  
- What would lower: Fresh vessel attacks or major strikes.  
- Confidence: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 12%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~10%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Mild upward for ongoing Oman channel and mutual cost pressures; offset by control dispute and recent MOU failure.  
- Core rationale: Technical route deal possible, but full free transit, insurance reset, and blockade lift remain low-probability within 30 days.  
- Main gating factor: Iranian willingness to drop maximalist conditions and US willingness on sanctions/blockade.  
- What would raise: Signed Iran-Oman-US framework with verified free transit and premium compression.  
- What would lower: Breakdown of Oman talks or renewed kinetic package.  
- Confidence: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 28%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~25%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest upward for economic incentives; still constrained by trust and sovereignty issues.  
- Core rationale: Window for a managed “no war, no full peace” arrangement with partial commercial recovery; full multi-domain normality still requires resolution of control and insurance lag.  
- Main gating factor: Durable verification mechanism and insurer confidence.  
- What would raise: Sustained 60+ day low-kinetic period + traffic >80/day + premium drop.  
- What would lower: Proxy escalation or hardliner veto in Tehran.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 42%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~40%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral-to-slight upward.  
- Core rationale: Historical chokepoint cases show partial stabilization feasible in this window if kinetic intensity falls; full definition still demanding.  
- Main gating factor: Whether any interim arrangement proves stable enough for commercial re-entry.  
- What would raise: Formalized multilateral transit regime without exclusive Iranian control.  
- What would lower: Prolonged “no war, no peace” with intermittent attacks.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 58%

- Current-evidence adjustment: Slight upward for cumulative economic and political pressures.  
- Core rationale: Base rates and cost accumulation favor eventual managed reduction in intensity; residual risk and possible structural changes to routing/insurance keep probability below 2/3.  
- Main gating factor: Whether control dispute is settled or merely papered over.  
- What would raise: Clear US-Iran political settlement including nuclear/sanctions elements.  
- What would lower: Entrenched Iranian administrative control accepted as new normal + persistent high premiums.

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 68%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~65%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slight upward.  
- Core rationale: Longer horizon allows for exhaustion, leadership changes, or institutional arrangements; still not near-certainty because of the depth of the Hormuz sovereignty issue and commercial memory of disruption.  
- Main gating factor: Structural shift in Iranian strategy or decisive change in US posture.  
- What would raise: Verifiable long-term free-navigation regime + insurance market reset.  
- What would lower: Normalization of Iranian control claims + sustained proxy campaigns.  
- Confidence: Medium-Low (long-horizon uncertainty)

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Extended “no war, no peace” with intermittent low-intensity exchanges, Oman-brokered temporary lanes under contested Iranian influence, gradual but incomplete traffic recovery (perhaps 40–70% of baseline), elevated but slowly declining insurance, and persistent geopolitical risk premium. Full multi-domain normality is more probable after 12+ months than before, but remains contingent.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Rapid convergence on a face-saving technical arrangement (Iran-Oman routes + limited US sanctions relief/blockade suspension), verified free transit for 30+ days, and insurer response. Could push 3- and 6-month probabilities materially higher if kinetic activity ceases and Asian buyers/Gulf producers aggressively restore flows.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
IRGC hardliner dominance, repeated vessel attacks or mining, US refusal on core Iranian demands, Houthi intensification, or linkage to other fronts. Traffic remains suppressed, insurance stays high, and “functional normality” is deferred beyond 18 months or redefined downward by markets.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Overestimation risk: Commercial and insurance recovery may be slower than kinetic pauses imply (Red Sea precedent); Iranian control claims may become sticky; US domestic politics could constrain deals. Underestimation risk: Mutual economic pain and Trump administration preference for “deal” outcomes could produce faster pragmatic arrangements than historical base rates suggest; once a credible free-transit window opens, traffic and premiums can move faster than expected. Load-bearing assumptions include continued Iranian capacity to contest the strait and continued US willingness to sustain the blockade/campaign costs.
