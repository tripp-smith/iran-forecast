**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means the Persian Gulf operates as a reliable, low-friction commercial chokepoint with minimal risk premium, comparable to pre-February 2026 conditions in practice (not rhetoric). It requires **all** of the following sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal:

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, interceptions, or naval engagements by US/Israel/Iran or their forces in/near the Gulf.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant attacks (e.g., by Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, or IRGC-linked groups) on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or energy assets attributable to Iranian direction.
- **Shipping security**: Free, unhindered transit through Strait of Hormuz without Iranian "permission/registration/toll" regimes, attacks, or credible threats; consistent naval escort or de-mining as needed but no coercion.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic (tankers, containers, etc.) returns to ≥80-90% of pre-war daily averages (~100-140 transits/day historically); no widespread rerouting around Africa or avoidance.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: War-risk premiums drop to near pre-war levels (low single-digit add-ons or standard hull coverage); major insurers (e.g., Lloyd's, P&I clubs) resume normal underwriting without exclusions or government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian, Saudi, UAE, Qatari, etc., exports flow at near-capacity without sustained disruptions; no major shortages or forced reroutes.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained >$10-15/bbl war premium); Asian/European buyers resume routine Gulf sourcing without rationing or panic buying.
- **Force posture**: Major actors (US CENTCOM, Iranian Navy/IRGC, Gulf states) de-escalate to routine patrols; no dual blockades or heightened alerts.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No unresolved flashpoints (e.g., Lebanon/Hezbollah violations, nuclear breakthroughs, proxy surges, or Iranian asset freezes) likely to disrupt within the horizon.

A temporary pause, partial reopening, or diplomatic MOU does not qualify.
