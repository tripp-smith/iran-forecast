**Step 1: Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality in the Persian Gulf means a sustained, observable state where commercial and security risks have normalized sufficiently for routine operations without extraordinary mitigations. It requires **all** of the following, sustained for at least 30 days without major reversal:

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes by US/Israel/Iran or major exchanges; Iranian coastal/radar/missile capabilities significantly degraded or restrained.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, PMF, etc., show sustained restraint on Gulf/Red Sea shipping and energy targets (sporadic low-level incidents tolerable if not disruptive).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No routine attacks, mining, or credible threats; US/coalition escorts optional rather than required; freedom of navigation without Iranian "protection" rackets dominating.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Sustained at ≥70-80% of pre-war daily averages (~50-60+ vessels/day, with tanker/LNG flows near normal volumes).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit multiples of pre-war (e.g., <1% of hull value for 7-day coverage, not 1-5%+); full commercial availability without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian, Saudi, UAE, etc., exports flowing at near-capacity with minimal stockpiling or rerouting; no sustained blockades.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalized (e.g., no persistent war premium >$5-10/bbl); no fuel shortages or panic buying.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US drawdown or rotation to pre-crisis levels; Iranian IRGC Navy not postured for closure; Gulf states de-escalating alerts.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No active nuclear crisis, major proxy flare-ups tied to Gulf, or unresolved Hormuz administration disputes blocking transit.

A temporary ceasefire, partial traffic resumption, or lower oil prices alone do **not** qualify. Normality is commercial viability and low perceived risk, not just absence of hot war.<grok:render card_id="920c97" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">37</argument></grok:render>

**Step 2: Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant historical cases (post-WWII maritime chokepoint/regional wars):

1. **Iran-Iraq War (1980-88, Tanker War phase)**: Relevant for prolonged Hormuz disruptions, attacks on shipping, insurance spikes. Normalization took years post-ceasefire; shipping recovered gradually with escorts. Suggests 6-18+ months for full commercial return. Breakdown: No nuclear/US direct involvement scale.
2. **Gulf War 1990-91**: Quick military victory, rapid shipping/oil recovery (months) once Iraq neutralized. Relevant for coalition intervention; faster if decisive.
3. **Red Sea/Houthi crisis (2023-25)**: Shipping rerouting, insurance spikes, partial recovery with naval protection but lingering risk. Months to partial normality, years for full.
4. **Post-2003 Iraq stabilization / Gulf security**: Proxy/insurgent threats persisted; commercial flows resumed unevenly over 1-2 years.
5. **Black Sea grain deal disruptions (2022+)**: Ceasefires fragile; transit partial and reversible; full normalization elusive.
6. **Post-ceasefire Lebanon/Israel dynamics**: Proxy restraint slow and incomplete.

**Base-rate prior** (before case-specific evidence): Low short-term, rising slowly. ~5-15% by 1 month, 20-40% by 3 months, 40-60% by 6 months, 60-75% by 1 year, 70-85% by 18 months.
