**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (as of 11 November 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained (minimum two weeks of confirming data) satisfaction of all of the following observable conditions:

- Direct military activity reduced to residual/defensive levels only: no significant kinetic strikes by the US, Israel, or Iran on territory, forces, or energy infrastructure in the Gulf theater.
- Proxy activity at near-zero disruptive levels: Houthi, Iraqi militia, or other proxy attacks on Gulf shipping or energy assets limited to sporadic non-disruptive incidents.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters: absence of mines, boardings, projectile attacks, or IRGC interdiction; free commercial transit without mandatory Iranian permits/fees or routine US naval escorts for normal vessels.
- Commercial transit levels: combined AIS-visible and dark/facilitated volumes ≥80% of the pre-February 2026 baseline (roughly 70+ equivalent commercial vessels per day), without systematic dark sailing driven by threat.
- War-risk insurance conditions: additional premiums ≤1% of hull value (or ≤~4× the pre-war ~0.25% baseline), with standard terms available and low cancellation risk.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: regional oil exports (via Hormuz or reliable alternatives) ≥90% of pre-war levels excluding permanent Iranian sanctions effects; QatarEnergy LNG force majeure lifted and exports approaching pre-war volumes without major capacity losses.
- Energy market stress and volatility low: no sustained war-risk premium >$10–15/bbl on Brent; TTF/JKM not elevated primarily by Gulf disruption.
- Force posture of major actors normalized: US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted or reduced to pre-war sanctions enforcement; major US carrier/strike group surge posture drawn down; Iranian IRGC Navy not actively interdicting.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: no new nuclear red-line crossings, large-scale ground involvement, or new front openings.

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price decline, or battlefield pause alone does not satisfy this definition. Commercial and insurance normalization are required.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot**  
As of 11 November 2026 (approximately day 257 since 28 February 2026 US-Israeli strikes), the Strait of Hormuz remains effectively restricted. AIS-visible commercial traffic is reported in the low single digits to ~8% of pre-war levels in multiple trackers (e.g., ~7/day recent average vs. ~85 pre-crisis); US-facilitated/dark transits are higher but still far below normal and subject to conflicting Iranian and US arrangements. Attacks continue: IRGC claims of mine strikes and navigation disruption on tankers, UKMTO-reported incidents, and periodic vessel fires/sinkings. October saw a record number of attacks.  

Oil flows have shown partial recovery via US-escorted corridors, ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman, pipelines (Saudi East-West, UAE), and other workarounds; regional non-Iranian crude exports have approached or matched pre-war levels at peaks, but Hormuz-specific flows remain lower and volatile. LNG is weaker: QatarEnergy force majeure extended into November/December for key Asian and European buyers; October loadings highest since the war’s start but still well below pre-war (Qatar ~30%+ recovery in some metrics). War-risk premiums remain elevated at 6–10% of hull value (vs. ~0.25% pre-war).  

Diplomacy via Qatar continues with “positive responses” reported after the Qatari prime minister’s Washington meetings; Trump has stated the war is “militarily effectively over” and further major strikes “quite unlikely,” contributing to oil-price declines below $90. Iran maintains seven preconditions (end blockade, sanctions relief, asset release, etc.) before full reopening. Houthis remain active against Saudi targets. Some energy infrastructure (refineries, LNG trains) has sustained damage. Confirmed facts are mixed with disputed claims (especially Iranian mine/attack assertions vs. US accounts); primary maritime and energy trackers (UKMTO, Kpler, S&P, IMF PortWatch) provide the most reliable traffic and flow data.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes:  
- 1980s Tanker War (1984–1988 phase of Iran-Iraq War): Multi-year low-level attacks on shipping during a larger conflict; traffic continued under risk and eventual US escorts; ended with August 1988 UN ceasefire; US convoy operations wound down within ~1–3 months. Relevant for duration of contested chokepoint under proxy/state pressure; breaks down because it was embedded in a land war between two regional powers rather than direct US-Iran kinetic exchanges. Suggests multi-month post-ceasefire commercial stabilization.  
- 2019–2020 Gulf tanker attacks and vessel seizures: Short-lived spikes without full-scale war; insurance and transit normalized over weeks to a few months once peak incidents subsided. Relevant for pure maritime disruption; breaks down due to lower intensity and absence of sustained blockade.  
- Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–ongoing): Prolonged elevated risk, higher insurance, partial rerouting, but never total closure; adaptation over years. Relevant for proxy persistence and insurance stickiness; breaks down because Bab el-Mandeb is less critical than Hormuz for overall Gulf oil/LNG.  
- 1991 Gulf War aftermath: Rapid (weeks-months) commercial recovery after decisive end of hostilities. Relevant for post-kinetic drawdown; breaks down due to one-sided outcome and different chokepoint dynamics.  
- Post-Tanker War 1988 specifics and analogous mine-clearance/insurance lag cases: Physical and market confidence restoration lagged formal ceasefire by weeks to months.  

Base-rate prior (probability of meeting the full operational definition by each horizon, before case-specific updates; assumes typical resolution paths with ceasefire then gradual commercial recovery):  
1 week: ~5%; 1 month: ~15%; 3 months: ~35%; 6 months: ~55%; 1 year: ~70%; 18 months: ~80%.  

These priors already embed that military pauses precede commercial normality by months, and that insurance and transit confidence are lagging indicators.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- Diplomatic channel open with positive Qatari mediation signals and post-midterm US de-escalatory posture.  
- Partial physical oil-flow recovery via escorts, STS, and pipelines, reducing immediate market panic.  
- Mutual economic costs (insurance, lost volumes, domestic political pressure) create incentives for de-escalation.  
- Ranked importance: diplomatic progress > partial flow recovery > cost incentives.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Persistent shipping attacks and Iranian insistence on control/preconditions.  
- Elevated insurance and low unrestricted transit volumes.  
- LNG force majeure and infrastructure damage requiring physical repair time.  
- Proxy activity and US blockade as ongoing friction points.  
- Ranked importance: unresolved preconditions and attacks > insurance/transit lag > infrastructure/proxy issues.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-level contestation for weeks to months, followed by a mediated deal that ends major kinetic exchanges and the formal blockade. Unrestricted commercial transit and insurance normalization lag by 1–3 months after that deal due to demining, confidence, and infrastructure. Functional normality most probable in the 6–12 month window. Oil flows remain partially supported by workarounds throughout.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Rapid deal within weeks that meets enough Iranian conditions for immediate attack halt and blockade lift, combined with aggressive demining and insurer risk appetite. Probability of normality rises sharply by 3 months. Strongest evidence would be verified multi-week zero-incident period and premium collapse.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks fail; low-level attacks and dual-control arrangements persist for 6–12+ months; insurance stays elevated; LNG recovery delayed by physical damage. Functional normality pushed beyond 18 months. Strongest evidence would be repeated failed mediation rounds and rising incident rates.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that this forecast overestimates normalization: Iranian leadership may prefer sustained leverage via the strait even after a military pause, keeping insurance and transit restricted indefinitely (Red Sea analogy). Strongest case that it underestimates normalization: Post-midterm US political pressure and mutual economic pain force a faster commercial reopening than reference classes suggest, with markets front-running physical recovery. Load-bearing assumptions: that a political deal is achievable within 6 months and that physical/insurance lags will not exceed 3 months post-deal. Evidence of either a hard Iranian rejection of any compromise or an unexpectedly rapid demining/insurance drop would force a major update.
