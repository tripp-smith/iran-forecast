**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from ~27 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

“Return to functional normality” requires simultaneous satisfaction of all the following observable multi-domain criteria for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot):

- Direct military activity: No US/coalition or Iranian strikes on each other’s military assets, commercial shipping, or energy infrastructure in/around the Gulf or Strait of Hormuz for ≥14 consecutive days; no large-scale ballistic/drone exchanges.
- Proxy activity: No significant Houthi, Iraqi PMF, Hezbollah, or other Iran-aligned attacks on commercial shipping, energy facilities, or US/partner forces originating from or targeting the Gulf theater at levels materially above 2025 baselines.
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters): Freedom of navigation restored without mandatory Iranian coordination, fees/tolls, or IRGC interdiction; mines cleared or residual risk at peacetime levels; no projectile/drone/mine incidents affecting commercial vessels for ≥14 days.
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible + dark-fleet adjusted daily commodity vessel transits ≥70% of pre-war baseline (~90–100+ of ~130–140/day); backlog of stranded vessels substantially cleared.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional premiums ≤2× peacetime baseline (or ≤0.5% of hull value per voyage for typical tankers); Joint War Committee listing status relaxed or cover available at near-normal terms without voyage-by-voyage special terms that deter mainstream operators.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf oil exports (Saudi, UAE, Kuwait, Iraq, etc.) and Qatar LNG loadings/exports restored to ≥80% of pre-war volumes, with loadings proceeding without systematic force majeure or security-driven curtailments beyond residual facility damage.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent/WTI and Asian LNG markers reflecting physical availability rather than acute Hormuz risk premium; no sustained multi-week spikes driven by transit uncertainty.
- Force posture of major actors: Material drawdown or de-escalation of US naval blockade/enforcement posture and Iranian coastal anti-shipping deployments from peak wartime levels; no active claims of exclusive Iranian administrative control enforced by force.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new assassinations of leadership, major nuclear-site incidents, or formal declarations reopening full hostilities.

A temporary ceasefire, lower oil prices alone, or a battlefield pause do not meet this standard. Commercial normalization (transit + insurance + export continuity) is the binding multi-domain test.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of ~27 August 2026, Day ~180–181)**

War began ~28 February 2026 with US-Israeli strikes. A June 17 MOU produced a brief partial reopening (transits briefly rose toward half pre-war levels) before collapsing after early July Iranian ship attacks and US retaliatory strikes; Trump declared the MOU over. Traffic remains ~5–15 visible commodity vessels/day (Kpler/UKMTO; ~4–10% of pre-war ~130–140), with many dark/AIS-off and US-facilitated southern-corridor movements. **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant reference classes (Gulf/chokepoint/maritime disruption + regional limited wars):

1. Tanker War phase of Iran-Iraq War (1984–88): Attacks on shipping persisted years; full commercial normalization only after ceasefire + political settlement. Partial transit recovery lagged kinetic pauses by months–years. Suggests multi-year drag for insurance/commerce even after major fighting ends.
2. 2019 Gulf tanker attacks / Iranian seizures + Soleimani aftermath: Episodes of disruption lasted weeks–months; insurance spiked and normalized only after de-escalation + quiet diplomacy. Base rate for full commercial reset after limited kinetic exchange: 3–9 months.
3. Red Sea / Houthi attacks (2023–ongoing into 2026 context): Sustained proxy disruption kept premiums elevated and rerouting persistent for >1–2 years despite partial kinetic responses. Commercial “normality” incomplete years later.
4. 1990–91 Gulf War / Kuwait liberation: Military victory relatively rapid; shipping and oil exports recovered in months once clear security umbrella existed, but residual mine/insurance effects lingered.
5. Post-2003 Iraq or Libya intervention aftermaths (maritime/energy angle): Force posture and proxy activity prolonged commercial risk far beyond major combat.
6. Brief 2010s Hormuz tensions (mining threats, seizures): Normalized in weeks–months when both sides preferred de-escalation and no core sovereignty claim was enforced.

Base-rate prior (before case-specific update; probability of meeting the multi-domain definition above):
- 1 week: <5%
- 1 month: 10–15%
- 3 months: 25–35%
- 6 months: 40–50%
- 1 year: 55–65%
- 18 months: 65–75%

These reflect historical lag between kinetic pauses and commercial/insurance normalization, plus the stickiness of chokepoint risk pricing.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**

Ranked: (1) Mutual exhaustion + economic pain (Iran currency/petrol crisis; Gulf exporters and global markets pressured); (2) Oman/Qatar mediation channels producing incremental corridor deals; (3) US preference for economic pressure over new large-scale kinetic waves; (4) Residual military degradation of Iranian anti-shipping systems from earlier strikes; (5) Market incentives for insurers/shippers once a credible 30–60-day quiet period appears.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**

Ranked: (1) Iranian core demand for recognized administrative/control role (or equivalent leverage) over Hormuz as postwar residual power; (2) US rejection of any Iranian toll/control arrangement + sustained blockade; (3) Stickiness of war-risk insurance and shipper risk aversion (history shows multi-month lag); (4) Residual mines, dark-fleet complexity, and proxy residual capacity; (5) Domestic Iranian politics and IRGC institutional interest in the strait as leverage; (6) Potential for new triggers (leadership incidents, proxy activation, nuclear file).

**10. Most Likely Path**

Continued low-level contest: intermittent mediation (Oman/Qatar), limited US-facilitated or dark-fleet traffic, elevated but gradually declining insurance, partial oil recovery via alternative logistics, Qatar LNG constrained by residual damage and risk aversion. Kinetic pauses predominate, punctuated by incidents. Functional normality (full multi-domain) arrives only if/when economic pain produces a pragmatic corridor arrangement that both sides can live with without formal surrender of core claims—most probably in the 9–18 month window, incomplete before then.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

US decides energy-price and midterm political costs outweigh maximalist navigation principles; accepts or ignores a limited Iranian/Omani administrative role; insurers respond to 30+ quiet days; traffic surges; residual mines cleared under joint mechanism. Probability of this path producing normality by 3–6 months: roughly 15–25% conditional on the political shift.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Iran treats Hormuz control as non-negotiable residual leverage; US maintains blockade and rejects any toll/control; occasional attacks continue; proxies activated episodically; insurance remains elevated; LNG and some oil flows stay suppressed. This path keeps probabilities below 40% even at 18 months.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Possible overestimation of normalization: Insurance and shipper behavior are stickier than kinetic metrics; Iranian IRGC institutional interest in the strait may be under-weighted; Trump administration rhetoric can shift rapidly toward renewed pressure. Possible underestimation: Mutual economic pain (Iranian domestic crisis + global energy costs) and Oman’s mediating skill could produce a workable quiet arrangement faster than reference classes suggest; residual Iranian anti-shipping capacity may be more degraded than visible. Load-bearing assumptions: (a) neither side accepts formal defeat on the control question in the near term; (b) commercial actors require multi-week evidence, not announcements.
