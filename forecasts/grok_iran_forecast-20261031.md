**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of the following observable thresholds for at least 14 consecutive days (longer for the 6-month+ horizons):

- **Direct military activity**: No significant US/Israeli airstrikes, missile/drone barrages, or naval engagements against Iranian territory, forces, or assets, and reciprocal Iranian restraint beyond sporadic low-level incidents. No active blockade enforcement that prevents normal commercial traffic.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthi, Iraqi militia, and other proxy attacks on Gulf shipping, energy infrastructure, or states reduced to pre-February 2026 baseline levels (near-zero for Gulf-directed kinetic attacks).
- **Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters)**: UKMTO/IMO/JMIC threat level downgraded from “severe”; no successful attacks on commercial vessels in a rolling 14-day window; mines cleared from primary shipping lanes with third-party verification; no IRGC “unauthorized route” enforcement or limpet-mine operations.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily AIS-visible commercial transits (tankers + cargo) averaging ≥70% of the pre-war baseline (~60+ of the ~85–90 daily average), with outbound oil/LNG tankers at ≥80% of pre-war volumes without mandatory US naval escorts as the dominant mode.
- **War-risk insurance**: Additional premiums for Hormuz/Gulf transits ≤1% of hull value (from current 6–10%), with standard 7-day cover widely available and no mass cancellation or exclusion clauses.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Combined Gulf oil exports through Hormuz ≥15–16 mb/d (near pre-war ~17–18 mb/d including Iranian volumes where permitted) and LNG flows restored without systematic disruption; no major port or terminal damage constraining loadings.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent crude in a sustained range of $70–85/bbl (near or modestly above pre-war ~$60–75) with implied volatility and forward-curve backwardation/contango returning to non-crisis norms; no emergency stock releases required.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US carrier strike groups and amphibious forces reduced from peak wartime levels; Iranian IRGC Navy and missile forces at non-heightened readiness; no active US blockade of Iranian ports.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No new leadership decapitation attempts, nuclear-related strikes, large-scale proxy activation, or mine-laying campaigns that reset the above metrics.

A temporary ceasefire, partial oil-flow rebound under escorts, or price dips alone do not qualify. Normality is commercial and multi-domain, not merely a battlefield pause.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **Tanker War (1984–1988)**: Relevant as sustained IRGC/Iraqi anti-shipping campaign in the same waters with US escorts eventually containing but not immediately ending attacks. Breakdown: embedded in a larger land war that ended only after years of attrition and UN ceasefire. Suggests 2–4+ years for functional commercial normalization while hostilities continue; partial recovery under escorts within 6–12 months of dedicated naval protection.
- **Red Sea/Houthi crisis (2023–ongoing)**: Relevant for proxy-driven chokepoint disruption with insurance spikes and traffic diversion. Breakdown: lower volume of oil and alternative routing available. Suggests multi-year residual disruption and elevated premiums until the underlying conflict ends; traffic recovery lagged political de-escalation by many months.
- **1990–91 Gulf War**: Relevant for rapid US-led resolution of a Gulf crisis with energy implications. Breakdown: short, decisive conventional campaign against a conventional state actor, not a resilient missile/proxy power. Suggests commercial recovery within 1–3 months after hostilities cease.
- **Suez closures (1956 ~5 months; 1967–75 8 years; 2021 Ever Given 6 days)**: Relevant for pure chokepoint physical or political closure effects on trade. Breakdown: Suez has substitutes; Hormuz for Gulf oil largely does not. Suggests recovery within weeks after physical clearance if political will exists, or years if political closure persists.
- **Post-2020 COVID / Red Sea traffic hysteresis**: Relevant for lingering commercial caution and insurance lag after acute shocks. Suggests 3–9 months of residual under-utilization even after security improves.

**Base-rate prior** (before current-case adjustment): 1 week <5%; 1 month 5–10%; 3 months 10–15%; 6 months 20–30%; 1 year 35–45%; 18 months 50–60%. These reflect that durable multi-domain commercial normalization in Hormuz-class crises has historically required either war termination or sustained naval dominance plus political settlement, which typically takes many months to years.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. US electoral calendar and Trump’s public desire to “wrap up” the conflict (load-bearing near-term).  
2. Partial oil-flow recovery under escorts demonstrates that physical throughput is possible when protected.  
3. Active Qatari (and prior Pakistani) mediation channels.  
4. Mutual economic pain (US costs, Iranian export constraints, global energy prices).  
5. Historical precedent that dedicated naval escort + mine clearance can restore partial traffic within months.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Iran’s continued high-tempo shipping attacks and insistence on control/conditions as core leverage (gating).  
2. US military buildup and post-midterm strike options explicitly on the table.  
3. Insurance market inertia and seafarer/owner risk aversion (second-order lag of months).  
4. Proxy activation (Houthis) and IRGC residual capability for mines, drones, and fast-attack craft.  
5. Sequencing disputes (nuclear vs blockade vs Hormuz control) that collapsed the June MOU.  
6. No physical or political substitute for Hormuz volumes.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

- **1 Week**  
  - Probability: 3%  
  - Base-rate anchor: Near-zero for multi-domain recovery in first week of any ongoing Hormuz disruption.  
  - Current-evidence adjustment: Slight upward from active diplomacy and US strike pause; downward from ongoing attacks and midterm timing.  
  - Core rationale: No pathway to clear the definition’s security, insurance, and transit thresholds in 7 days.  
  - Main gating factor: Continued IRGC attacks and unresolved political terms.  
  - What would raise: Immediate durable ceasefire announcement with verified mine-clearance and attack halt.  
  - What would lower: Post-midterm strike resumption or major new tanker sinking.  
  - Confidence: High  

- **1 Month**  
  - Probability: 8%  
  - Base-rate anchor: 5–10%.  
  - Current-evidence adjustment: Downward from October attack peak and US buildup; modest upward from mediation and partial oil flows.  
  - Core rationale: Midterms and possible post-election decision window dominate; commercial actors will not treat a pause as normality.  
  - Main gating factor: Insurance and owner risk aversion plus unresolved Iranian conditions.  
  - What would raise: Signed interim deal with attack halt and premium collapse.  
  - What would lower: Renewed US/Israeli strikes or sustained >20 monthly attacks.  
  - Confidence: High  

- **3 Months**  
  - Probability: 18%  
  - Base-rate anchor: 10–15%.  
  - Current-evidence adjustment: Modest upward for possible post-midterm deal or limited campaign; downward for residual IRGC capability and insurance lag.  
  - Core rationale: Enough time for a short intense operation or negotiated pause, but commercial normalization (escorts optional, premiums normal) lags military outcomes by months.  
  - Main gating factor: Whether a political settlement removes the need for escorts and ends mine/limpet threats.  
  - What would raise: Verified sustained zero-attack window + premium drop below 2%.  
  - What would lower: Stalemated attrition or new escalation cycle.  
  - Confidence: Medium  

- **6 Months**  
  - Probability: 32%  
  - Base-rate anchor: 20–30%.  
  - Current-evidence adjustment: Upward from mutual costs and historical escort success; downward from 1980s-style protracted risk and current attack tempo.  
  - Core rationale: Plausible window for either decisive US pressure or negotiated reopening with demining; insurance and traffic still expected to lag.  
  - Main gating factor: Insurance market and seafarer confidence (second-order).  
  - What would raise: Formal agreement + third-party verification of lanes + traffic >50% without escorts.  
  - What would lower: Continued monthly double-digit attacks or force expansion.  
  - Confidence: Medium  

- **1 Year**  
  - Probability: 48%  
  - Base-rate anchor: 35–45%.  
  - Current-evidence adjustment: Slight upward for attrition and economic incentives; downward for residual proxy and political sequencing risks.  
  - Core rationale: Reference classes show many chokepoint crises resolve or stabilize within 12 months once one side prioritizes exit, but Hormuz leverage and IRGC persistence argue against rapid full commercial return.  
  - Main gating factor: End of active Iranian anti-shipping campaign.  
  - What would raise: Regime-level decision to prioritize oil exports over disruption.  
  - What would lower: New leadership or nuclear crisis that restarts high-intensity fighting.  
  - Confidence: Medium  

- **18 Months**  
  - Probability: 62%  
  - Base-rate anchor: 50–60%.  
  - Current-evidence adjustment: Modest upward; long horizon allows for exhaustion, leadership change, or mediated settlement.  
  - Core rationale: Even the Tanker War eventually ended; 18 months provides room for demining, insurance normalization, and force drawdown once hostilities cease. Still below 70% because residual risk aversion and possible low-level incidents can persist.  
  - Main gating factor: Durable political settlement that removes Iran’s incentive to use Hormuz as leverage.  
  - What would raise: Comprehensive deal with nuclear and sanctions elements.  
  - What would lower: Frozen conflict with periodic attacks.  
  - Confidence: Low-Medium  

Synthesis: The gating factors are sustained attack cessation and insurance/traffic confidence, not merely oil volumes under escort or a ceasefire. Probabilities are not simple averages; the commercial components lag the military ones, producing the final figures. Long-horizon numbers stay below 70% because the operational definition is deliberately demanding and historical residual disruption is common.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued intermittent maritime attacks and elevated insurance through early 2027, with oil flows partially maintained under escorts. Post-midterm US decision (limited strikes or intensified diplomacy) in November–December 2026. Partial commercial recovery (traffic 40–60%, premiums 2–4%) by mid-2027 if a mediated arrangement holds; full functional normality (definition met) more likely in late 2027 or 2028.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm deal that meets core Iranian sequencing demands in exchange for verified attack halt, joint or third-party demining, and phased US blockade lift. Insurance markets reprice within weeks of zero-incident window; traffic rebounds to >70% within 2–3 months. Probability of this path: ~15–20% by 6 months.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Post-midterm strikes restart a high-intensity cycle; IRGC retains mine/drone/fast-attack capacity; proxies expand; insurance remains exclusionary. Traffic stays escort-dependent and depressed for 12–24 months. Probability of functional normality still <30% at 18 months under this path: ~25–30% likelihood.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that I overestimate normalization: IRGC has demonstrated persistent capability and political will to use Hormuz as leverage; insurance markets and shipowners have multi-month memory; a frozen conflict with low-level attacks can persist indefinitely (Red Sea analogy). Strongest case that I underestimate: Mutual economic pain plus US naval dominance could force a quicker Iranian accommodation than the Tanker War, and partial volume recovery already shows commercial adaptation. Load-bearing assumptions: that Iran prioritizes export revenue eventually, and that US political will for open-ended operations is finite. Evidence that would prove the view wrong: sustained zero-attack 30-day window with premiums collapsing below 1% (upside) or a new major escalation cycle after midterms that returns oil flows below 5 mb/d (downside).
