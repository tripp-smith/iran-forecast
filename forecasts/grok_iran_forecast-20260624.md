**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain): 

Functional normality means **all** of the following are sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major disruption:
- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes between US/Israel and Iran (or vice versa) or major Iranian attacks on Gulf infrastructure.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, and other Iranian-backed groups (e.g., no sustained rocket/drone campaigns against Israel/Gulf states or shipping; attacks at pre-2026 baseline or lower).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No mines, attacks on commercial vessels, or forced "tolls"/IRGC escorts; freedom of navigation via standard international routes without credible threats.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel transits and throughput approaching ~80-90%+ of pre-war daily averages (~60+ transits/day, ~10M+ DWT throughput).<grok:render card_id="a15d84" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">45</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="ff4f97" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">51</argument></grok:render>
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Standard commercial coverage widely available at premiums near pre-war levels (e.g., <<1% of hull value per transit); no broad government backstops or high-risk designations required.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (including Iranian) at or near pre-war volumes (~20M bpd oil equivalent through Hormuz region) with minimal force majeure declarations or rerouting.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent and regional benchmarks stable with low volatility (e.g., no sustained spikes >$20-30/bbl attributable to Gulf risks); Asian/European buyers resuming normal contracting without premiums.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval/air assets drawing down toward pre-crisis levels; no active blockades or heightened alerts.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No major incidents (e.g., leadership strikes, nuclear breakout signals, proxy escalations) that credibly risk reversal.

A temporary pause, partial shipping rebound, or lower oil prices alone do **not** qualify.
