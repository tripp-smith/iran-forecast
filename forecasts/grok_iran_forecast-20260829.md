**1. Forecast Question**

Probability that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (US-Israeli strikes beginning ~28 February 2026) by: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months from 29 August 2026.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of all the following observable conditions for a sustained period (not a temporary lull or single-day spike):

- Direct military activity: No sustained direct strikes by Iran/US/Israel on each other’s core territory, military bases, or critical energy infrastructure in the Gulf theater beyond isolated, non-escalatory incidents. No large-scale missile/drone campaigns.
- Proxy activity: No sustained, high-tempo proxy attacks (Houthis, Iraqi groups, Hezbollah) on commercial shipping, Gulf energy infrastructure, or US/partner forces that materially disrupt operations.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Incident rate (attacks, boardings, mine threats, projectile strikes) at or near pre-war baseline; verified clearance of mines from Traffic Separation Scheme and adjacent areas with independent confirmation; free passage without Iranian unilateral authorization, tolls, or revenue-sharing enforcement that effectively controls transit.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits through Hormuz consistently at ≥70–80% of pre-war average (~95–130 commodity vessels/day, depending on source baseline), with majority operating with AIS active and using established international routes rather than “dark” or unilaterally controlled corridors.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional war-risk premiums for Hormuz transit returned to near pre-war levels (historically ~0.25% of hull value or lower) or at most low single digits with broad market capacity and no Joint War Committee high-risk listing that prices most traffic out.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude/condensate and LNG flows through Hormuz (and associated loading) restored to ≥80–90% of pre-war volumes without systemic force majeure, widespread ship-to-ship transfers outside the strait as primary workaround, or prolonged port/refinery shutdowns driven by security.
- Energy market stress and volatility: No persistent war-driven premium dominating price formation; volatility metrics and forward curves reflecting primarily non-war fundamentals rather than ongoing Hormuz/Gulf security risk.
- Force posture of major actors: Material reduction or stabilization of US naval/air presence from peak wartime levels; Iranian forces no longer systematically interdicting or claiming exclusive administrative control over the strait; no ongoing US naval blockade of Iranian ports as a primary tool.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new high-impact events (leadership decapitation attempts, major infrastructure strikes, nuclear-related crises, or large-scale proxy activation) that reset the risk premium.

A temporary ceasefire, partial traffic rebound under Iranian administrative control, lower oil prices alone, or a battlefield pause do **not** meet the definition. Commercial and insurance normalization are gating factors equal in weight to kinetic quiet.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant historical cases (adjusted for modern insurance markets, AIS tracking, sanctions architecture, and energy interdependence):

1. **1980–88 Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Prolonged attacks on shipping in Gulf/Hormuz approaches. Partial recovery of traffic during lulls, but full commercial/insurance normalization lagged major combat reduction by months to years. Suggests multi-month to multi-year horizon for functional normality after major kinetic phase ends. Breaks down because modern precision munitions, real-time tracking, and war-risk markets price risk faster and more punitively; no equivalent superpower direct involvement.

2. **2019–2021 Gulf tanker attacks / Max Planck / other Iranian interdictions + COVID overlap**: Temporary spikes in premiums and avoidance; recovery of most traffic within weeks to a few months once attacks subsided and insurance capacity returned. Suggests relatively rapid partial rebound possible if kinetic activity drops sharply. Breaks down due to far lower intensity and duration than 2026 war; no large-scale mining or sustained blockade.

3. **Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing, peak disruption 2024–25)**: Dramatic traffic collapse via Bab el-Mandeb, elevated premiums, rerouting. Partial recovery with naval escorts and reduced attack tempo, but full pre-disruption normality (insurance + volume + route preference) still incomplete years later while residual threat persists. Suggests that even after major reduction in attacks, insurance and commercial caution can keep “functional normality” delayed 6–18+ months. Highly relevant for chokepoint + proxy dynamics.

4. **Post-ceasefire Levant/Gulf de-escalations after major rounds (e.g., post-2006 Lebanon, various Gaza/Israel pauses, 2024–25 Iran-Israel exchanges)**: Kinetic quiet often arrives faster than commercial confidence. Insurance and shipping normalize only after sustained incident-free periods and political settlement signals. Timelines for commercial recovery typically 1–6 months after durable quiet if no unresolved control claims.

5. **Other modern chokepoint disruptions (Suez 2021 grounding recovery; Black Sea grain corridor post-2022)**: Physical clearance + political framework can restore volume relatively quickly (weeks–months), but residual risk premiums and alternative routing persist longer.

**Base-rate prior (pre-case-specific evidence, rough synthesis for demanding multi-domain normality):**  
- 1 week: ~1–3%  
- 1 month: ~5–10%  
- 3 months: ~15–25%  
- 6 months: ~30–45%  
- 1 year: ~50–65%  
- 18 months: ~60–75%  

These reflect that kinetic pauses are common relatively quickly, but full commercial + insurance + control-normalization is rarer and slower, especially with unresolved sovereignty/control claims over a chokepoint.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**

- Multi-week pause in major direct strikes on Iranian territory reduces immediate escalation risk.  
- Active third-party mediation and Iranian willingness to list conditions create a diplomatic track.  
- Economic pressure on Iran (sanctions + blockade + oil export constraints) creates incentive for a deal that restores some revenue.  
- US and Gulf interest in restoring energy flows and reducing military strain.  
- Historical pattern that shipping volumes can rebound faster than full political settlement once kinetic risk falls.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**

- Unresolved Iranian claim to administrative/control rights over Hormuz (corridor, revenue share, authorization).  
- High war-risk insurance and commercial caution that require sustained incident-free evidence, not just announcements.  
- US insistence on free navigation without Iranian control + continued blockade.  
- Residual mines, dark shipping, and recent incidents keep risk elevated.  
- Proxy networks and Iranian domestic politics after leadership change make rapid full restraint difficult.  
- Second-order effects: once traffic collapses and alternative workarounds (STS, other routes) embed, reversion is sticky.  
- Load-bearing assumption: any deal must resolve both kinetic quiet **and** the control/insurance gap simultaneously.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

- Core rationale: No realistic path to multi-domain normality (especially insurance, volume, free transit) in days.  
- Main gating factor: Commercial/insurance response time and residual kinetic risk.  
- What would raise: Sudden comprehensive deal + verified zero incidents + immediate premium collapse (extremely low probability).  
- What would lower: Further attacks or formal Iranian closure announcement.  
- Confidence: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5–10%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral to slight downward (diplomacy exists but conditions far apart; traffic still suppressed).  
- Core rationale: Possible kinetic quiet and limited traffic rebound under temporary corridor, but full free transit + insurance normalization + volume recovery highly unlikely.  
- Main gating factor: Whether any corridor deal is accepted by insurers and major shippers as equivalent to free navigation.  
- What would raise: Signed deal with verified mine clearance, sustained zero incidents, and rapid premium drop.  
- What would lower: Breakdown of mediation or renewed strikes.  
- Confidence: Medium-High

- What would raise: Durable deal + 4–6 weeks incident-free traffic at rising volumes + JWC de-listing signals.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Continued “no war, no peace”: intermittent diplomacy, temporary corridors or facilitated transits, suppressed but non-zero traffic, elevated premiums, and gradual partial recovery of volumes via workarounds and selective insurance. Full multi-domain normality arrives only if/when a broader political settlement resolves the control dispute and produces months of clean data for insurers. Most probable window for crossing the threshold is 9–18 months if no major re-escalation.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Comprehensive deal within 1–3 months that includes verified demining, explicit free navigation language acceptable to US/Gulf/insurers, phased sanctions relief, and immediate large-scale facilitated transits. Markets respond with rapid premium compression and volume rebound. Probability of this path: low (15–20% range).

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Iran successfully embeds administrative control or intermittent interdiction; US maintains blockade and pressure; insurance stays elevated; traffic recovers only partially via dark/STS methods; proxies remain active. Functional normality delayed beyond 18 months or never fully achieved under the strict definition. Material probability (~25–35%).

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Overestimation risk: Treating diplomatic activity as more predictive of commercial normalization than historical lag in insurance and shipper behavior justifies; under-weighting how sticky “dark” shipping and alternative logistics become. Underestimation risk: Economic pain on Iran + US desire to declare success could produce a faster corridor deal than base rates suggest, and markets sometimes front-run political quiet. Strongest counter-evidence would be sustained high-volume, AIS-visible, incident-free transit for 30+ days with premiums collapsing below 1%.

- Official statements on blockade, sanctions, and free navigation.
