**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):<grok:render card_id="0d32d5" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="d6950b" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">55</argument></grok:render>

Functional normality requires **all** of the following sustained for at least 14 consecutive days without major reversal:

- **Direct military activity**: No US/Iran/Israeli strikes or significant kinetic exchanges in/near the Gulf; no active naval blockade.
- **Proxy activity**: No major Houthi/Hezbollah/IRGC-backed attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US assets.
- **Shipping security**: Strait of Hormuz transits at ≥80% of pre-war baseline (~100-140 ships/day bidirectional); no routine attacks, mining, or harassment; safe passage without mandatory Iranian permits/fees or escorts.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Consistent high-volume tanker/LNG/container flows; backlog clearance; no widespread vessel avoidance or U-turns.
- **War-risk insurance**: Private market availability at premiums ≤5-10x pre-war levels (not government backstops or cancellations); Joint War Committee removes high-risk designation.
- **Oil/LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Iran) at or near pre-war export rates (~17-20 mb/d combined through Hormuz); no sustained force majeure or major production cuts.
- **Energy market stress**: Brent/WTI volatility < historical crisis peaks; no persistent $10+/bbl risk premium attributable to Gulf disruption; Asian/European buyers resuming normal contracting.
- **Force posture**: Drawdown of heightened US/coalition naval/air presence; no Iranian IRGC dominance assertions or mass mobilization.
- **Absence of major escalation triggers**: No new attacks on commercial vessels, leadership strikes, nuclear site incidents, or proxy surges that could rapidly reverse gains.

A temporary pause, reduced (but still elevated) insurance, or partial traffic recovery does **not** qualify. Normalization is commercial/market-driven, not just a battlefield lull.<grok:render card_id="1bd0e3" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">36</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="d212a8" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">42</argument></grok:render>
