**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means observable, sustained restoration across these criteria (all must hold concurrently for “return”):
- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, drone/missile exchanges, or naval clashes involving Iran, US, Israel, or Gulf states in/near the Gulf for ≥30 days.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi/Hezbollah/IRGC-Quds Force attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or escalation triggers.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe commercial transits without routine threats, attacks, or IRGC interference; US/Gulf naval presence supports rather than contests passage.
- **Commercial transit levels**: ≥70-80% of pre-war daily averages (~50-60+ large vessels/day, including major tankers; bidirectional throughput near normal ~10M+ DWT oil/LNG equivalent).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit multiples of peacetime (e.g., <0.5% of hull value per transit vs. current elevated 1%+); broad private market availability without government backstops dominating.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exporters (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Iraq, Iran) at or near pre-war export rates; no sustained force majeure or rerouting dominance.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (no sustained risk premium spikes); inventories rebuilt; Asian/European buyers resume standard contracting without panic premiums.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Coalition drawdown from heightened alert; Iranian IRGC Navy not postured for closure; no major new deployments.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakthroughs, leadership assassinations, major proxy surges, or unresolved blockade/closure declarations.

A temporary pause, partial transits under escort/insurance backstops, or lower oil prices alone do **not** qualify.
