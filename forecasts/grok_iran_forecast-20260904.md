**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality requires concurrent satisfaction of all the following observable conditions for a sustained period (not a single day or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No sustained kinetic exchanges between major state actors (US/Iran primary; Israel secondary) involving strikes on territory, bases, or critical infrastructure in the Gulf theater. Limited, isolated incidents do not disqualify if they do not cascade.
- Proxy activity: No systematic or high-tempo proxy attacks (Houthis, Iraqi militias, others) targeting shipping, energy infrastructure, or Gulf state assets that materially disrupt operations.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial vessels transit without routine kinetic threat (drones, missiles, mines, small-boat attacks). No active mining campaigns. AIS-visible traffic not systematically forced into contested or restricted corridors under duress.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits (commodity + general) recover to ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (pre-Feb 28 2026 averages of roughly 100–180 total vessels/day depending on source; tanker component a key subset). Sustained multi-week average, not spike-and-drop.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional war-risk premiums for Hormuz/Gulf transit fall to ≤1.5–2× pre-war baseline (pre-war typically ~0.1–0.25% of hull value) and remain available without widespread capacity withdrawal or voyage-by-voyage extreme pricing. Joint War Committee listed-area status materially relaxed.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf oil exports (Hormuz + bypass) and Qatari/other LNG loadings recover to ≥80–85% of pre-war volumes on a sustained basis, with ports and terminals operating without force majeure or chronic backlog constraints driven by security.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent/WTI not chronically elevated solely due to Hormuz risk premium; implied volatility and risk premia normalize toward pre-war ranges absent other global shocks.
- Force posture of major actors: Material reduction from peak wartime presence (US carrier/strike groups, Iranian coastal/missile forces, Gulf state heightened readiness). Not full pre-war posture, but no ongoing high-tempo combat operations posture.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new high-impact events (large-scale mining, major base attacks, leadership decapitation attempts, nuclear-related incidents, or multi-state Gulf involvement) that reset the conflict trajectory.

A temporary ceasefire, lower oil prices from demand destruction or alternative supply, or a battlefield pause without commercial normalization does **not** qualify. Normality is multi-domain and commercial as well as military.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant classes (3–6):

1. **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Sustained attacks on shipping in Gulf/Hormuz approaches over years. Partial commercial adaptation (escorts, flags of convenience) but elevated risk and insurance for prolonged periods. Full functional normalization lagged formal ceasefire by 1–2+ years in commercial confidence terms. Relevance: direct chokepoint + state attacks on shipping. Breaks down: bilateral Iran-Iraq vs current US/Israel-Iran multipolar; less global energy dependence then.

2. **2019–2021 Gulf tanker attacks / Iranian seizures + 2024–2025 Red Sea/Houthi campaign**: Intermittent attacks produced sharp insurance spikes and traffic diversion; recovery to near-normal required sustained de-escalation + capacity return (months to >1 year for full insurance normalization). Relevance: same geography/insurance dynamics + proxy element. Breaks down: lower intensity than 2026 full war.

3. **Suez 1956 / 1967–1975 closures and post-1973 oil shock adaptations**: Physical or political closures; recovery of confidence and flows took quarters to years after political resolution. Relevance: chokepoint + energy market stress. Breaks down: different political drivers.

4. **Post-major Middle East wars with residual maritime risk (e.g., after 1991 Gulf War, post-2003 residual threats)**: Military cessation often preceded commercial/insurance normalization by 6–18+ months. Relevance: US force posture + regional spillover. Breaks down: no direct Hormuz closure equivalent.

5. **Broader chokepoint disruptions with insurance markets (e.g., certain Black Sea/Ukraine periods)**: Insurance and commercial caution persist after kinetic pause.

**Base-rate prior (before case-specific evidence)**: Probability of functional normality (per definition) ≈ 1–3% at 1 week; 5–10% at 1 month; 15–25% at 3 months; 30–45% at 6 months; 50–65% at 1 year; 60–75% at 18 months. These reflect historical lag between kinetic de-escalation and multi-domain commercial recovery, plus the high bar of the definition.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**

Ranked: (1) Durable political settlement or enforced de facto ceasefire removing Hormuz as leverage tool; (2) Sustained multi-week incident-free transit window that allows insurers to reprice and capacity to return; (3) Demonstrable mine clearance + US/allied escort reliability; (4) Gulf state pressure for commercial recovery + Iranian economic desperation; (5) US political incentive (midterms/energy prices).

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**

Ranked: (1) Iranian strategic interest in retaining Hormuz leverage post-leadership losses and capability degradation; (2) Insurance market structural caution (rates slow to fall; capacity sticky); (3) Residual kinetic capability + proxy activation risk; (4) Absence of verified, mutual verification mechanisms for any deal; (5) Domestic Iranian politics and US domestic politics limiting concessions; (6) Second-order effects (stranded ships backlog, port/refinery restart friction, buyer risk aversion in Asia).

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts**

Approximate conditional probabilities (order-of-magnitude, not independent):

- Major reduction in direct strikes: 1w ~5–10%; 1m ~15–25%; 3m ~35–50%; 6m ~55–70%; 1y ~70–80%; 18m ~75–85%.
- Sustained safe commercial transit (≥70–80% volumes, low incident rate): 1w <5%; 1m ~10%; 3m ~20–30%; 6m ~40–55%; 1y ~55–70%; 18m ~65–75%.
- Meaningful insurance normalization (≤1.5–2×, available capacity): lags transit by weeks–months; lower at every horizon.
- Oil/LNG export recovery (≥80–85% sustained): higher than full transit due to bypasses (~20–40% higher than pure Hormuz transit at medium horizons).
- Proxy restraint: correlated with direct strikes but with lag/risk of independent activation.
- Force drawdown: lags kinetic pause.
- No new major escalation trigger: declining probability of “clean” path as horizons lengthen due to cumulative risk.

Gating factors are safe transit + insurance + absence of escalation. Military pause alone is insufficient.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

- Core rationale: Definition requires multi-domain sustained conditions impossible in 7 days from current baseline.  
- Main gating factor: Any kinetic incident or insurance non-response.  
- What would raise: Sudden verified comprehensive ceasefire + immediate multi-day high-volume safe transit (extremely unlikely).  
- What would lower: Further attacks (already near floor).  
- Confidence: High

- What would lower: Renewed ship attacks or base strikes.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Protracted “no war, no peace” with intermittent low-to-medium intensity incidents, partial traffic via authorized/bypass/dark routes, elevated but slowly declining insurance, and gradual oil/LNG recovery via adaptation. Functional normality (full definition) remains minority outcome until at least 6–12 months; commercial normalization lags any kinetic pause.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Strongest path: Credible, verified political settlement (post-midterms or via exhaustion) removing Hormuz as Iranian leverage, combined with sustained 4–6 week incident-free high-volume transit window that triggers insurer capacity return and JWC delisting progress. Gulf states actively facilitate. Probability of this case driving the higher end of the ranges: roughly 20–30% weight.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Iran prioritizes residual leverage; intermittent attacks or mining continue; insurance stays elevated; proxies activate periodically; US force posture remains high; oil markets price chronic risk premium. Functional normality delayed beyond 18 months or achieved only partially. This case is weighted ~30–40%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Possible overestimation of normalization: Underweighting Iranian regime survival incentives post-leadership losses and the structural stickiness of insurance markets (historical precedent shows multi-month lags even after kinetic pause). Possible underestimation: Underweighting rapid commercial adaptation (bypass pipelines already functional) and US/Gulf economic pressure producing de facto corridors faster than politics suggest. Load-bearing assumptions: Iranian residual capability remains usable for coercion; insurers require multi-week clean record; no sudden major capability collapse or external shock forcing settlement. Evidence that would most challenge the view: sustained 30+ day high-volume (>70%) incident-free transit with premiums falling below 2×, or confirmed comprehensive demining + mutual verification regime.
