**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War, by horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months (as of 20 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of all the following observable conditions for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot):

- Direct military activity: No kinetic exchanges (missile/drone/naval strikes) between Iran/US/Israel/Gulf states inside the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, or immediately adjacent waters for ≥30 consecutive days.
- Proxy activity: No significant proxy attacks on commercial shipping or energy infrastructure attributable to Iranian-aligned groups (Houthis, Iraqi militias, etc.) affecting Gulf transit routes.
- Shipping security: Commercial vessels transit the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters without routine kinetic interdiction, boarding seizures, or mine threats; UKMTO/JMIC incident reports return to pre-February 2026 baseline levels.
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible commodity vessel crossings average ≥80% of 2025 pre-war daily baseline (~100–140 vessels/day range; target ≥80–110 sustained).
- War-risk insurance: Additional premiums for Hormuz transit fall to ≤2–3× peacetime levels and remain available from major underwriters without prohibitive exclusions.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf producers (ex-Iran shadow fleet) restore seaborne crude/LNG exports via Hormuz or equivalent to ≥85–90% of pre-war volumes; floating storage and stranded vessel counts normalize; force majeure declarations largely lifted.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent risk premium attributable to Hormuz falls below $10–12/bbl relative to non-Gulf benchmarks; realized volatility returns toward 2024–early 2025 ranges.
- Force posture: Major actors (US CENTCOM, IRGC Navy, Gulf navies) reduce elevated alert postures and forward deployments tied specifically to Hormuz interdiction/escort operations.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new Iranian formal claims of exclusive sovereignty/toll rights, no new US naval blockade of Iranian ports, and no large-scale leadership or nuclear-related escalatory events that re-trigger kinetic cycles.

A temporary ceasefire, isolated price drop, or partial traffic rebound does not meet the definition. All domains must be simultaneously functional.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  

Relevant classes (3–6):  
1. Iran-Iraq Tanker War (1984–88): Hundreds of attacks; strait never fully closed; traffic continued under elevated risk with US reflagging/escorts (Earnest Will). Partial commercial normalization lagged ceasefire by months to >1 year; insurance and full confidence took longer. Breaks down because current episode involves near-total effective closure and explicit sovereignty claims. Timeline signal: 6–18+ months post-ceasefire for functional shipping recovery.  
2. Suez Canal closures (1956 ~5 months; 1967–75 8 years): Political/military closure required clearance of mines/wrecks plus political settlement. 1967–75 reopening after disengagement took years for full traffic confidence. Breaks down (different geography, less energy concentration). Timeline: months to years after political deal.  
3. 2019 Gulf of Oman attacks / tanker seizures: Brief spikes in risk premiums (×10); traffic maintained; rapid partial recovery once kinetic activity subsided. Breaks down (far smaller scale). Timeline: weeks to 2–3 months.  
4. Red Sea/Houthi disruption (2023–ongoing): Sustained attacks; partial rerouting; insurance elevated; incomplete normalization years later despite diplomacy. Breaks down (alternative Cape route available). Timeline: multi-year residual risk.  
5. Ever Given Suez blockage (2021, 6 days): Physical clearance → traffic resumed in days; backlog clearance weeks. Breaks down (non-conflict). Timeline: days–weeks.  
6. Post-1991 Gulf War / Earnest Will residual: Naval presence and risk premium persisted after major combat; commercial patterns adjusted over 6–12+ months.

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence, averaged and adjusted for scale of current near-closure + sovereignty dispute):  
- 1 week: <5%  
- 1 month: 8–12%  
- 3 months: 20–25%  
- 6 months: 35–40%  
- 1 year: 50–55%  
- 18 months: 60–65%  

These reflect that military pauses historically precede commercial/insurance normalization by months, and sovereignty disputes prolong the gap.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Economic pain on all parties (Iran sanctions/blockade costs; Gulf export losses; global inflation/energy stress) creating mutual incentive for deal.  
2. Availability of partial bypasses and stock draws that limit immediate catastrophic pressure.  
3. Historical precedent that prolonged chokepoint disruption eventually produces mediated technical arrangements (Oman channel talks).  
4. US domestic and midterm political calendar potentially favoring de-escalation optics.  
5. Chinese commercial interest in stable flows (though currently risk-averse).

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Core sovereignty dispute over Hormuz administration/tolls/control (Iran maximalist; US/GCC rejection).  
2. IRGC institutional interest in retaining leverage tool; hardline vs. pragmatic factional split.  
3. Insurance and commercial risk aversion: once elevated, premiums and routing decisions lag kinetic pauses by months.  
4. Proxy residual capacity and Red Sea linkage.  
5. Mutual credibility problems after repeated MOU/ceasefire collapses.  
6. Elevated force postures and easy re-escalation triggers (missile incidents, seizures).

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued stalemate with intermittent kinetic incidents and low single-digit-to-low-double-digit traffic for 1–3 months. Eventual mediated technical corridor arrangement (Oman-centric) emerges under economic pressure, producing partial volume recovery by 6 months. Insurance and full commercial confidence lag into the 9–18 month window. Residual risk premium and elevated force posture persist beyond full “normality.” Oil markets clear via bypasses + non-Gulf supply more than via full Hormuz restoration.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Probability mass shifts left if a comprehensive package (sanctions relief + verified Iranian restraint on interdiction + neutral traffic management + US blockade lift) is reached within weeks, followed by rapid mine clearance and major insurer re-entry. Historical analogue: rapid post-ceasefire traffic rebound in limited prior Gulf episodes. Requires simultaneous political and commercial confidence restoration.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran consolidates de-facto control claims; US maintains blockade/sanctions; low-level interdiction and seizures continue; proxies expand Red Sea pressure. Traffic remains structurally suppressed; insurance stays elevated; Gulf states accelerate permanent bypass infrastructure. Normalization delayed beyond 18 months or becomes “new normal” of managed high-risk corridor. Analogue: multi-year Red Sea residual or 1967–75 Suez duration.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Overestimation risk: Base rates from Tanker War may be too optimistic because the current episode features near-total effective closure + explicit sovereignty maximalism + post-leadership-strike Iranian internal dynamics not present in 1980s. Commercial/insurance recovery could be slower than kinetic history suggests. Underestimation risk: Global economic costs (inflation, Asian buyer pressure, US political calendar) may force faster pragmatic technical deals than sovereignty rhetoric implies. Load-bearing assumptions: (1) no sudden leadership collapse or nuclear breakout that resets incentives; (2) Chinese commercial caution continues; (3) definition’s multi-domain simultaneity remains the right bar.
