### 1. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”
“Functional normality” means observable, sustained multi-domain conditions where the Persian Gulf operates as a reliable commercial and energy chokepoint with minimal disruption risk, comparable to pre-February 2026 baselines (adjusted for any permanent shifts). It requires **all** of the following for a period of at least 30 consecutive days:

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, drone/missile attacks, or naval clashes between state actors (US, Israel, Iran, Gulf states) in the Gulf or immediate environs.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi/Hezbollah/IRGC-linked attacks on shipping, ports, or infrastructure.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Daily transits at ≥70% of pre-war average (~90–100+ vessels/day sustained); no routine attacks, seizures, or forced rerouting; AIS/transponders functional without widespread "going dark."
- **Commercial transit levels**: Insurance-available commercial traffic (tankers, LNG, containers) resuming at levels supporting normal Gulf export/import flows; backlog cleared without force majeure dominance.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Lloyd’s/JWC war-risk premiums return to near pre-war levels (or modest elevation); standard coverage widely available without exclusions for Hormuz.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian, Saudi, UAE, Qatari, Iraqi, Kuwaiti exports flowing at ≥80% of pre-war capacity; no sustained production shut-ins due to security.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalized (no persistent geopolitical premium >$10–15/bbl); no rationing/shortages in Asia/Europe traceable to Gulf flows.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Coalition naval/air presence at deterrent (not surge) levels; Iranian IRGC Navy not actively harassing or claiming sovereignty-based control; Gulf states not in heightened alert.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No imminent nuclear breakout claims, leadership assassinations, or proxy surges tied to the conflict.

A temporary ceasefire, partial transits, or lower spot oil prices alone do **not** qualify. Normalization is commercial/security-led, not just military pause.<grok:render card_id="555fc5" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">16</argument></grok:render>

### 2. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior
Relevant historical cases:

- **Iran-Iraq War (1980–1988) Tanker War phase**: Relevant for Hormuz mining/attacks; shipping disruptions lasted years with insurance spikes and rerouting. Normalization took 1–3+ years post-ceasefire for full commercial flows. Breaks down: less direct US involvement, different proxy dynamics. Suggests slow recovery (6–18+ months).<grok:render card_id="7c0323" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">33</argument></grok:render>
- **1990–1991 Gulf War**: Short intense conflict; shipping/oil flows recovered in weeks–months after liberation/Kuwait liberation due to decisive victory and coalition presence. Relevant for rapid post-combat normalization when victor imposes order. Breaks down: no equivalent regime change here; Hormuz focus different.
- **Houthi Red Sea attacks (2023–2025)**: Proxy-driven shipping disruptions; partial rerouting, elevated insurance persisting 12+ months despite naval responses. Suggests 6–12 months for partial normalization.
- **Israel-Hezbollah 2006 or 2024–2025 rounds**: Short wars with quick de-escalation but lingering proxy risks; commercial recovery faster (months) but not chokepoint-specific.
- **Post-2019–2020 US-Iran tensions (Soleimani, tanker attacks)**: Brief spikes; quick return but low baseline disruption.
- **General chokepoint recoveries (e.g., Suez 1956/1967, Bab el-Mandeb)**: Often 3–12 months for full commercial normalization even after military pauses.

**Base-rate prior** (pre-case evidence): Low short-term (near 0% at 1 week/1 month), rising gradually: ~5–15% at 3 months, 30–50% at 6 months, 60–75% at 1 year, 75–85% at 18 months. Chokepoint wars show commercial lags far behind ceasefires.<grok:render card_id="df8611" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">5</argument></grok:render>

### 5. Key Drivers Toward Normalization
1. Mutual exhaustion and economic pain (high importance).
2. US desire for deal under Trump; Iranian need for sanctions relief.
3. Gulf states pushing for stability.
4. Diplomatic channels (Oman/Pakistan mediation).

### 6. Key Drivers Against Normalization
1. Iranian leverage via Hormuz "protection racket" claims (highest importance).
2. Lingering proxy risks and Israel-Hezbollah spillover.

### 7. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts
- Major reduction in direct strikes: High by 1–3 months (~70–90%).
- Sustained safe commercial transit: Low short-term, medium by 6 months.
- Insurance normalization: Lags transit by 1–3 months.
- Oil/LNG export recovery: Gated by shipping/insurance; partial by 3–6 months.
- Proxy restraint: Medium risk of flare-ups.
- Force drawdown: Likely gradual.
- No new escalation trigger: Challenging due to unresolved issues.

Gating factors: Hormuz security and insurance.
