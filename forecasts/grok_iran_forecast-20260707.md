**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (as of early July 2026):<grok:render card_id="3a95bf" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">9</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="935772" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">10</argument></grok:render>

Functional normality requires **all** of the following observable conditions sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal:

- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes (missiles, drones, naval) between US/Israel/Gulf states and Iran or its forces in/near the Gulf.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant activation or attacks by Iranian proxies (e.g., Houthis, Iraqi militias) targeting Gulf shipping, infrastructure, or US/Gulf forces.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe, unhindered transit without routine threats, forced routing through Iranian schemes, or attacks; freedom of navigation per international norms (not de facto Iranian control with tolls/selective passage).
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel transits and throughput approaching pre-war baselines (~60+ ships/day, ~10M+ DWT/day oil/LNG equivalent; currently ~27-32% of normal).<grok:render card_id="05c01e" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">73</argument></grok:render>
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Standard commercial coverage widely available at non-punitive premiums (e.g., <<1% of hull value per voyage); no reliance on government backstops or major exclusions.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar) at or near pre-war levels (~20M bpd oil equivalent through/around Hormuz) with minimal force majeure or production curtailments; no major stranded cargoes.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalized (e.g., no sustained spikes >$100+ with war premium); Asian/European buyers resuming normal contracting without panic stockpiling.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Coalition naval presence de-escalated to routine deterrence (not blockade/enforcement); Iranian IRGC Navy not dominating routing; no heightened alerts.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No imminent nuclear breakout concerns, leadership crises, or proxy flare-ups directly tied to Gulf operations.

A temporary pause, partial traffic recovery, or lower prices alone do **not** suffice. Commercial normalization (insurance, insurer confidence, buyer behavior) lags military pauses significantly.<grok:render card_id="7e3a1a" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">62</argument></grok:render>

### Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior
1. **Red Sea/Houthi crisis (2023-ongoing)**: Relevant for proxy/chokepoint disruption. Traffic dropped sharply; even post-attacks lull, recovery slow/incomplete after 2+ years due to persistent risk/insurance. Suggests 6-18+ months for full commercial normalization. Breakdown: State actor (Iran) vs. non-state; higher stakes.<grok:render card_id="d91992" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">62</argument></grok:render>
2. **Tanker War (1980s Iran-Iraq)**: Direct Gulf chokepoint attacks. Re-flagging, convoys; shipping/insurance normalized over 1-2 years post-ceasefire but with ongoing risks. Relevant for maritime focus; shorter modern timelines due to global dependence.
3. **Post-major Gulf conflicts (1991, 2003)**: Quick military pauses but infrastructure/insurance recovery months; oil flows resumed faster with coalition presence. Breakdown: No sustained Iranian Hormuz dominance.
4. **Other chokepoint disruptions** (e.g., Suez 1956/67, Bab el-Mandeb recent): Commercial normalization often 3-12 months post-resolution due to rerouting, insurance repricing, buyer caution.
5. **Post-ceasefire fragile pauses** (e.g., Yemen, Lebanon): High reversion risk; full stability rare in <6-12 months.

**Base-rate prior** (pre-case evidence): Very low at 1 week/1 month (~5-15%); low-medium at 3 months (~20-40%); medium at 6 months (~40-60%); higher at 1 year (~60-75%); substantial at 18 months (~75-85%).
