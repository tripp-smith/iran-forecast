**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means the Persian Gulf operates as a reliable, low-friction chokepoint for global energy and commerce with minimal risk premium, without reliance on ad-hoc military escorts, special permits, or government backstops. Observable thresholds (all must hold sustainably for the definition to be met):

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, drone/missile attacks, or mine-laying by state or proxy forces in/near the Gulf for at least 30 days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Iran-backed groups (e.g., Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, PMF) with no successful attacks on shipping or Gulf infrastructure.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Open international transit without Iranian-imposed TSS tolls/permits/insurance mandates or effective Iranian veto; routine naval patrols by multiple actors but no dominance by one side.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Sustained return to ~70-80%+ of pre-war daily averages (~50-60+ vessels/day through Hormuz; full Gulf traffic volumes), with diverse flags and non-Iranian tankers dominant (not just shadow fleet or escorted convoys).<grok:render card_id="105f25" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">39</argument></grok:render>

- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, etc.) exporting near pre-war volumes via Hormuz without major force majeure or rerouting; Iran able to export at market levels; no sustained global supply shock.
- **Energy market stress/volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility and risk premia normalized (e.g., no persistent $10+/bbl war premium); Asian/European buyers resuming routine contracting without panic buying.
