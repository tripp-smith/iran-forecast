**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from ~12 September 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, observable conditions across domains for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot or temporary lull):  
- Direct military activity: No major reciprocal strikes on Iranian territory, US/coalition forces/bases, or Gulf host states for ≥30 consecutive days; no active “mow-the-lawn” or tanker-for-tanker campaigns.  
- Proxy activity: Material reduction/restraint by Iran-aligned groups (especially Houthis) affecting Gulf approaches or Red Sea chokepoints; no major new proxy offensives.  
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters): Sustained safe commercial transit without routine attacks, mining threats, or enforced Iranian “permission/fee” regimes; AIS-visible + dark traffic approaching or exceeding ~80–100+ large commercial vessels/day (pre-war baseline ~125).  
- Commercial transit levels: Aggregate commodity/energy carrier volumes recovering to ≥70–80% of pre-war averages on a multi-week basis.  
- War-risk insurance: Rates falling to ≤2–3× peacetime baseline (from current ~15× / ~1.5% of hull value per voyage or higher reported ranges) with broad capacity and longer quote validity; Joint War Committee listed-area status meaningfully narrowed or conditions eased.  
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf (ex-Iran) + residual Iranian flows recovering toward pre-war aggregate levels (~20+ mb/d range historically through Hormuz-related routes), with LNG (esp. Qatar) showing sustained recovery and minimal structural outage drag.  
- Energy market stress/volatility: Brent and related benchmarks no longer pricing acute Hormuz risk premium as the dominant driver; volatility and spreads normalizing relative to broader macro factors.  
- Force posture of major actors: Visible drawdown or stabilization of US/CENTCOM forward presence and Iranian anti-access posture away from peak crisis levels; no active blockade enforcement as primary tool.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new regime-change push, nuclear breakout crisis, major host-state strikes, or secondary chokepoint closures that restart the cycle.  

A temporary ceasefire, battlefield pause, single-day high transit count, or oil-price dip does not qualify. Commercial and insurance normalization lag military pauses and are gating.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (with breakdowns):  
- Iran-Iraq Tanker War (1984–88): Prolonged asymmetric maritime attacks (~400+ ships hit); traffic continued at reduced/risk-adjusted levels with US reflagging/escorts (Earnest Will); full commercial/insurance normalization only after ceasefire and years of de-escalation. Suggests multi-year tail for full normality even after kinetic peak. Breaks down: no full closure, different technology/actors.  
- 2019 Gulf of Oman tanker attacks + subsequent tensions: Brief sharp insurance spike and caution; relatively rapid (weeks–months) partial recovery once kinetic intensity dropped. Breaks down: limited scale, no sustained blockade or regime-targeting war.  
- Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing into 2026 context): Sustained war-risk premiums, traffic diversion, partial recovery only with sustained naval presence + diplomatic pressure; multi-month to year+ for meaningful normalization. Relevant for proxy persistence and insurance stickiness.  
- Temporary Hormuz MOU/partial reopenings in 2026 itself (June window): Rapid but fragile traffic rebound to still-subnormal levels, followed by collapse on renewed attacks. Shows commercial confidence is highly conditional and lagging.  
- Broader chokepoint disruptions (Suez 1967–75 full closure; Ever Given 2021; Black Sea 2022): Physical or high-risk closures produce multi-month to multi-year recovery lags for volumes/insurance once reopened, especially with residual mines/threats or political unresolved issues.  

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment, timeline to functional normality meeting the multi-domain definition):  
- 1 week: <5%  
- 1 month: 5–10%  
- 3 months: 15–25%  
- 6 months: 30–45%  
- 1 year: 50–65%  
- 18 months: 60–75%  

These reflect that maritime commercial/insurance recovery historically lags kinetic pauses, proxy activity persists, and unresolved political objectives (regime pressure, control of the strait) extend timelines. Current war’s intensity and dual-blockade character push the prior toward the longer end of historical ranges.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
Ranked: (1) Exhaustion + economic pain on both sides creating space for a durable deal or managed “no-war/no-peace” with safe-passage protocols; (2) Effective US/coalition escorts + demining producing sustained low-incident corridors that rebuild insurer/owner confidence; (3) Iranian restraint (or capacity degradation) on anti-shipping + proxy de-escalation; (4) Alternative export routes (pipelines, Red Sea) + non-Gulf supply growth reducing Hormuz criticality enough for partial commercial recovery; (5) Political off-ramps (midterms, leadership changes, third-party mediation).

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
Ranked: (1) Unresolved core political objectives (US regime pressure/sanctions + Iranian insistence on strait control/fees + linkage to other theaters); (2) Persistent Iranian A2/AD, mining, and asymmetric attack capacity + proxy (Houthi) expansion creating dual-chokepoint risk; (3) Insurance market stickiness and owner risk aversion (rates “quick up, slow down”); (4) Tit-for-tat dynamics and “mow-the-lawn” cycles that restart after every lull; (5) Second-order effects (Gulf state caution, buyer diversification away from region, force posture inertia).

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Protracted “no war/no peace” with intermittent kinetic spikes, managed but subnormal corridors (US-facilitated + some dark/Iranian-permitted traffic), elevated but slowly declining insurance, partial Gulf export recovery via workarounds and alternative routes, and oil markets that gradually price in chronic rather than acute risk. Functional normality (full definition) most likely only in the 12–18 month window if a durable arrangement emerges.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Durable comprehensive agreement (ceasefire + demining + non-fee transit + sanctions relief elements) within weeks–months, verified by sustained high transit volumes, rapid insurance compression, and proxy de-escalation. Probability of this path is low given collapsed prior MOUs, but would push 3–6 month probabilities materially higher.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Entrenched dual-chokepoint (Hormuz + Bab al-Mandeb) contest, Iranian parallel control regime, continued tanker/host-state exchanges, and insurance/owner aversion that keeps volumes structurally depressed. Markets adapt via non-Gulf supply and pipelines; normality (per definition) delayed beyond 18 months. This is a credible downside given current drivers.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible overestimation of normalization: Underweights Iranian strategic interest in prolonged leverage, insurance market structural stickiness, and second-order buyer diversification that permanently lowers Hormuz dependence (and thus pressure for full reopening). Possible underestimation: Overweights political deadlock; underweights cumulative economic pain, Iranian capacity degradation from sustained strikes, and US ability to impose effective protected corridors that force commercial recovery even without a formal deal. Load-bearing assumptions include continued Iranian ability to contest the strait asymmetrically and absence of a sudden decisive political shift. Evidence that would prove the view wrong: sustained multi-week 80–100+ vessel days with collapsing insurance rates and no kinetic response, or conversely a major new escalation (nuclear, major infrastructure destruction, or multi-state widening).
