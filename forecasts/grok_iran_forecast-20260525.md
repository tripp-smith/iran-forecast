**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality means observable, sustained conditions across multiple domains where commercial and strategic actors treat the Gulf as a low-risk operating environment comparable to pre-February 2026 baselines (adjusted for any permanent shifts). It requires *all* of the following to hold simultaneously for a sustained period (e.g., 2–4 weeks without reversal):  
- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes, mine-laying, or naval confrontations by state actors in/near the Gulf.  
- **Proxy activity**: No significant attacks on shipping, infrastructure, or Gulf states by Iran-aligned groups (e.g., Houthis, Hezbollah remnants).  
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Free navigation without mandatory Iranian permits, tolls/fees, or credible threats; US/Gulf naval presence supports rather than contests transit.  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel transits recover to at least 70–80% of pre-war daily averages (~120–140 vessels/day); no widespread loitering or rerouting via Cape of Good Hope.  
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Private insurers resume standard coverage at pre-war or modestly elevated rates (not prohibitive premiums or government backstops as primary option); Joint War Committee removes or narrows high-risk designation.  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, etc.) export at near-capacity with minimal force majeure declarations; Iranian exports resume without blockade.  
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained >$10–15/bbl swings from Gulf risk); no chronic Asian/European fuel shortages.  
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Israeli/Gulf forces de-escalate from wartime readiness (e.g., reduced carrier groups, mine countermeasures); Iran withdraws heavy IRGC naval presence or threats.  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No unresolved nuclear breakthroughs, regime collapse risks, or proxy flare-ups that could rapidly reverse gains.  

A temporary ceasefire or partial transit does not qualify. Lower oil prices alone do not qualify if driven by demand destruction rather than restored flows.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot** (as of ~May 25, 2026)  
A fragile ceasefire has held since early April (extended indefinitely but strained), with direct US/Israeli strikes paused but US naval blockade of Iranian ports ongoing. Iran imposes de facto control via "Persian Gulf Strait Authority" rules, fees/tolls (~$150k+), bilateral agreements favoring allies (China/Russia), and denials for adversaries. Shipping traffic remains severely depressed (down 70–95% at peaks). Insurance is prohibitive or withdrawn for many; governments (including US) provide backstops. Negotiations show "slight progress" on a framework to reopen Hormuz, but core disputes persist over Iran's nuclear stockpile/HEU and control of the strait. Proxies and Lebanon tensions continue at lower intensity. Oil markets reflect ongoing stress but partial adaptation. **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **Tanker War (Iran-Iraq War, 1980s)**: Relevant for maritime chokepoint attacks, reflagging, insurance spikes. Normalization took 1–3+ years post-ceasefire for full commercial confidence. Breaks down due to less direct US involvement initially. Suggests 6–18+ months.  
- **1990–91 Gulf War (Desert Storm)**: Quick military victory, rapid coalition naval clearance. Commercial shipping/oil flows recovered in weeks–months. Breaks down: no Iranian regime survival with asymmetric tools. Suggests faster (1–6 months).  
- **Red Sea/Houthi disruptions (2023–2025)**: Proxy-driven; partial rerouting, elevated insurance. Recovery slow and incomplete until pressure eased. Suggests prolonged commercial lag.  
- **Post-2003 Iraq stabilization attempts / Libya 2011**: Regime change/disruption led to years of proxy/instability.  
- **Other chokepoint recoveries (e.g., Suez 1967–75, partial Gulf incidents)**: Often 3–12+ months for insurance/transit normalization.  

**Base-rate prior** (pre-case adjustment): Very low at short horizons (~5–15% by 1 month), rising gradually (30–50% by 6 months, 60–75% by 18 months). **6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Mutual exhaustion + economic pain (high importance).  
2. US leverage via blockade/naval presence and Gulf allies.  
3. Diplomatic channels (Pakistan/Qatar mediation).  
4. Global pressure from energy buyers (Asia/Europe).  

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked higher overall)  
1. Core disputes (Iran nuclear retention + strait veto power) — gating.  
2. Iranian incentive to lock in gains via fees/control during pause.  
3. Regime survival logic: asymmetric tools remain viable.  
4. Proxy/Hezbollah risks and Israeli pushback.  
5. Insurer/shipper risk aversion (second-order lag).  
6. Potential regime fragmentation or hardliner dominance.  

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate by horizon; key gating: sustained safe transit + insurance + no escalation)  
- Major reduction in direct strikes: Higher (50%+ by 1–3 months).  
- Sustained safe commercial transit: Low short-term, medium longer.  
- Insurance normalization: Lags military (very low short, medium by 6–12 months).  
- Oil/LNG export recovery: Tied to transit; partial faster than full.  
- Proxy restraint: Medium risk of flare.  
- Force drawdown: Possible but conditional.  
- No new escalation trigger: Challenging due to nuclear/strait.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Shaky extended ceasefire with incremental, contested Hormuz openings (some compliant traffic, selective fees). Partial military de-escalation by 3–6 months, commercial normalization lagging to 6–12+ months amid high insurance and caution. Periodic flare risks. Negotiated framework possible but incomplete on nuclear/strait core issues.  

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Decisive US/Iran deal resolves nuclear minimally and opens strait freely; Gulf states + China pressure Iran; insurers quickly re-enter. Strong exhaustion + economic incentives. Probability ~20–30% of baseline path.  

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks fail; Iran entrenches control/fees; proxies activate; Israel/US strike again or blockade tightens. Regime uses disruption for leverage/survival. Commercial rerouting becomes semi-permanent. Strongest case given Iranian strategic goals and historical asymmetry.  

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
I may overestimate normalization speed by underweighting Iranian incentives to lock in strait gains and insurer hysteresis (commercial actors need sustained evidence, not promises). Conversely, I may underestimate US leverage and global pressure for energy security, potentially accelerating a messy compromise. Load-bearing: Assumption that exhaustion dominates over regime survival logic. Evidence thin on private insurer intentions and exact transit data.
