**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain, observable):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following to hold simultaneously and sustainably (not just a pause or temporary dip in prices/activity):

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes by US/Israel on Iranian targets or Iranian missile/drone attacks on US/Israeli/Gulf assets for at least 30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint (≥70% reduction from peak) by Iranian-backed groups (Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, PMF, etc.) in attacks on shipping or Gulf infrastructure.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No IRGC attacks/seizures on commercial vessels; US/Iranian naval forces in de-conflicted postures allowing safe passage without routine escorts or AIS evasion.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic ≥70% of pre-war baseline (~3,000 vessels/month; ~100+/day average, with oil/LNG tankers at ~15-20 mb/d effective flow).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Standard P&I/war-risk cover widely available at premiums ≤2-3x pre-war levels (not suspended or 10x+ hull percentages); private insurers not requiring government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Persian Gulf exporters (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Iraq, Kuwait, Iran) operating at ≥80% of pre-war export rates via Hormuz without major rerouting losses or stranded cargoes.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalized (e.g., <30-day realized vol in typical peacetime range); no sustained premiums from supply fears; Asian/European buyers resuming normal spot/term contracts.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US Fifth Fleet and allies at routine (not surged) deployment; Iranian IRGC Navy not enforcing tolls/blockade; no active US naval blockade.
