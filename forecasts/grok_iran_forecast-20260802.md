**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (onset ~28 February 2026) by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from 2 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Observable, multi-domain criteria that must hold concurrently and sustainably (not a temporary pause):  
- Direct military activity: No sustained US/Iran/Israel strikes on each other’s territory, bases, or forces in the Gulf theater; no major air/missile campaigns.  
- Proxy activity: Material restraint by IRGC-linked proxies (no sustained attacks on shipping, energy infrastructure, or US/Gulf partners at scale).  
- Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial transit free of kinetic attacks, mines, or coercive interdiction; dual or multilateral traffic management without exclusive Iranian control asserted by force.  
- Commercial transit levels: Daily transits sustained near pre-war baseline (~120–140 vessels/day, roughly half tankers) for consecutive weeks, not intermittent spikes.  
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war premiums returned to or near pre-war levels (~0.1–0.25% of hull value) with broad capacity availability; cargo war cover normalized.  
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf (non-Iranian + residual Iranian) oil exports and Qatari/UAE LNG flows restored to near pre-war volumes without large shut-ins or reliance on shadow fleets/STS workarounds as primary mode.  
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent and Asian LNG spot prices and volatility metrics no longer dominated by Hormuz risk premium; physical availability and freight rates consistent with normal supply.  
- Force posture of major actors: Material reduction from peak wartime US/CENTCOM presence and Iranian coastal/naval densification; no active large-scale escort or blockade operations as the default.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No nuclear breakout signals, leadership assassinations, major proxy mobilizations, or new chokepoint closures (e.g., Bab al-Mandeb linkage) that reset risk.  

A temporary ceasefire, partial traffic rebound, or price drop alone does not qualify.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot** (as of early August 2026)  
War ongoing ~5 months. June 17 Islamabad MOU produced partial reopening and traffic recovery (peaks of ~50–70+ daily transits in late June, still well below ~130 pre-war). Subsequent Iranian attacks on vessels, US retaliatory strikes (including large packages in early July), Iranian responses against Gulf bases, reimposition of US measures, and Iranian assertions of route control caused repeated collapses. Recent traffic frequently single-digit to low tens of vessels; some days near-zero outbound tankers. Mines and dual contested lanes persist. Insurance remains elevated (multiples of peacetime; reports of 3–10% hull ranges earlier, still high). Oil/LNG flows severely constrained relative to baseline; Qatar LNG heavily impacted. Force postures elevated; negotiations stalled or broken. **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (with breakdowns):  
- Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, 1980s, especially 1984–88 peak): Prolonged attacks on shipping in Gulf/Hormuz approaches. Partial commercial adaptation (escorts, flags) occurred within months, but full normalization of security, insurance, and unfettered transit took years after major fighting ended. Suggests multi-year tail for full functional normality after kinetic peak. Breaks down because it was intra-Gulf bilateral with superpower restraint; today’s case involves direct US-Iran exchanges and nuclear overlay.  
- 2019–2021 Gulf tanker/drone attacks + Soleimani period: Discrete spikes in risk, insurance, and some rerouting; relatively rapid partial recovery (weeks–months) once kinetic intensity dropped, but residual elevated premiums and caution persisted. Suggests 1–6 months for partial commercial recovery if kinetic stops cleanly. Breaks down: no full-scale war or sustained Hormuz closure.  
- Red Sea/Houthi disruptions (2023–present, overlapping): Insurance spikes, major rerouting around Africa, partial traffic recovery with escorts/coalitions but incomplete normalization of costs/security. Multi-month to multi-year for full commercial/insurance reset even with reduced attacks. Relevant for insurance and shipping behavior persistence.  
- 1990–91 Gulf War and post-liberation Kuwait: Rapid military resolution followed by relatively quick restoration of oil flows and shipping once major combat ended, aided by overwhelming coalition dominance. Suggests faster (months) if decisive military outcome; less relevant if war ends in negotiated ambiguity.  
- Broader chokepoint disruptions (Suez 1956/1967/2021, earlier Hormuz threats): Physical/closure events often resolve in weeks–months for traffic; insurance and behavioral caution lag. Political/contested control cases take longer.  

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence, timeline for functional normality meeting the full definition): 1 week ~1–3%; 1 month ~5–10%; 3 months ~15–25%; 6 months ~30–45%; 1 year ~50–65%; 18 months ~60–75%. Anchored in the rarity of rapid full multi-domain normalization after multi-month kinetic disruption involving a major power and a chokepoint under contested sovereignty.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
Ranked: (1) Mutual interest in avoiding prolonged economic damage (oil/LNG revenues for producers, global price/volatility costs); (2) US political desire for de-escalation/deal under Trump administration; (3) Iranian need for sanctions relief/export revenue and regime survival; (4) Gulf states’ preference for stable transit; (5) Adaptation capacity (pipelines, alternative routes, escorts) that can support partial flows.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
Ranked: (1) Iranian strategic interest in retaining Hormuz leverage as core deterrent/bargaining chip; (2) Contested sovereignty/interpretation of any deal language on transit management; (3) Tit-for-tat dynamics and domestic political constraints on both sides; (4) Proxy and secondary theater linkages (Lebanon, Houthis, etc.); (5) Insurance and commercial risk aversion that persists after kinetic peaks; (6) Elevated force postures creating accidental escalation risk.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Intermittent low-to-medium intensity exchanges or fragile pauses continue for months. Partial traffic recovers during lulls but remains well below baseline with elevated insurance and dual contested management. A more durable political arrangement emerges only after further economic pain or leadership calculations shift, producing gradual rather than rapid multi-domain normalization. Residual risk premium and caution persist beyond any formal ceasefire.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Decisive US military degradation of Iranian coastal/missile capabilities combined with a face-saving deal that restores free transit and sanctions relief. Gulf states and insurers rapidly regain confidence; traffic and exports rebound within weeks of kinetic stop. Probability of this path is low given current patterns.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran prioritizes long-term Hormuz leverage; negotiations fail or produce only temporary, contested arrangements. Low-intensity attacks, insurance elevation, and partial shut-ins continue into 2027. Secondary effects (Houthi linkage, domestic pressures) compound. This is a material probability path.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible overestimation of normalization at longer horizons: Iranian regime survival incentives and demonstrated willingness to absorb costs may keep the strait as a permanent contested zone longer than base rates from less existential conflicts suggest. Possible underestimation of near-term commercial adaptation: Markets and Gulf producers have already shown ability to move some volume via alternatives and shadow methods; a clean kinetic pause could unlock faster insurance/traffic recovery than historical tanker-war lags imply. Load-bearing assumptions include continued mutual aversion to full-scale unrestricted war and no sudden nuclear or leadership-decapitation shocks. Evidence base on exact current daily transits and precise insurance quotes is incomplete/variable day-to-day.
