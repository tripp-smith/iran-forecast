**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain): 

Functional normality requires **all** of the following sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal: 
- No direct kinetic military activity (missile/drone strikes, naval engagements) between Iran, US, Israel, or Gulf states in/near the Gulf. 
- Proxy/militia activity restrained at pre-2025 baseline levels (no major attacks on shipping, infrastructure, or Gulf states). 
- Commercial shipping/transit through Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters at ≥70% of pre-war daily average (~100+ vessels/day, with oil/LNG flows near normal volumes). 
- War-risk insurance available at premiums ≤1% of hull value per transit (or equivalent normalized conditions with routine coverage). 
- Oil and LNG export continuity from Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Iraq, Iran) at ≥80% pre-war levels without forced rerouting or significant storage issues. 
- Energy market stress low: Brent volatility < normal crisis levels, no sustained premiums/spikes from Gulf risk. 
- Major actors’ force posture de-escalated (US/Gulf naval presence reduced to routine patrols; Iranian IRGC Navy not actively harassing or claiming sovereignty enforcement). 
- No new major escalation triggers (e.g., nuclear breakthroughs, leadership changes triggering retaliation, or proxy surges). 

A ceasefire or pause alone does **not** suffice; commercial behavior, insurer willingness, and sustained flows are decisive.
