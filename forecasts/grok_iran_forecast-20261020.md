**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by the horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from approximately 20 October 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained (minimum 30 consecutive days of observed compliance) multi-domain conditions. It is not a ceasefire declaration, a temporary drop in oil prices, or a pause in major strikes. Required elements:

- Direct military activity: No significant kinetic exchanges (strikes, ballistic/cruise missiles, large drone barrages) between US/Israeli forces and Iranian state forces.
- Proxy activity: No significant attacks by Iranian-aligned proxies (Houthis, Hezbollah, Iraqi groups, etc.) on commercial shipping, energy infrastructure, or allied military targets linked to the conflict.
- Shipping security: No attacks, mining, or credible interdiction threats in the Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters (Persian Gulf, Gulf of Oman). Free commercial transit without Iranian permits, fees, or US-facilitated “dark”/escorted arrangements as the dominant mode.
- Commercial transit levels: Vessel transits (AIS-visible and total) approaching pre-war baselines of roughly 80–100+ per day, with normal routing and without military coordination as a prerequisite for the majority of traffic.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Joint War Committee high-risk designation lifted or premiums returned to near-pre-conflict multiples (low single-digit percentage or less of hull value for a transit); broad, commercially available cover without extreme additional premiums or exclusions.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Combined flows through Hormuz and Gulf export terminals at or near pre-war levels (roughly 15–20 million barrels per day equivalent including associated products), without predominant reliance on ship-to-ship transfers outside the strait, pipeline bypasses as the primary route, or shadow-fleet workarounds.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent and regional differentials not elevated primarily by Gulf risk (return toward pre-war ranges with volatility driven by non-conflict factors).
- Force posture of major actors: US naval presence reduced from wartime escort/blockade levels; Iranian IRGC Navy and coastal forces not conducting active interdiction or mining operations; no large-scale reinforcements tied to the conflict.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new leadership assassinations, major nuclear-related strikes, blockade expansions, or proxy surges that restart the above disruptions.

Temporary volume recovery via escorts or pipelines, or a political pause, does not satisfy the definition.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot**  
As of 19–20 October 2026 (approximately Day 235 of the conflict that began with US-Israeli strikes on 28 February 2026):

- Direct strikes: US kinetic operations against Iranian territory are paused by presidential pledge until after the 3 November midterm elections. Iranian missile/drone activity against regional targets continues at lower levels. IRGC claims recent shootdowns of US drones.
- Maritime: Iranian/IRGC attacks on commercial shipping are at the highest tempo of the war (multiple reports of 12–26+ incidents in October, including sinkings of container ships and tankers; attacks reported by projectiles, claimed mines, drones, and RPGs). Attacks have extended beyond the main transit lanes (near Saudi Arabia, Oman, UAE). UKMTO and JMIC maintain severe threat assessments. US CENTCOM reports facilitating tens of transits per day and redirecting vessels attempting to evade the blockade; independent AIS tracking shows far lower visible commercial traffic (reports of single-digit or sub-1% of pre-war daily averages in recent periods).
- Transit and flows: Pre-war baselines were roughly 80–140 vessels/day. Recent US-facilitated averages cited around 25–30/day in mid-October windows, with cumulative facilitated transits in the thousands since summer but still well below normal. Oil flows through Hormuz have fluctuated between roughly 60–80% of pre-war levels in some periods but drop sharply with attack spikes; overall Gulf exports partially supported by Saudi/Emirati pipeline bypasses. Brent has traded in the $105–110 range (pre-war nearer $70–75).
- Insurance and commercial: War-risk premiums reported at extreme multiples (reports of 10–30× peacetime levels or 7.5–10% of hull value). Major carriers have restricted or suspended Hormuz routing. Hundreds of vessels are loitering or waiting outside the strait.
- Diplomacy and proxies: Indirect talks continue via mediators. Iran maintains conditions for any limited reopening (end to US blockade of Iranian ports, sanctions relief, asset release, ceasefire on other fronts). Houthis are conducting an air and maritime campaign against Saudi targets. Hezbollah readiness has been raised in reporting.
- Other: US blockade of Iranian ports remains in force. Iran claims effective control of the strait and has threatened undersea cables. Leadership transition (Mojtaba Khamenei) is established. No comprehensive settlement exists.

Confirmed elements include the midterm pause pledge, continued high-tempo shipping attacks (UKMTO/JMIC/IMO reporting), elevated oil prices, and active blockade. Attribution of specific incidents (mines vs. projectiles) and exact transit volumes remain partly disputed between US military statements, Iranian claims, and independent AIS/Kpler-type data. Iranian statements on “complete closure” and US statements of “control” are treated as competing claims rather than established facts.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant historical cases:

- 1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, peak 1984–1988): Sustained attacks on neutral shipping in the Gulf and Hormuz for years. US Operation Earnest Will escorts (1987–1988) allowed continued but reduced transit under protection. Full cessation of attacks and commercial normalization followed the August 1988 ceasefire, not during active hostilities. Relevant because of asymmetric mining/speedboat tactics against commercial traffic and US escort response. Breakdown: no nuclear dimension, different leadership incentives, and eventual total war exhaustion. Suggests multi-month to multi-year disruption even with escorts; functional commercial recovery after political settlement.
- 2019 Gulf tanker/shipping incidents and Soleimani period: Limited attacks and one major escalation, followed by de-escalation. Shipping continued with elevated insurance; no sustained closure. Relevant for short-lived Iranian asymmetric signaling. Breakdown: far lower intensity and no blockade. Suggests weeks-to-months recovery after de-escalation.
- Houthi Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb campaign (2023–2025/6): Years of attacks produced permanent partial rerouting, elevated insurance that declined only slowly after attack pauses, and traffic still substantially below baseline months after relative quiet. Relevant for insurance and commercial behavior lagging kinetic pauses, and for proxy sustainment. Breakdown: lower strategic stakes for a major power and alternative routes available. Suggests 6–18 months for meaningful commercial/insurance normalization after threat reduction.
- Brief chokepoint or conflict interruptions (e.g., early 1991 Gulf War period): Rapid military resolution produced quick volume recovery. Relevant as a lower bound. Breakdown: decisive conventional outcome and short duration.
- Other Gulf disruptions and insurance market behavior: Insurers reprice quickly upward and slowly downward; 60–90+ days of incident-free transit is commonly cited as a threshold for material premium relief.

Base-rate prior (before current-case evidence): Near-term functional normality is rare during active asymmetric campaigns against shipping. Partial escorted or volume recovery can appear within weeks of a political pause, but multi-domain normality (especially insurance, free transit, and absence of proxy/direct activity) typically requires 6–18 months after sustained de-escalation and a credible political settlement. Probability of full functional normality within 1 month is low (<15% in most analogues); within 6 months moderate only after clear settlement; within 12–18 months higher but not certain if root political issues remain.

**5. Evidence Ledger**  
Key items (direction relative to normalization; confidence; status):

- US presidential pledge of no strikes on Iran before 3 November 2026 (toward near-term stability; medium-high; confirmed by public statements).
- Highest October shipping attack tempo of the war, including sinkings and extension beyond main lanes (strongly against; high; confirmed by UKMTO, JMIC, multiple reputable outlets; some attribution details disputed).
- US-facilitated transits continuing at roughly 25–30/day in recent windows while AIS-visible traffic is far lower (mixed: shows partial military-enabled flow, against full normality; medium; US statements vs. independent tracking).
- Oil flows partially recovered via Hormuz + pipelines but volatile and still below consistent pre-war levels; Brent ~$105–110 (against full normality; medium-high; Kpler and market reporting).
- Extreme war-risk premiums (7.5–10%+ or high multiples) and carrier restrictions (strongly against; high; insurer and trade reporting).
- Ongoing mediated talks and Iranian seven-day/limited ceasefire proposal still on table (mildly toward; low-medium; official statements; conditions far apart).
- Active Houthi campaign against Saudi targets and raised Hezbollah readiness reporting (against; medium; multiple reports, some disputed in scale).
- US blockade of Iranian ports in force since mid-July; Iran linking reopening to its end plus sanctions relief (against near-term; high; official).
- IRGC claims of mines and control; US claims of control and high success rate of facilitated transits (disputed; medium; competing official claims).
- Midterm elections and post-election options language from both sides (mixed/uncertain; medium; public statements).

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
Ranked: (1) US military ability to sustain escorts and blockade, creating economic pressure and partial flow restoration; (2) Mutual interest in avoiding unlimited escalation (oil markets, Iranian regime survival, US electoral and fiscal costs); (3) Existence of ongoing mediated talks and prior MoU experience; (4) Pipeline bypass capacity reducing absolute dependence on Hormuz; (5) Historical pattern that sustained commercial attacks eventually become costly for the attacker if major-power escorts hold.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
Ranked: (1) Iran’s demonstrated capacity and incentive to sustain asymmetric shipping attacks as leverage while US strikes are paused; (2) Hard Iranian conditions (blockade end, sanctions, assets, other fronts) versus US focus on nuclear constraints and blockade maintenance; (3) Insurance and commercial inertia—premiums and routing decisions lag kinetic changes by months; (4) Proxy activation (Houthis already active; potential for others); (5) Post-midterm risk of resumed major US strikes if talks fail, restarting the cycle; (6) Leadership and regime survival incentives under new Supreme Leader.

Gating factors for the joint outcome are sustained attack cessation + insurance/commercial recovery, which lag any military pause.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: Near-zero during active high-tempo shipping campaigns in historical analogues.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward due to ongoing attacks and extreme insurance/transit conditions.  
- Core rationale: Attacks are at war-high levels; free commercial transit and insurance normality are absent. A sudden multi-domain shift in one week is historically rare.  
- Main gating factor: Immediate cessation of shipping attacks and commercial response.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Verified multi-day halt in all attacks plus rapid insurer signals.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Additional sinkings or mine claims.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: Low single digits to low teens for full functional recovery one month after peak disruption in tanker-war and Red Sea analogues.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly upward for possible post-midterm de-escalation signals, but offset by current attack tempo and hard conditions.  
- Core rationale: Midterms create a decision window, but insurance, routing, and proxy dynamics move slowly. Partial flows can continue under escort without meeting the definition.  
- Main gating factor: Post-election US posture and Iranian attack decisions.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Credible post-midterm pause agreement with verified attack halt.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumed major US strikes or further proxy expansion.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 18%  
- Base-rate anchor: Low-to-mid teens to low 20s in cases with a political pause but unresolved root issues.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest upward for talk channels and US pressure capacity; downward for demonstrated Iranian attack capacity and insurance lag.  
- Core rationale: Possible limited de-escalation after elections, but sustained free transit and insurance normalization remain unlikely without a more durable settlement. Workarounds do not count.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance market and commercial operator confidence.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: 30+ days incident-free free transit and premium declines.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Renewed high-tempo attacks or nuclear-related escalation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 32%  
- Base-rate anchor: Roughly 25–40% in analogues once a sustained pause or limited settlement appears.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Neutral to slightly downward given current leverage tactics and proxy activity.  
- Core rationale: Enough time for a post-election deal or exhaustion to reduce attacks and begin commercial recovery, but multi-domain normality (especially insurance and force posture) is not the base case.  
- Main gating factor: Durable attack cessation long enough for insurer and operator behavior to shift.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Formal limited settlement plus 60+ days of normal-pattern transit.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumption of major strikes or Houthi/other proxy expansion.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 52%  
- Base-rate anchor: Around 45–60% in longer tanker-war and Red Sea-style cases once political pressure accumulates.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly upward for economic costs on both sides and historical tendency toward eventual stabilization.  
- Core rationale: Cumulative pressure (blockade effects, oil market costs, reconstruction needs) raises the chance of a workable arrangement, after which commercial recovery can occur. Residual risks of spoiler activity remain.  
- Main gating factor: Political settlement sufficient to end the attack campaign.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Verified nuclear constraints plus sanctions relief package with observed transit normalization.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Regime instability or major new front.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 68%  
- Base-rate anchor: Higher (60–75%) for functional recovery over this horizon in most historical maritime disruption cases once fighting intensity declines.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Mild downward for the depth of the current dispute and insurance inertia observed in Red Sea analogues.  
- Core rationale: Sufficient time for economic and military realities to force a lower-intensity equilibrium or limited deal, allowing transit, insurance, and export conditions to approach the operational definition. Full pre-war “normal” may still not be reached, but functional normality is more probable than not.  
- Main gating factor: Absence of a new major escalation cycle.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Sustained multi-month incident-free period with force drawdown.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Persistent low-level attacks or nuclear breakout dynamics.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-Low  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued high maritime tension through the midterms, followed by a post-election decision point. Most probable outcome is a limited or tacit de-escalation (reduced attack tempo, continued partial escorted flows) rather than rapid multi-domain normality. Insurance and free commercial transit lag any kinetic pause by months. Oil volumes remain partially supported by workarounds. A durable settlement remains possible within 6–12 months but is not the base case.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm US restraint combined with Iranian acceptance of a limited package (partial sanctions relief and blockade adjustment in exchange for attack halt and nuclear steps). Incident-free transit for 45–60 days triggers insurer re-entry and operator return. Probability of this path materializing by 6 months is low-to-moderate; it would push the 6-month and 1-year forecasts upward by 15–25 points.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran sustains shipping attacks as core leverage; post-midterm US strikes resume; proxies expand; insurance market treats the Gulf as a structural high-risk zone. Functional normality is delayed beyond 18 months. This path is plausible if regime survival incentives dominate or if nuclear issues remain unresolved.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case for faster normalization: US escorts have already restored substantial oil flows in periods of lower attacks; economic pain on Iran is real; both sides have previously accepted temporary arrangements. The forecast may underweight the speed of volume recovery once attacks drop. Strongest case for prolonged instability: Iran has successfully used the pause to intensify maritime pressure; insurance markets in Red Sea analogues stayed elevated long after kinetic pauses; leadership change has not moderated maximalist conditions. The forecast may overstate the probability of a durable settlement within 12–18 months and understate residual low-level attack risk. Load-bearing assumptions are that Iran’s attack capacity is finite under blockade and that commercial actors will respond within months to a verified pause. Evidence that would prove the view wrong includes either a rapid multi-domain normalization by 3 months or continued high-tempo attacks plus insurance exclusion beyond 12 months.
