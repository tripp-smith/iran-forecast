**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon (1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained (minimum 30 consecutive days, preferably longer) satisfaction of all of the following observable conditions:

- Direct military activity: No significant kinetic exchanges (missile, drone, air, or naval strikes) between US/Israeli and Iranian forces or against Gulf state targets. Isolated incidents below pre-war baseline noise levels do not count as normality.
- Proxy activity: Houthi, Iraqi militia, and other Iranian-aligned proxy attacks on shipping, energy infrastructure, or bases at or near pre-war levels (near-zero for commercial shipping).
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: No credible threat of mines, missile/drone strikes, or IRGC boarding/interdiction of commercial vessels; US/allied protective corridors no longer required as the primary mode of transit.
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible and dark/STS-adjusted daily commercial vessel crossings through Hormuz averaging ≥70% of the Feb 2025–Feb 2026 baseline (roughly 60+ large commercial vessels/day including tankers).
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional war-risk premiums for Hormuz transits at ≤1% of hull value (and preferably ≤0.5%), with standard 7-day cover readily available without special government facilities or consortia. Lloyd’s Joint War Committee designation of the area as a high-risk zone lifted or effectively nullified.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf (non-Iranian) crude exports via Hormuz + bypasses averaging ≥90% of pre-war levels on a sustained basis; Qatari LNG exports recovering toward pre-war volumes without major facility outages from war damage.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent crude sustained in a range consistent with pre-war supply/demand balance without a persistent geopolitical risk premium of >$15–20/bbl attributable to Hormuz; realized volatility near historical norms.
- Force posture of major actors: Material drawdown of US carrier/strike groups and blockade posture against Iranian ports; Iranian IRGC naval and coastal missile deployments returned toward peacetime posture.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new US/Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear or critical energy infrastructure, no Iranian massed retaliation, no formal blockade reimposition or closure declaration, and no major proxy escalation outside the Gulf.

A temporary ceasefire, a drop in oil prices alone, or a pause in strikes does not meet this standard. Partial recovery of flows under high insurance and military protection does not constitute normality.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran–Iraq, ~1984–1988)**: Direct analogue for sustained attacks on Gulf shipping. Traffic never fully stopped; US escorts (Operation Earnest Will) enabled continuation. Full stabilization followed only the August 1988 ceasefire. Suggests multi-year timelines when underlying interstate war continues. Breakdown: different actors, no nuclear dimension, lower Iranian missile/drone capability.  
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Proxy maritime disruption of a chokepoint. Elevated insurance and rerouting persisted for 2+ years despite pauses and military operations. Full commercial normalization has not occurred. Relevant for insurance lag and commercial risk aversion. Breakdown: secondary chokepoint, different principal combatants.  
- **Shorter Gulf/Hormuz crises (e.g., 2019 tanker incidents, limited 2020s flare-ups)**: Recovered in weeks to a few months once kinetic activity stopped and insurance adjusted. Suggests faster recovery if fighting ends cleanly.  
- **Other chokepoint disruptions (Suez blockages, prior Red Sea events)**: Commercial and insurance normalization typically lags kinetic resolution by 1–6 months.  

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment)**: 1 week ~2–5%; 1 month ~5–10%; 3 months ~15–25%; 6 months ~30–45%; 1 year ~50–65%; 18 months ~60–75%. These reflect the historical difficulty of rapid full commercial normalization while underlying conflict or residual capability persists.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- Sustained mediation channel (Qatar) and mutual interest in avoiding further economic damage.  
- Demonstrated ability of Gulf producers to adapt (protected corridor, bypasses, STS).  
- US domestic political incentives (energy prices, midterm aftermath) for de-escalation if terms can be met.  
- Iranian economic pressure and regime interest in sanctions relief.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Core unresolved issues (nuclear program sequencing, sanctions, blockade, Hormuz control).  
- Iranian residual capability and demonstrated willingness to attack shipping for leverage.  
- Insurance market lag and commercial risk aversion (Red Sea precedent).  
- High US and Iranian force postures; risk of post-midterm escalation or miscalculation.  
- Proxy networks remain active and hard to restrain quickly.  
- Physical damage and rebuild timelines for energy assets.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-to-medium intensity conflict or fragile mediation for several months, with partial oil flows under military protection and high insurance. Possible limited de-escalation or temporary Hormuz arrangements by mid-2027, but full multi-domain normality (especially insurance and force posture) more likely only later if a political deal emerges. Probability mass is concentrated on delayed rather than rapid normalization.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
A post-midterm US decision to prioritize energy price relief leads to acceptance of an Iranian-conditioned deal within weeks. 30–60 days of verified calm produces rapid insurer rate cuts and transit recovery. Probability of this path is low (~15–20% by 6 months) given current statements and unresolved issues.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Post-midterm strikes trigger Iranian shipping/energy retaliation and proxy activation. Conflict settles into a multi-year Tanker War analogue with elevated insurance and incomplete flows. This path remains plausible (~25–35% at 18 months).

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that I am underestimating speed: Adaptive commercial workarounds and mutual economic pain could force a faster practical normalization even without a perfect political deal (flows already partially recovered). Strongest case that I am overestimating: Nuclear issue and Iranian residual maritime capability make true insurance/force-posture normality extremely sticky; Red Sea precedent suggests multi-year elevated risk is the default. Load-bearing assumptions: that a political settlement is possible within 18 months and that insurance markets respond within months of kinetic calm. Evidence of a major new escalation phase or total collapse of mediation would require large downward revisions.
