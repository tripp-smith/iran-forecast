**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal:

- **Direct military activity**: No significant US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory or Iranian missile/drone attacks on US/Israeli/Gulf targets (sporadic low-level incidents below escalation thresholds may occur).
- **Proxy activity**: Restraint or sharp de-escalation by Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi PMF, etc.; no sustained campaigns against shipping, Gulf states, or Israel.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe commercial transit without routine attacks, mining, or IRGC-enforced tolls/blockades; US naval blockade lifted or significantly eased.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic and tanker throughput returning to at least 70-80% of pre-war averages (~100-120+ vessels/day transiting Hormuz; full crude/LNG flows).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums normalized (near pre-war levels, e.g., <<1% of hull value for standard transits); full P&I coverage available without exclusions or massive surcharges.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, Kuwait, Iran) exporting near full capacity with minimal forced curtailments; no major stranded volumes.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI implied volatility back to normal ranges (<30-40%); prices reflecting fundamentals without large geopolitical premium; no sustained shortages or force majeure declarations.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Israeli drawdown from heightened alert; Iranian forces/IRGC not postured for imminent disruption; Gulf states resuming normal operations.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No assassinations of senior leaders, major infrastructure attacks, nuclear breakthroughs, or proxy surges that reset risk perceptions.<grok:render card_id="5d4fdb" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">11</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="d501a0" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">12</argument></grok:render>

A temporary ceasefire or lower oil prices alone does **not** suffice. Commercial/insurance normalization lags military pauses due to risk aversion, mine-clearing, and trust deficits.
