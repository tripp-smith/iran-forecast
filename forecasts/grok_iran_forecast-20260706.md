**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means:
- **Direct military activity**: No sustained kinetic exchanges (missile/drone strikes, naval clashes) between Iran, US, Israel, or Gulf states for at least 30 consecutive days; sporadic low-level incidents below a threshold that triggers insurance/market reactions.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi militias, etc. (e.g., attacks reduced >80% from peak and not targeting energy/shipping).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe commercial transit without routine attacks, mining, or forced boardings; daily transits approaching 70-100+ vessels (pre-war ~150); effective demining and escort options normalized.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic and tanker loadings at ≥80% of pre-war baselines for 2+ consecutive weeks; no major backlogs or force majeure declarations.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to near pre-war levels (e.g., <0.5% of hull value for 7-day policies vs. peaks of 1-5%+); standard private coverage widely available without government backstops or prohibitive surcharges; Joint War Committee removes or narrows high-risk designation.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Regional exports (Iran + Gulf states) at ≥85% of pre-war volumes; no sustained production curtailments or stranded cargoes; Asian/European buyers resuming normal contracting without war premiums.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalized (e.g., no repeated spikes >$10-15/bbl on Gulf news); forward curves stable without contango/backwardation driven by chokepoint fears.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Gulf drawdown of surge deployments; Iran IRGC not in heightened alert/blockade mode; no new major bases or blockades.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No assassinations, nuclear breakthroughs, major proxy escalations, or unresolved MOU clauses leading to renewed blockades.
