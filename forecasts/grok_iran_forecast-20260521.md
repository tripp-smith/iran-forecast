**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain): 

Functional normality requires **all** of the following to hold simultaneously and sustainably (not just a pause or selective access): 

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, mine-laying, or naval clashes by state actors in/near the Gulf for at least 30 days; US/Iran/Israel forces at de-escalated postures. 
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi/Hezbollah/PMF attacks on shipping, infrastructure, or Gulf states linked to the Iran conflict. 
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Open transit for all commercial flags (no Iranian “permission”/tolls required, no selective routing); mine clearance complete or verified low-risk; negligible attacks/seizures. 
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily vessel transits ≥80% of pre-war baseline (~100-140 vessels/day); no widespread avoidance or rerouting. 
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Standard commercial policies available at near-pre-war premiums (e.g., <0.5% of hull value); no broad exclusions or government backstops needed. 
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Full export flows from Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, etc.) via sea routes at ≥90% pre-war volumes; Iranian exports normalized without blockade. 
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index back to pre-war ranges; no sustained premiums >$10-15/bbl risk or major supply shortfalls. 
- **Force posture of major actors**: US Navy drawdown from blockade/escort ops; Iranian IRGC Navy not enforcing control zones; no heightened alerts. 
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakthroughs, leadership crises, or proxy flare-ups that could rapidly reverse gains. 

A temporary ceasefire, selective Iranian-approved transits, or lower spot prices alone do **not** count.
