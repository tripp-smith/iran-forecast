**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain; a temporary pause or partial flows do not suffice):

- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes (missiles, drones, naval clashes) between US/Israel/Gulf states and Iran/proxies in the Gulf region for ≥30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: No sustained or escalated attacks by Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, PMF, or IRGC-linked groups on Gulf shipping, ports, or energy infrastructure.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe commercial transit without credible threats, attacks, or mandatory “tolls/permissions” from Iran; US escorts no longer required as primary deterrent.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic (tankers, LNG, containers) returns to ≥80% of pre-war (early 2026) baseline volumes; no significant stranding/backlog of hundreds of vessels.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums fall to near pre-war levels (e.g., ~0.25% of hull value or low single-digit multiples); full private-market availability without government backstops or frequent cancellations.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Persian Gulf exports (including Iran, Saudi, UAE, etc.) at ≥85-90% of pre-war rates through Hormuz without major rerouting or production curtailments; no persistent dual blockades.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained >$10-15 daily swings tied to Gulf news); no ongoing supply shock equivalent to 10-20% global disruption.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Iranian naval/air forces draw down from heightened alert/blockade/escort modes; no active mine-laying or fast-attack boat harassment.
