**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of the following observable thresholds for at least 14 consecutive days (longer for insurance and force posture):  
- Direct military activity: No significant kinetic exchanges (missile, drone, or airstrikes) between US/Israeli forces and Iranian forces.  
- Proxy activity: Houthi, Iraqi Shia militia, and other proxy attacks on shipping, energy infrastructure, or bases reduced to or below pre-February 2026 baseline levels, with no new major campaigns.  
- Shipping security (Hormuz and nearby waters): UKMTO or equivalent threat level reduced from severe; zero mine, missile, drone, or limpet attacks on commercial vessels; safe bidirectional transit without US naval coordination or dark-AIS requirements.  
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible (or total equivalent) daily transits through Hormuz ≥70–80% of pre-crisis ~85/day average.  
- War-risk insurance: Additional premiums return to <1% of hull value (ideally near pre-war ~0.25%), with available capacity, longer cancellation windows, and no extreme voyage-specific surcharges.  
- Oil/LNG export continuity: Non-Iranian Gulf oil/condensate/product exports stable at ≥90% of pre-war levels via normal (non-dark, non-escorted, non-bypass-dominant) routes; LNG flows recovered without major stranding. Iranian exports may remain constrained by residual sanctions but are not under active naval blockade.  
- Energy market stress: Brent and equivalent volatility reduced; risk premium attributable to Hormuz not persistently elevating prices >$20–30/bbl above pre-war baseline; stranded volumes inside the Gulf near normal.  
- Force posture: US surge forces (extra carriers, elevated troop levels ~50k, nightly blockade/enforcement sorties) drawn down toward pre-war baselines; no elevated alert for imminent large-scale strikes.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new territorial seizures (e.g., further Houthi advances at Bab al-Mandab), major infrastructure attacks, nuclear advances, or leadership-driven escalations that restart the cycle.  

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price drop from demand destruction or inventory draws, or battlefield pause without commercial recovery does not qualify.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **Tanker War (1984–88 Iran-Iraq)**: Prolonged maritime attacks on commercial shipping in the Gulf. Relevant for insurance pricing, traffic suppression, and lag between military pause and commercial recovery. Breaks down: no superpower blockade or modern proxy network; ended with formal ceasefire. Suggested timeline: commercial traffic and insurance partial recovery in 3–6 months post-ceasefire; fuller normality 12+ months.  
- **2019–2020 Hormuz tanker incidents + Abqaiq**: Brief spikes in attacks and risk premiums resolved by deterrence and limited escalation. Relevant for short-cycle risk pricing. Breaks down: not a multi-month sustained war with blockade. Suggested: weeks to 1–2 months for insurance and traffic partial recovery after pause.  
- **Houthi Red Sea crisis (2023–2025)**: Sustained proxy disruption of a chokepoint, elevated insurance, traffic diversion lasting 12–24 months until political deals. Relevant for proxy persistence and insurance stickiness. Breaks down: secondary theater, not core Gulf energy terminal attacks. Suggested: 6–18 months for meaningful normalization post-political restraint.  
- **Post-1991 Gulf War / no-fly enforcement eras**: Extended US force posture and residual risk. Relevant for drawdown timelines. Breaks down: different political end-state. Suggested: force posture elevated for years; commercial recovery faster once direct threat subsided.  
- **Black Sea shipping corridors (Ukraine 2022–)**: Partial recovery via protected corridors amid ongoing conflict; risk premiums and insurance elevated for years. Relevant for “managed” rather than full normality. Breaks down: different geography and actors. Suggested: partial commercial function in months; full normality years or never while conflict persists.  

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment)**: 1 week ~2–5%; 1 month ~8–12%; 3 months ~20–25%; 6 months ~35–45%; 1 year ~50–60%; 18 months ~60–70%. Anchored in the longer commercial/insurance/force lags of Tanker War and Houthi cases, tempered by faster resolution possible in short-spike historical incidents. Current case starts from an active low-intensity war, not a post-ceasefire baseline, so priors are conservative.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Active Qatari/Pakistani mediation and Iranian willingness to discuss short reopening plans (contingent).  
2. Partial oil-volume recovery demonstrates commercial adaptability under US protection.  
3. US domestic political incentives (post-midterm) for deal that reduces energy costs.  
4. Iranian leadership transition and economic pressure from blockade.  
5. Gulf states’ preference for de-escalation to protect infrastructure.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Persistent direct and proxy kinetic activity (shipping attacks, limited strikes, Houthi Red Sea/Saudi campaign).  
2. US blockade and elevated force posture (carriers, nightly ops) required to sustain current (partial) flows.  
3. War-risk insurance pricing and capacity reflecting sustained severe threat.  
4. Maximalist Iranian conditions and unresolved nuclear/regional fronts (Lebanon, Yemen).  
5. Structural damage to ports/refineries/LNG and Houthi territorial gains creating second-order chokepoint risk.  
6. Mutual incentives to retain leverage (Iran on Hormuz; US on blockade).

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

**• 1 Week**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~2–5% (active conflict, not post-ceasefire).  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly down (ongoing strikes, mine claims, traffic at 10%, premiums extreme).  
- Core rationale: Kinetic activity and commercial disruption remain live; mediation has not produced a pause.  
- Main gating factor: Cessation of direct/proxy attacks and traffic recovery.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Immediate mutual pause announcement + verified traffic surge.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Major new strike wave or Houthi escalation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**• 1 Month**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~8–12%.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Down (no deal, force surge continuing, insurance sticky).  
- Core rationale: Even a diplomatic breakthrough would require weeks for demining, insurance repricing, and traffic confidence; current trajectory shows continued low-level fighting.  
- Main gating factor: Sustained safe transit and insurance movement.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Signed deal with verified 14-day pause and insurance quotes dropping.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumption of large-scale strikes or blockade tightening.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-High

- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization and proxy restraint.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Comprehensive deal covering Yemen/Lebanon + rapid demining and insurer re-entry.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: New escalation cycle or failed talks.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium

- Main gating factor: Absence of new escalation triggers and insurance market re-entry.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Clear post-conflict security architecture and verified multi-month calm.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Regime instability in Iran or expanded regional war.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Low  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-intensity conflict and mediation through late 2026–early 2027, with intermittent limited strikes and shipping harassment. Partial oil volumes persist via protected/dark/alternative routes. A limited or framework deal possible in 3–9 months produces a military pause but incomplete commercial recovery (traffic 40–60%, premiums 2–5%, residual proxy activity). Full functional normality (as defined) more probable only in the 12–18 month window if talks succeed and Houthis are restrained; otherwise a managed “forever conflict” with elevated baseline risk.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm US political pressure + Iranian economic pain produce a rapid deal covering Hormuz reopening, sanctions relief, and Yemen restraint within 4–8 weeks. Demining and US force drawdown follow; insurers reprice sharply lower by month 3; traffic recovers to >70% by month 4–6. Probability of this path: ~15–20%.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks fail; US launches larger post-midterm strikes; Iran and proxies escalate against Gulf infrastructure and Bab al-Mandab. Traffic remains suppressed, premiums stay extreme, force posture elevated through 2027. Structural damage and Houthi gains create multi-year second chokepoint risk. Probability of this path: ~25–30%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case for faster normalization: oil-volume recovery already shows markets can function under protection; mediation is active; both sides face domestic costs. I may be overweighting insurance stickiness and underweighting adaptability. Strongest case for prolonged instability: Iran’s geographic advantage and maximalist demands, Houthi territorial gains, and US sunk costs in blockade create incentives for frozen conflict. I may be underestimating the probability that partial commercial function (dark/escorted flows) is treated as “normality” by markets even if the operational definition is not met, or that a new escalation cycle restarts after any pause. Load-bearing assumptions: mediation remains viable; no major new nuclear or leadership trigger; US willingness to sustain blockade costs is finite.
