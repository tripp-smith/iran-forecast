**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (initiated ~28 February 2026) by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months from 20 September 2026.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of these observable, multi-domain criteria for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No major kinetic exchanges between Iran/US/Israel or major Iranian strikes on Gulf host-state military/energy infrastructure for ≥30 consecutive days; residual low-level incidents below the threshold that triggers systematic retaliation.
- Proxy activity: Meaningful restraint by Houthis and other Iran-aligned groups on Red Sea/Gulf shipping and energy infrastructure (attack frequency and severity at or below late-2023/early-2024 baselines, not wartime peaks).
- Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Consistent safe commercial transit without routine Iranian interdiction, mining, drone/missile strikes on merchant vessels, or forced routing under Iranian “authority”; AIS-visible transits restored to ≥70–80% of pre-war averages (~60–100+ vessels/day depending on source baseline of ~85–130).
- Commercial transit levels: Sustained recovery of commodity (especially crude/LNG) vessel throughput through Hormuz to levels supporting pre-war export volumes from Gulf producers.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war-risk premiums for Hormuz transits returned to ≤2–3× pre-crisis baseline (pre-war ~0.1–0.25% of hull value; current ~1.5% or ~15×), with broad availability of cover and no widespread cancellations or “severe” JWC ratings blocking commercial activity.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf (ex-Iran) crude and LNG exports recovered to ≥85–90% of pre-war volumes via Hormuz or reliable bypasses, without chronic ship-to-ship reliance or major pipeline outages driven by conflict.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent and related benchmarks no longer pricing a persistent Hormuz risk premium of the current magnitude; volatility normalized relative to non-conflict baselines; no sustained $100+ pricing driven primarily by Gulf disruption.
- Force posture of major actors: Material drawdown or stabilization of US/Coalition escort and strike posture in the Gulf, and Iranian coastal/anti-shipping deployments, consistent with de-escalation rather than active contest for control of the waterway.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new large-scale attacks on energy infrastructure, major naval clashes, nuclear-related escalations, or proxy surges that reset the risk cycle.

A temporary ceasefire, battlefield pause, partial traffic recovery under military escort, or oil-price dip alone does **not** qualify. Commercial normalization (insurance + voluntary transit volumes + export continuity) is the binding constraint and typically lags kinetic pauses.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (with relevance, breakdown points, and implied timelines for partial/functional stabilization):

1. **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Prolonged attacks on shipping in the Gulf; escort operations (Operation Earnest Will); eventual end with ceasefire. Relevant for asymmetric anti-shipping and insurance response. Breaks down because it was bilateral Iraq-Iran without direct US-Iran war or nuclear overlay, and global energy markets were different. Suggests multi-year disruption with gradual commercial recovery measured in years after major fighting tapered, not weeks/months.
2. **2019–2021 Gulf tanker incidents / Soleimani aftermath / residual Iranian harassment**: Episodic mining, seizures, drone strikes; temporary spikes in war risk and cautious transit. Relevant for Iranian denial capabilities and insurance sensitivity. Breaks down in scale (far smaller than 2026 full war). Stabilization often occurred in weeks to a few months after peak incidents once deterrence/signaling settled, but residual elevated risk persisted.
3. **Houthi Red Sea campaign 2023–2025/6**: Sustained proxy disruption of a chokepoint; insurance spikes; partial rerouting; incomplete normalization even after reduced intensity. Highly relevant for proxy persistence and insurance lag. Breaks down because Bab el-Mandeb is not Hormuz (less volume, different geography) and lacks direct great-power blockade. Functional commercial recovery has been slow and incomplete even with pauses—months to >1 year for meaningful volume/insurance normalization.
4. **2022–2024 Black Sea grain corridor / Ukraine maritime disruptions**: War-driven chokepoint risks, insurance complications, partial corridors under military/diplomatic arrangements. Relevant for commercial lag behind kinetic pauses. Stabilization of volumes required sustained diplomatic + security arrangements and often remained partial for 6–18+ months.
5. **Historical Hormuz closure scares (e.g., 1980s peaks, 2011–12 threats)**: Short-lived threats produced rapid market reactions but limited sustained closure. Less relevant because 2026 involves actual sustained kinetic contest + blockade. Prior implies faster recovery once threat credibility dropped, but only if political settlement exists.
6. **Post-major Middle East wars with energy infrastructure damage (e.g., 1991 Gulf War aftermath, 2003 Iraq)**: Kinetic end did not immediately restore commercial confidence or full export normalcy. Relevant for second-order effects. Timelines of 6–24 months common for full functional recovery.

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment, approximate cumulative probability of meeting the full operational definition):**  
- 1 week: 1–3%  
- 1 month: 5–10%  
- 3 months: 15–25%  
- 6 months: 30–40%  
- 1 year: 45–60%  
- 18 months: 55–70%  

These reflect that kinetic pauses occur faster than insurance + voluntary commercial recovery; full multi-domain normality is rare within months of active chokepoint contest involving major powers.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Mutual economic/political exhaustion + US midterm calendar creating incentive for de-escalation package.  
2. Sustained kinetic pause reducing immediate risk perception.  
3. Diplomatic channel (Qatar/Oman/Pakistan) remaining open.  
4. Iranian economic collapse increasing regime willingness to trade control claims for sanctions/blockade relief.  
5. Bypass capacity (pipelines, ship-to-ship) and strategic stock releases limiting pure scarcity pressure.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Iranian core war aim of recognized leverage/control over Hormuz conflicting with US freedom-of-navigation posture.  
2. Insurance and commercial risk aversion: underwriters and owners require sustained evidence, not announcements.  
3. Proxy autonomy (Houthis) and residual Iranian anti-shipping capabilities.  
4. Domestic political constraints (US midterms rhetoric; Iranian hardliners; Gulf state hedging).  
5. Second-order damage (mines residual, infrastructure hits, force posture inertia) and risk of new triggers.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate probabilities of material progress on each component by horizon; these are not independent)

- Major reduction in direct strikes: 1w 15–25%; 1m 40–55%; 3m 60–75%; 6m 70–85%; 1y/18m 80%+.  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (Hormuz volumes): 1w <5%; 1m 10–20%; 3m 25–40%; 6m 40–55%; 1y 55–70%; 18m 65–80%.  
- Meaningful insurance normalization: lags transit; 1w <5%; 1m 5–15%; 3m 20–35%; 6m 35–50%; 1y 50–65%; 18m 60–75%.  
- Oil/LNG export recovery to near-normal: tracks transit + bypasses; similar to or slightly ahead of full transit recovery.  
- Proxy restraint: slower; 1w/1m low; 3m 30–45%; 6m 45–60%; longer horizons 55–75%.  
- Force drawdown / no new major escalation trigger: contingent on political deal; low in near term, rising with settlement probability.

Gating factors are commercial transit + insurance; kinetic pause alone is insufficient.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 2%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~1–3%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slight upward from pause and diplomatic messaging; downward from traffic/insurance data and recent attack patterns.  
- Core rationale: Impossible to meet multi-domain commercial criteria in 7 days. Even a formal ceasefire announcement would not move insurance or voluntary volumes.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance and shipowner risk perception.  
- What would raise: Immediate, verified, broad-based safe-transit corridor with insurer buy-in and multi-day high transit counts.  
- What would lower: Any new shipping strike or host-state attack.  
- Confidence: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5–10%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest upward from pause + Qatar channel; capped by commercial lag and MOU precedent.  
- Core rationale: Kinetic de-escalation possible around midterms/diplomacy, but insurance normalization and sustained high voluntary transit require longer evidence of safety.  
- Main gating factor: Transition from escorted/partial to commercially normal transit + premium reduction.  
- What would raise: Durable political understanding + 2–3 weeks of high AIS transit with falling premiums.  
- What would lower: Breakdown of pause, Houthi escalation, or explicit Iranian reassertion of control.  
- Confidence: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 22%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~15–25%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Near base rate; economic pressure and calendar help, structural Hormuz contest hurts.  
- Core rationale: Window for a political package exists, but history of this conflict and reference classes show commercial recovery lags. Full definition remains demanding.  
- Main gating factor: Whether any settlement includes enforceable navigation arrangements that insurers and owners trust.  
- What would raise: Verified multi-week safe high-volume transit + clear premium compression + proxy quieting.  
- What would lower: Renewed tanker campaign or major energy infrastructure hits.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 38%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~30–40%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly below mid-range of prior due to depth of disruption and Iranian control claims.  
- Core rationale: Higher chance of some political accommodation post-midterms and under economic strain, but full multi-domain normality (especially insurance + voluntary volumes without residual risk premium) still faces structural obstacles.  
- Main gating factor: Depth and durability of any post-election or mediated settlement on the strait.  
- What would raise: Sustained 60–90 day safe transit at high volumes with premiums near 2–3× baseline and export recovery.  
- What would lower: Prolonged low-level contest or new proxy/energy attacks resetting confidence.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 55%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~45–60%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Near center; long enough for exhaustion dynamics and possible settlement, but residual risk and second-order effects persist.  
- Core rationale: Reference classes and economic incentives favor eventual de-escalation; commercial lag means many “ends” of fighting do not equal functional normality. Over 50% reflects cumulative pressure.  
- Main gating factor: Whether a political settlement produces lasting commercial confidence rather than managed competition.  
- What would raise: Clear post-settlement track record of safe high-volume traffic and normalized insurance for several months.  
- What would lower: Frozen conflict with permanent elevated risk pricing or repeated flare-ups.  
- Confidence: Medium  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 68%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~55–70%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest upward within range; longer horizon allows more settlement and recovery pathways.  
- Core rationale: Most comparable disruptions eventually see substantial commercial recovery, though full pre-crisis “normality” (zero residual risk premium, full force posture reset) remains incomplete in many cases. Definition is still demanding, so not higher.  
- Main gating factor: Persistence of residual Iranian leverage claims or proxy capabilities versus full political resolution.  
- What would raise: Multi-quarter track record of normal volumes, premiums, and exports without major incidents.  
- What would lower: Entrenchment of a contested strait regime or major new escalation cycle.  
- Confidence: Medium (long-horizon uncertainty higher)

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Intermittent pauses and limited diplomatic progress produce partial kinetic de-escalation over 1–3 months, especially around political calendars. Traffic recovers modestly under continued military facilitation or limited corridors, but insurance premiums and voluntary commercial volumes lag. A more durable political understanding becomes possible by 6–12 months under economic pressure, enabling gradual multi-domain improvement. Full functional normality arrives later than kinetic quieting, if at all within 18 months, with residual elevated risk.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Rapid US-Iran political package (sanctions/blockade relief traded for navigation guarantees and restraint) shortly after midterms or via mediators, combined with verified demining/escort drawdown, produces 4–8 weeks of incident-free high-volume transit. Insurers reprice aggressively; owners return; exports recover. Probability mass shifts upward if this sequence begins within weeks.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran prioritizes long-term leverage over Hormuz; proxies remain active; intermittent attacks or assertions continue; insurance stays elevated; commercial volumes stay suppressed. US maintains pressure posture. Functional normality delayed well beyond 18 months or achieved only partially via bypasses and managed risk. Triggered by failed diplomacy or domestic incentives on either side for continued contest.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case I am overestimating normalization: Iranian commitment to recognized control is load-bearing and incompatible with rapid commercial confidence; insurance markets are sticky; proxies have autonomy; reference classes show multi-year tails. Strongest case I am underestimating: Extreme Iranian economic pain + US political calendar could force a faster, more comprehensive package than historical analogs, and markets can reprice quickly once sustained evidence appears. Load-bearing assumptions include continued Iranian regime coherence under pressure and absence of a sudden major escalation (nuclear or mass infrastructure). Evidence that would prove the view wrong: sustained multi-week high AIS transit counts with simultaneous sharp premium compression and export recovery without a political settlement (unlikely but possible under pure military facilitation).
