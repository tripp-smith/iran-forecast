**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by these horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained (multi-week) satisfaction of these observable conditions. A temporary ceasefire, partial oil-flow recovery, or lower prices alone does not qualify:

- **Direct military activity**: No sustained US/Israeli kinetic strikes on Iranian territory or Iranian strikes on US/Gulf military targets; any incidents limited to sporadic, low-intensity probes without escalation spiral.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthis and other Iran-aligned proxies cease major attacks on Saudi/Gulf infrastructure, airports, oil facilities, or shipping (isolated residual incidents acceptable if not disrupting operations).
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and approaches)**: JMIC/UKMTO threat level reduced from “SEVERE”; no routine projectile/mine attacks or harassment requiring military escorts or “dark” (AIS-off) routing for safety; free transit without Iranian permit/toll regime.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Total (AIS-visible + facilitated) daily commercial transits recover to ~80–100+ (approaching pre-war baseline of ~120–140). AIS-visible alone near pre-war norms preferred.
- **War-risk insurance**: Additional premiums fall to ≤1% of hull value (ideally 0.1–0.5%), with broad underwriter appetite restored for mainstream commercial traffic.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Combined crude/product flows through Hormuz at ≥90% of pre-war ~17 mb/d baseline for oil; LNG loadings from Qatar and others near pre-war volumes without structural capacity loss dominating.
- **Energy market stress**: Sustained risk premium largely removed; Brent not persistently >$90–100 solely due to Hormuz security (fundamentals and other supply can still influence prices).
- **Force posture**: US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted or suspended; carrier/force surge reduced toward pre-war presence; no active large-scale escort requirement.
- **No major new escalation triggers**: No collapse of talks into full war resumption, no large-scale proxy activation against Gulf states, no new minefields or mass attacks.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (partial/functional stabilization timelines):

- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Multi-year attacks (~450+), mines, US escorts (Operation Earnest Will 1987–88) kept traffic moving but never fully normalized until the broader war ended (August 1988). Suggests 12–24+ months of disruption even with naval protection; full recovery post-ceasefire. Relevant for mine/harassment persistence and escort model; breaks down because current conflict is US-Iran direct with nuclear/blockade dimensions and higher global oil dependence sensitivity.
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Attacks from Nov 2023 drove insurance spikes (to ~1–2%+), diversions, and sustained traffic reductions. Partial recovery with naval efforts, but disruption and elevated premiums persisted for 1–2+ years without durable political settlement. Relevant for proxy maritime coercion and insurance market stickiness; breaks down as Hormuz volume is larger and current attacks more intense with state actor.
- **2019 Gulf tanker incidents / limited strikes**: Short spikes in attacks recovered to near-normal within weeks–months once escalation contained and no sustained campaign. Suggests rapid (1–3 month) commercial recovery after de-escalation. Less relevant given scale of current campaign and ongoing war.
- **1991 Gulf War / post-ceasefire chokepoints**: Short high-intensity phase; shipping and markets largely normalized within months after ceasefire. Relevant for post-decisive-settlement recovery speed; breaks down as 2026 lacks decisive outcome.
- **Black Sea / Ukraine grain corridors**: Prolonged disruption with partial, fragile corridors under military risk; insurance and volumes recovered unevenly over 6–18 months with external guarantees. Relevant for “managed” high-risk transit; breaks down on scale and adversary type.

**Base-rate prior (pre-current evidence, outside-in):** Major active-war chokepoint disruption with state + proxy involvement typically sees partial military-protected flows within 1–3 months of any pause, insurance lag of 3–6+ months, and full commercial normality only after durable political settlement (often 6–18 months post-pause, longer if core issues unresolved). Prior roughly: 1w <5%; 1m ~8–12%; 3m ~20–25%; 6m ~30–40%; 1y ~45–55%; 18m ~55–65%. Current case is adjusted downward for persistence of Iranian maritime leverage, nuclear sequencing disputes, proxy activity, and insurance/market memory.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. US strike pause until midterms and active (if stalled) mediation channels (Qatar/Pakistan).  
2. Mutual economic pain: high oil prices, Iranian export blockage, Gulf state exposure.  
3. US ability to sustain facilitated “dark” transits, providing a floor under total flows.  
4. Historical precedent that commercial traffic can resume under escorts once attack tempo drops.  
5. Potential post-midterm political incentives for deal (US domestic fuel costs; Iranian revenue needs).

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Iranian retention of effective maritime denial capacity and demonstrated willingness to raise attack tempo to protect leverage (Hormuz as core bargaining chip).  
2. Deep sequencing mistrust (US wants nuclear first; Iran wants blockade/Hormuz first) and prior MOU collapse.  
3. Persistent proxy (Houthi) activity and risk of wider Gulf targeting.  
4. Insurance market stickiness and shipowner risk aversion—even after pauses, premiums and diversions lag.  
5. Absence of decisive military outcome or verified de-mining/force drawdown; force postures remain elevated.  
6. Structural issues (Iranian nuclear program, frozen assets, sanctions) that outlast any short military pause.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate probabilities that each major component is largely met by the horizon; these are inputs, not mechanical averages)

- Major reduction in direct strikes: High short-term due to pause (1w–1m ~70–80%), but fragile; falls then recovers only with deal (3m ~40%, 6m ~50%, 1y+ ~60%).  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (no routine attacks/escorts required): Very low near-term (1w <5%, 1m ~10%); rises slowly (3m ~20%, 6m ~35%, 1y ~50%, 18m ~60%). Gating.  
- Meaningful insurance normalization: Lags transit (1m ~5%, 3m ~15%, 6m ~30%, 1y ~45%, 18m ~55%).  
- Oil/LNG export recovery to near-normal: Partial flows already exist; full (incl. Iranian) requires blockade lift + security (1m ~15%, 3m ~25%, 6m ~40%, 1y ~55%).  
- Proxy restraint: Ongoing Houthi activity keeps low (1–3m ~20–30%; longer horizons depend on broader settlement ~40–55%).  
- Force drawdown (blockade/escorts reduced): Tied to deal (low until 3–6m+).  
- No new major escalation trigger: Medium near-term due to pause (~60% 1m), lower as midterms pass and leverage games continue (~40–50% longer).

Gating factors for full normality are shipping security + insurance + absence of attacks, which lag any military pause. Partial oil flows under protection are insufficient.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 2%  
- Base-rate anchor: Near-zero for functional normality during active high-tempo attacks.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Down—record recent attacks, SEVERE threat, low traffic.  
- Core rationale: Immediate conditions (attacks, threat level, insurance, transit) remain far from definition. Pause in US strikes is not commercial normality.  
- Main gating factor: Ongoing Iranian shipping attacks and SEVERE assessment.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Sudden verified halt in attacks + rapid traffic surge (unlikely).  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Further sinkings or new minefields.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 6%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~8–12% for early partial stabilization in comparable cases.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Down—attack tempo elevated, talks stalled, midterms approaching.  
- Core rationale: Possible modest attack reduction if diplomacy advances or both sides conserve capacity, but insurance, full transit, proxy restraint, and blockade lift remain distant.  
- Main gating factor: Shipping security and insurance.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Formal de-escalation agreement with verified attack halt and traffic recovery >50% baseline.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Post-midterm US strike resumption or major Houthi/Saudi escalation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 15%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~20–25%.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Down—fragility of prior MOU, Iranian maritime adaptation, nuclear sequencing.  
- Core rationale: Window after midterms could produce limited deal enabling reduced attacks and higher facilitated flows, but full definition (insurance, unrestricted transit, proxies) unlikely without deeper settlement.  
- Main gating factor: Durable reduction in attacks sufficient for underwriters and shipowners.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Signed agreement with verifiable monitoring, blockade suspension, and multi-week attack-free period.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Talks collapse + renewed large-scale strikes or mine campaign.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 28%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~30–40%.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Down modestly—core disputes unresolved, insurance memory, proxy risks.  
- Core rationale: Greater chance of negotiated pause or managed status quo with higher flows under residual risk, but functional normality (unrestricted commercial transit + normal insurance) still requires political breakthrough that has repeatedly failed.  
- Main gating factor: Political settlement sufficient to end Iranian denial operations.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Broader deal including nuclear steps + force reductions + sustained traffic near baseline.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Entrenched low-intensity campaign or new front (e.g., wider Gulf strikes).  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 42%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~45–55%.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly down—history of MOU collapse, deep mistrust, unresolved nuclear/blockade issues.  
- Core rationale: Cumulative economic and political pressure raises odds of some durable arrangement; markets and insurers can normalize faster once attacks stop for months. Still substantial risk of prolonged managed disruption.  
- Main gating factor: Existence of a holding agreement that removes Iranian incentive/ability for routine attacks.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Verified multi-month normality in all domains + force drawdown.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumption of high-tempo war or permanent Iranian control claims enforced.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-Low  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 55%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~55–65%.  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest down for structural issues (nuclear, regional order).  
- Core rationale: Longer horizon allows more time for attrition, mediation, or limited settlement; historical cases show eventual commercial recovery even after prolonged disruption once kinetic phase ends. Overconfidence avoided because definition is demanding and core disputes can persist.  
- Main gating factor: Whether the conflict ends in a durable political arrangement versus frozen low-intensity denial.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Clear end-of-war agreement with monitoring and economic reopening.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Entrenchment of a new “normal” of elevated risk and reduced baseline flows.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Low-Medium  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Low-intensity attrition continues through and beyond US midterms. Facilitated/dark transits maintain a partial oil-flow floor (~50–70% of baseline on average) under elevated risk and cost. Attacks fluctuate but do not cease. Talks produce intermittent proposals without breakthrough on sequencing. Insurance remains expensive; full unrestricted commercial normality does not occur in the next 6–12 months. Proxies remain active at lower intensity. A limited or phased deal becomes more plausible in the 6–18 month window under mutual economic pressure, but full definition of normality is more likely post-18 months or not at all if frozen conflict solidifies.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm US political incentives + Iranian revenue pressure produce a phased deal (blockade relief sequenced with limited nuclear steps and Hormuz security guarantees). Attacks drop sharply within weeks; traffic recovers toward 70–80%+ within 1–2 months; insurers begin cutting premiums after 1–3 months of quiet. Proxies restrained via Iranian instruction. Probability of full normality by 6 months rises toward 40–50% in this branch.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks fail; post-midterm strikes resume or Iranian denial operations intensify (more mines, higher tempo). Houthis expand. Traffic remains chronically suppressed; insurance stays prohibitive for non-escorted mainstream traffic. Oil risk premium endures. Functional normality delayed beyond 18 months or never fully achieved under a new higher-risk baseline. Strong historical parallel to extended Tanker War or Red Sea campaign.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case I may be *overestimating* normalization speed: Iranian leadership has repeatedly prioritized strategic leverage (Hormuz control claims, nuclear) over short-term revenue; prior MOU collapsed quickly; insurance markets and shipowners have long memories; proxies can act with partial autonomy. A frozen conflict with partial escorted flows may become the de facto status quo for years.  

Strongest case I may be *underestimating* speed: Mutual pain is real (US fuel prices, Iranian economy under blockade, Gulf states); US demonstrated capacity to sustain facilitated flows; a narrow post-midterm deal focused only on Hormuz security (deferring nuclear) could unlock commercial recovery faster than full political settlement. Historical escorts restored functional (if not risk-free) transit in the Tanker War.  

Load-bearing assumptions: Iranian maritime capacity remains effective; neither side will accept a decisive military outcome soon; insurance lags kinetic improvements by months.
