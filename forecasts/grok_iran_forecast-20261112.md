**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon (1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained (minimum 30 consecutive days) satisfaction of all of the following observable conditions:

- Direct military activity: No kinetic strikes by principal parties (US, Israel, Iran) on each other’s territory or forces for 30+ days; no active blockade or interdiction of commercial shipping by either side.
- Proxy activity: No major energy/infrastructure or maritime attacks by Iranian-aligned proxies (Houthis, PMF, Hezbollah) that are linked to the Iran conflict and disrupt Gulf flows or Gulf-state assets for 30+ days.
- Shipping security (Hormuz and approaches): No confirmed mine, missile, drone, or other attacks on commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, Gulf of Oman, or northern Persian Gulf for 30+ days; traffic separation schemes used without Iranian “coordination” or permit requirements.
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible commercial transits (vessels ≥80 m) averaging ≥70% of pre-crisis baseline (~85/day, so ≥60/day) or equivalent effective throughput for 30+ days.
- War-risk insurance: Additional premiums for Hormuz/Gulf transits ≤3× pre-war levels (~0.25% of hull value) or within historical peacetime ranges for the listed area, with broad availability and no systemic withdrawal of cover.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude exports through Hormuz or functional bypasses near pre-war rates (~20 Mb/d effective) without force majeure; Qatar LNG exports restored to ≥80% of pre-war capacity with force-majeure notices lifted.
- Energy market stress: Brent volatility and risk premium not elevated by ongoing Hormuz disruption (secondary indicator only; prices alone do not equal normality).
- Force posture: US naval and air posture reduced toward pre-crisis levels or stable peacetime presence; Iranian naval/mine capabilities not actively deployed for interdiction.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new nuclear, territorial, or mass-casualty incidents that restart high-intensity operations.

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price decline, or battlefield pause does not satisfy the definition. Partial escorted traffic or elevated insurance does not count.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of 12 November 2026, ~Day 258)**  
War began with US-Israeli strikes on 28 February 2026. Multiple ceasefire/MoU attempts (April, June) collapsed. Current state is low-intensity kinetic activity with continued maritime disruption. AIS commercial traffic through Hormuz is ~6–10/day (~8% of pre-crisis ~85/day). Recent attacks include projectile strikes and mine claims on tankers (ADNOC vessel, Fujairah Energy, others 10–11 Nov). IRGC claims control and requires coordination; US maintains blockade (Task Force Iron Gate) and escorts limited traffic. War-risk premiums remain 6–10% of hull value (24–40× pre-war). Qatar LNG under prolonged force majeure (capacity damage ~17%, exports far below normal; at least one long-term contract terminated). Crude flows partially recovered with escorts but remain well below pre-war. Diplomacy stalled: Iran demands seven conditions (sanctions/blockade end, asset release, reduced US presence, regional ceasefire, compensation, permanent peace) for Hormuz reopening; US (Rubio, Trump) calls demands unacceptable while stating economic pressure is effective and major combat is not currently needed. US posture: two carriers in theater, third en route; ~50k personnel. Houthis continue attacks on Saudi targets. Brent ~$93. Confirmed facts outweigh belligerent claims; IRGC mine/tanker claims are frequently disputed or unverified by independent maritime authorities.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, 1984–88)**: Sustained anti-shipping (hundreds of attacks) over ~4 years despite US escorts. Traffic continued at reduced risk-adjusted levels. Relevance: closest Hormuz-specific interdiction case. Breakdown: ended with broader ceasefire, not unilateral control; lower modern insurance sensitivity and proxy networks. Suggests multi-year disruption if conflict unresolved (mean ~2–3 years in long-tail cases).
- **2019 Gulf shipping incidents**: Limited seizures/attacks resolved in ~4–5 months after de-escalation. Relevance: short Hormuz disruptions. Breakdown: far lower intensity and no blockade. Suggests months once political will aligns.
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–2025)**: Months-to-years of disruption; partial recovery via rerouting and escorts, insurance elevated. Relevance: proxy maritime coercion. Breakdown: different chokepoint, no full blockade. Suggests 6–18 months for functional (not full) recovery post-pause.
- **1991 Gulf War**: Rapid post-hostilities recovery of shipping/exports within weeks-months. Relevance: decisive military outcome. Breakdown: no prolonged Iranian residual capability or nuclear file. Suggests fast recovery only after clear resolution.
- **2022 Black Sea grain corridor**: Recovery lagged ceasefire by weeks due to mines/insurance. Relevance: post-agreement commercial lag. Breakdown: smaller scale. Suggests 1–3 months lag after any durable deal.

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment)**: 1 week ~1%; 1 month ~5%; 3 months ~15%; 6 months ~30%; 1 year ~45%; 18 months ~55%. Anchored on multi-month commercial lags after pauses and multi-year tails when underlying political conflict persists.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. US preference for economic pressure over sustained high-intensity strikes (stated by Rubio/Trump).  
2. Partial crude flows already moving under escort, showing technical feasibility.  
3. Qatari/Pakistani mediation channels remain open.  
4. Domestic US political constraints post-midterms reduce appetite for open-ended war.  
5. Insurance markets have not fully withdrawn cover.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Iranian control/leverage over Hormuz as core bargaining chip; explicit refusal to restore pre-war status without major concessions.  
2. Persistent attacks and mine threat keeping insurance elevated and traffic suppressed.  
3. Physical damage to Qatar LNG infrastructure requiring multi-month/year repairs.  
4. Unresolved nuclear, sanctions, and regional proxy files creating escalation triggers.  
5. Proxy activity (Houthis) continuing independently.  
6. History of rapid collapse of prior pauses in this war.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-intensity disruption with partial escorted crude flows, suppressed LNG, elevated insurance, and stalled diplomacy. Gradual traffic creep possible under US naval umbrella, but Iranian residual capability and conditions prevent full commercial normalization within 12 months. Probability mass concentrates on partial recovery by 18 months rather than full functional normality.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
US accepts limited concessions (sanctions relief, blockade lift) in exchange for verified demining and no-attack commitment; Iranian leadership prioritizes economic recovery over leverage. Insurance drops within weeks of verified quiet; traffic recovers to 70%+ in 2–3 months. Probability of this path: ~15–20% by 6 months.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran retains effective veto via mines, small boats, and proxies; US maintains blockade; nuclear file unresolved; attacks continue at low level. Traffic stays <30% baseline; insurance remains elevated; LNG repairs delayed. Probability of still abnormal at 18 months: ~50%+.
