**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
“Functional normality” means observable, sustained multi-domain conditions where:  
- **Direct military activity**: No significant US/Iranian strikes, naval engagements, or mine-laying in/near the Gulf for ≥30 days.  
- **Proxy activity**: No sustained Houthi/Hezbollah/IRGC-QF attacks disrupting Gulf or adjacent shipping/routes.  
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Safe commercial transit without routine attacks, coercion, or credible threats; US/Gulf naval presence at routine pre-war levels.  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic (tankers, LNG carriers) at ≥80% of pre-war baselines for ≥30 consecutive days, per tracking data.  
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Private insurers offering standard coverage at premiums near historical norms (not 1,000x+ surges or government backstops required).  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian, Saudi, UAE, etc., exports flowing at near-capacity with minimal rerouting/delays; no major supply shocks.  
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility and risk premia normalized (e.g., no sustained $10+/bbl war premium).  
- **Force posture of major actors**: US CENTCOM drawdown toward pre-crisis levels; Iran not posturing for closure/blockade.  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No major incidents (e.g., tanker strikes, proxy barrages, nuclear-related moves) for the period.  

A temporary ceasefire or pause does not qualify; commercial/insurance behavior must normalize.
