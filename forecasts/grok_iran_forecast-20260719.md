**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means **all** of the following are observably met and sustained for at least 30 consecutive days:
- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, mine-laying, or naval/air clashes involving Iran, US, Israel, or Gulf states in/near the Gulf.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi/Hezbollah/Shia militia attacks on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US/ally assets traceable to Iranian direction.
- **Shipping security**: No attacks on commercial vessels in Hormuz or adjacent waters; US/coalition naval presence at de-escalatory (non-blockade) levels.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily transits through Hormuz return to ≥80% of pre-war baseline (~50-60+ commercial vessels/day); no widespread rerouting or avoidance.
- **War-risk insurance**: Private market war-risk premiums return to near pre-war levels (<0.5% of hull value); no broad cancellations or government backstops required as primary coverage.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Persian Gulf crude/LNG exports at ≥85% of pre-war volumes; no force majeure declarations; Iranian and GCC flows normalized without major disruptions.
- **Energy market stress**: Brent/WTI volatility and risk premia normalized (e.g., no sustained >$10-15/bbl war premium); Asian/European buyers resuming standard contracts without panic stockpiling.
- **Force posture**: Major actors (US, Iran, GCC) draw down to routine patrols/exercises; no heightened alerts or massed forces threatening Hormuz.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No credible threats to close Hormuz, nuclear breakthroughs, leadership decapitations, or major proxy escalations.

A temporary ceasefire or pause does **not** count. Lower oil prices alone do not count.
