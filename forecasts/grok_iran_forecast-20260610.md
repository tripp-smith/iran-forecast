**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain): 

Functional normality means all of the following sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal:
- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes (missiles, drones, naval clashes) by state actors in/near the Persian Gulf or Strait of Hormuz, and no active US naval blockade of Iranian ports.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant Houthi or other Iran-aligned attacks on shipping or Gulf infrastructure; restraint by non-state actors.
- **Shipping security**: Safe commercial transit through Hormuz at ≥70-80% of pre-war daily transits (~50-60+ ships/day vs. normal ~138); minimal successful attacks or credible threats forcing rerouting.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Tanker and commercial vessel traffic restored to levels supporting meaningful throughput; no widespread loitering or avoidance.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to low single-digit multiples of pre-war levels (e.g., <0.5% of hull value for 7 days, not 1-4%+); broad private market availability without government backstops dominating.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Combined Gulf exports (via Hormuz and alternatives) at ≥80-85% of pre-war baselines (~17-18+ mb/d oil equivalent); no major curtailments or infrastructure damage preventing flows.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained geopolitical risk premium driving prices >$20-30 above fundamentals); no ongoing rationing or major supply shocks.
- **Force posture**: Drawdown of heightened US/coalition naval presence and Iranian IRGC deployments; no dual-blockade dynamics.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No assassinations, major facility strikes, or brinkmanship (e.g., toll demands, mining threats) likely to reignite disruption.

A temporary ceasefire or battlefield pause does **not** equal normality. Lower prices alone or selective Iranian shipments do not suffice.
