**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means observable, sustained commercial and security conditions approximating pre-war (pre-Feb 28, 2026) baselines, without equating temporary pauses, ceasefires, or lower prices to recovery. It requires:

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes by US/Israel/Iran or major proxies on Gulf targets, shipping, or infrastructure for ≥30 days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint (e.g., <10% of peak attack rates) by Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, Iraqi groups, etc.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Open transit without routine attacks, seizures, or mandatory tolls/fees; mine clearance largely complete; US/Iranian naval forces in de-escalated posture.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily transits ≥70-80% of pre-war norms (~50-60 ships/day); backlog cleared; no widespread rerouting via Cape of Good Hope.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums back to low single-digit multiples of pre-war (e.g., <0.5% hull value per voyage, not 1-3%+ or unavailable); standard private coverage widely available without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf production/exports at ≥80% pre-war levels; no major stranded cargoes or refinery/port shutdowns.
- **Energy market stress/volatility**: Brent volatility normalized (e.g., <30% annualized); no sustained spikes from Gulf risk premium; inventories rebuilt.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval presence drawdown from heightened levels; Iranian IRGC Navy not enforcing control/tolls; Gulf states resuming normal operations.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No credible threats to close Hormuz, nuclear breakthroughs, leadership changes, or proxy surges.

Temporary ceasefires or selective transits (e.g., Chinese vessels) do not qualify.
