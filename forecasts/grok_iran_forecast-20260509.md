**Step 1: Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality means observable, sustained multi-domain recovery to pre-war operational baselines (or close equivalents allowing reliable commercial activity), **not** a mere pause in major fighting or temporary diplomatic statements. It requires all of the following simultaneously for a sustained period (at least 2-4 weeks without reversal):

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, naval engagements, or air/missile exchanges by US/Israel/Iran or their forces in/near the Gulf.
- **Proxy activity**: Restraint or sharp de-escalation by Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi PMF, etc., with no attacks on Gulf shipping, infrastructure, or US/Israeli assets.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Open transit without mines, seizures, attacks, or credible threats; US/Iran blockades lifted or neutralized; safe passage for neutral commercial vessels.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic and throughput returning to at least 70-80% of pre-war daily averages (~100-140 commercial transits/day, with oil/LNG flows near normal volumes).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums normalizing to low single-digit percentages of hull value (or better); major insurers resuming standard coverage without government backstops or massive surcharges.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Iran) exporting at or near pre-war rates without major disruptions; no sustained shortages or forced rerouting.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility returning to normal ranges; no persistent risk premia driving prices >$20-30 above fundamentals; Asian/European buyers resuming normal contracting.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval/air assets de-escalating from heightened alert/blockade levels; Iranian IRGC-Navy standing down from Hormuz operations; no major new deployments.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakthroughs, leadership crises, proxy surges, or unresolved core disputes (e.g., enrichment, sanctions relief, Hormuz sovereignty) likely to reignite within months.<grok:render card_id="adf628" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">11</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="2735a1" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">12</argument></grok:render>

A temporary ceasefire or lower prices alone does **not** qualify. Normalization is gated by commercial confidence, not just battlefield silence.

Breaks down: lower proxy involvement, no US direct strikes/regime decapitation. 2. **Gulf War 1990-91 (Iraq invasion/Kuwait liberation)**: Major oil disruption; shipping recovered in weeks-months after coalition victory and mine clearance. Breaks down: decisive military outcome vs. current stalemate. Suggests 1-3 months for partial recovery if clear victor.

3. **Houthi Red Sea attacks (2023-2025)**: Prolonged low-level disruption; shipping rerouted, insurance spiked; partial recovery slow despite ceasefires. Relevant for asymmetric proxy threats. Suggests 3-12 months for full normalization even with de-escalation.

4. **Post-Suez Canal blockage or minor Gulf incidents**: Quick recovery (days-weeks) when no ongoing threat. Breaks down: current is high-intensity, multi-actor.

5. **Broader post-war stabilizations (e.g., post-2003 Iraq, Libya 2011)**: Commercial activity lagged military pauses by months-years due to lingering risks, insurance, and confidence issues.

**Step 6: Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate by horizon; gating elements bolded)

- Major reduction in direct strikes: Higher (20-40% at 1mo, 60-80% at 6mo) due to costs.
- **Sustained safe commercial transit**: Much lower (near 0 short-term; 30-50% at 6mo) — **primary gate**.

- Export recovery: Tied to transit/insurance.
- Proxy restraint/force drawdown/no new triggers: Moderate, 40-70% medium term, but reversible.

**Step 7: Synthesis**

Sub-components are not independent; **commercial transit + insurance** are dominant gates. Military pauses can occur faster, but full normality requires credible, sustained risk reduction for shippers/buyers/insurers. Synthesis weights gating factors heavily, producing conservative odds. Base rates adjusted upward modestly for ongoing talks, downward for recent clashes and resilience evidence.

**Step 8: Red-Team Critique**

Overestimating normalization: Underweighting Iranian post-regime-change resilience, proxy autonomy, and how insurance/shipping memory lingers (Red Sea precedent). Underestimating: Diplomatic breakthroughs or US pressure yielding quick Hormuz opening; Gulf economic pain forcing faster deals. Load-bearing assumptions: Fragile ceasefire holds without major miscalc; no nuclear dash or leadership collapse. Strongest fast case: Comprehensive deal lifts blockades quickly. Prolonged: Stalemate + sporadic clashes.
