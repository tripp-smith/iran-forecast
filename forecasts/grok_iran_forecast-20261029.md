**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (as of ~29 October 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires *all* of the following to hold simultaneously for a sustained period of at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal or re-escalation:

- Direct military activity: No significant kinetic strikes by the US or Israel on Iranian territory, or by Iran on US/allied military assets or territory in the region (beyond isolated, low-impact incidents).  
- Proxy activity: Houthi and other Iranian-aligned proxy attacks on Gulf states’ territory, energy infrastructure, airports, or commercial shipping reduced to negligible levels that do not disrupt commercial or energy operations.  
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Absence of mine threats, projectile/drone attacks on commercial vessels, IRGC “illegal route” enforcement, and mandatory military escorts for routine transit.  
- Commercial transit levels: Daily vessel transits through Hormuz near pre-war averages (~80–100+), with normal AIS broadcasting; dark/escorted southern-route operations are no longer the primary mode.  
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional premiums returned to near pre-war levels (<0.5–1% of hull value), with broad market availability and no systemic capacity withdrawal or short-notice cancellations.  
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf oil and LNG exports (non-Iranian volumes plus Iranian under pre-war sanctions norms) flowing at or near pre-war levels through normal channels, without elevated risk premia, heavy diversion to bypass pipelines/STS, or blockade effects.  
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent crude and related markets not persistently elevated or volatile specifically due to Gulf disruption risk (beyond fundamentals).  
- Force posture of major actors: Major US naval assets (carrier strike groups and associated escorts) drawn down toward pre-crisis levels; no elevated readiness for large-scale strikes.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No events (nuclear-related, major attacks, leadership shocks, or proxy surges) that would re-trigger the above conditions.

A temporary ceasefire, partial oil-flow recovery via protected routes or bypasses, lower (but still elevated) prices, or a battlefield pause does *not* constitute normality. The definition is multi-domain and operationally demanding.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot**  
As of late October 2026 (war day ~243 since ~28 February 2026 US-Israeli strikes that killed the prior Supreme Leader), the conflict is in a maritime-dominant phase. No major recent US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory have been reported; President Trump has stated no attacks before the 3 November US midterms, though reporting indicates preparations and Gulf-state warnings for a possible short, intense post-election campaign. The US maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports. Iranian attacks on commercial shipping in and near Hormuz remain elevated (October the highest monthly total of the war by multiple trackers, including multiple ships hit or mined; claims and attributions sometimes disputed).  

Gulf oil flows have adapted and, by some Kpler weekly figures, approached or briefly exceeded pre-war regional totals (~23–24 mbd) via a mix of US-escorted southern Hormuz routes (often AIS-dark), ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman, and increased use of Saudi/UAE bypass pipelines (now ~40% of non-Iranian crude in some periods vs. ~17% pre-war). Hormuz-specific flows remain well below pre-war (roughly 40–80% depending on the week and tracker; daily vessel counts far below ~85–100+). Iranian oil exports remain heavily constrained by the blockade.  

War-risk insurance premiums for regional tankers stand at 6–10% of hull value (vs. ~0.25% pre-war), with high costs, seven-day cancellation clauses, and capacity constraints. Brent crude trades near $114–117. Houthi attacks on Saudi airports, oil infrastructure, and cities continue at elevated levels, disrupting aviation and threatening energy assets. Qatari mediators have presented proposals; Iran is reviewing them amid hardline rhetoric on both sides. US carrier presence is elevated (multiple strike groups in or heading to the region). **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes:  
- 1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq): Multi-year low-intensity attacks on shipping with escorts; traffic continued but at risk. Full functional normalization followed the 1988 ceasefire by months. Relevant for resilient maritime harassment by Iran; breaks down because it was bilateral attrition without the current US-Israel intensity or nuclear dimension. Suggests 12–24+ months post-major de-escalation for commercial normalcy.  
- 2019 Hormuz/Saudi tanker attacks and Abqaiq: Limited incidents produced sharp but short-lived price spikes and insurance hikes; flows recovered in weeks to a few months. Relevant for Gulf energy sensitivity; breaks down as incidents were far smaller and non-sustained. Suggests rapid partial recovery if attacks stop.  
- Houthi Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb disruption (2023–ongoing): Prolonged reduced traffic, elevated insurance, and partial adaptation (Cape routing) lasting 1–2+ years without full normalcy. Highly relevant for proxy maritime disruption and insurer caution; breaks down as Hormuz has fewer easy bypasses. Suggests commercial lag of many months.  
- 1991 Gulf War: Rapid military conclusion; shipping largely normalized within weeks, though infrastructure damage lingered. Relevant for high-intensity but short conflicts; breaks down given the current attritional maritime character and lack of decisive ground campaign.  
- Broader limited interstate or chokepoint wars: Commercial normalization (insurance, unrestricted transit, force drawdown) typically lags military pauses by 3–12 months even after ceasefires, longer without political settlement.  

**Base-rate prior** (probability of full operational normality by horizon, before case-specific updating, assuming typical de-escalation pressures in such conflicts):  
1 week: ~2%; 1 month: ~5%; 3 months: ~15%; 6 months: ~30%; 1 year: ~45%; 18 months: ~60%.  

These are outside-in starting points reflecting that full multi-domain commercial and force-posture normalcy is slower than military pauses.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- Diplomatic channel open (Qatari mediation) and mutual interest in avoiding further economic damage.  
- Demonstrated commercial adaptation (escorts, bypass pipelines, STS) showing resilience and reducing Iranian leverage over total flows.  
- US political calendar (post-midterm decision point) could produce either settlement or decisive action that ends the maritime phase.  
- High economic costs to all parties (oil prices, insurance, disrupted trade).  

Ranked importance: diplomatic breakthrough > sustained attack cessation > insurance capacity return > force drawdown.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Active, elevated Iranian maritime campaign and mine threats that keep insurers and shippers cautious.  
- Ongoing proxy (Houthi) attacks on Saudi energy and infrastructure, extending the conflict domain.  
- Elevated US force posture and reporting of post-midterm strike preparations; blockade continues.  
- Absence of a durable political settlement; history of short-lived pauses (April ceasefire, June MOU).  
- Nuclear and leadership issues as unresolved escalatory risks.  

Ranked importance: continued shipping attacks + insurance pricing > proxy activity > force posture/strike risk > lack of settlement.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 1%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~2%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward (active October attacks, insurance peak, Houthi activity, no settlement).  
- Core rationale: Immediate conditions are the opposite of the definition; any pause would be temporary.  
- Main gating factor: Cessation of shipping attacks and insurance capacity.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Immediate, verified ceasefire covering maritime domain + mine clearance start.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: New major attack or confirmed strike preparations executing.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward.  
- Core rationale: Post-midterm window raises short-term escalation risk; commercial metrics require weeks of calm that do not exist.  
- Main gating factor: Post-election decision (strike vs. deal) and attack tempo.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Confirmed durable ceasefire by mid-November with insurance quotes dropping.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumption of large-scale strikes or surge in Houthi/shipping attacks.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 8%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~15%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward (persistent maritime campaign and proxy activity).  
- Core rationale: Even with a deal, mine clearance, insurer re-entry, and force adjustments take time; history of failed pauses.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization and unrestricted transit.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Sustained 30+ day attack-free period + insurance <2%.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Continued or intensified attacks into early 2027.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 18%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~30%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward.  
- Core rationale: Possible post-election settlement or exhaustion could produce partial calm, but full multi-domain normality (especially insurance and force posture) remains unlikely without clear political resolution.  
- Main gating factor: Durable political settlement that constrains proxies and ends blockade/enforcement.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Verified settlement + measured force drawdown.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Renewed high-intensity phase or nuclear-related escalation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 32%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~45%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward.  
- Core rationale: Longer horizon allows for adaptation, possible regime or policy shifts, or war-weariness, but reference classes show commercial lags and residual risks (mines, proxies, insurance memory) persist.  
- Main gating factor: Proxy restraint and sustained insurance normalization.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Full political settlement + verified mine-free status + insurance near pre-war.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Frozen conflict with ongoing low-level maritime harassment.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Low-Medium  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 48%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~60%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward.  
- Core rationale: Sufficient time for most reference-class outcomes to resolve toward normality if the war does not become permanently attritional; residual uncertainty from nuclear issues, proxy networks, and insurer caution keeps it below 50%. Justification for not higher: the operational definition is deliberately strict (requires simultaneous multi-domain conditions, not mere pauses), and evidence of resilience on both sides supports prolonged contestation risk.  
- Main gating factor: Absence of new major escalation triggers and complete commercial reversion.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Clear end-state agreement by mid-2027 with verified metrics improving.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Entrenched low-intensity maritime/proxy conflict.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Low  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued elevated maritime harassment and partial commercial adaptation through late 2026/early 2027, with a post-midterm decision point producing either a limited renewed air campaign or a fragile pause. Full multi-domain normality is unlikely before mid-to-late 2027 at the earliest; residual insurance and force-posture effects linger.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
A rapid post-midterm political settlement that ends the blockade, constrains Houthis, and allows mine clearance could produce meaningful commercial recovery by 3–6 months and near-normality by 1 year (insurance and transit catching up). Strongest if both sides face acute domestic pressure and mediators lock in verification.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
No durable settlement; Iran maintains low-cost maritime harassment and proxy pressure while the US sustains the blockade and elevated posture. Shipping and insurance remain disrupted for 12–18+ months, with occasional spikes. Strongest if nuclear issues or leadership dynamics prevent compromise.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that I overestimate normalization speed: the definition’s multi-domain strictness, insurer memory effects (seen in Red Sea), and Iran’s demonstrated ability to sustain low-cost maritime pressure could push timelines longer than reference classes suggest; residual mines and proxy networks are sticky. Strongest case that I underestimate it: commercial adaptation has already been more resilient than early 2026 expectations, and high costs create stronger incentives for a deal than pure military logic implies. Load-bearing assumptions: that post-midterm action does not produce a decisive but destabilizing outcome, and that insurance markets respond within months of sustained calm (historically variable).
