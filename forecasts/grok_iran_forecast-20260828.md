**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality after the 2026 Iran War outbreak, by horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months (as of 28 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
All of the following must be substantially and observably met on a sustained basis (not a temporary ceasefire, battlefield pause, partial corridor, or oil-price dip):

- Direct military activity: No sustained US/Iran or major Israeli-Iran kinetic exchanges targeting Gulf/Hormuz infrastructure, forces, or shipping for ≥30 consecutive days.
- Proxy activity: Material reduction in IRGC-linked proxy attacks (Houthi, Hezbollah, or others) on commercial shipping or Gulf targets to near pre-war or negligible sustained levels.
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and approaches): No successful attacks on commercial vessels for ≥30 days; mines cleared and independently verified; free navigation without discriminatory Iranian mandatory permits, coordination, or fees that effectively assert exclusive control.
- Commercial transit levels: Sustained AIS-visible commodity/energy vessel transits averaging ≥70% of pre-war baseline (roughly ≥60–70+/day) for ≥2 weeks.
- War-risk insurance: Premiums ≤2–3× peacetime baseline (current multiples reported 15–40×), with meaningful improvement or removal of elevated Joint War Committee listing conditions for standard routes.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf producers (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Iraq, and residual Iranian volumes) achieving ≥80% of pre-war seaborne export volumes via Hormuz or functional equivalents, without heavy reliance on ship-to-ship transfers or production shut-ins driven by transit risk.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Material erosion of the geopolitical risk premium; realized volatility returning toward non-crisis norms; prices not structurally elevated solely by Hormuz risk.
- Force posture of major actors: Measurable de-escalation or drawdown in US CENTCOM interdiction-focused presence relative to peak crisis levels; Iranian forces not actively enforcing exclusive administrative control.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No regime-threatening strikes, nuclear-related actions, or third-party interventions that re-ignite major hostilities.

A temporary MOU, partial corridor, or lower oil price alone does not satisfy the definition.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (28 August 2026)**  
War began ~28 February 2026 with US-Israeli strikes. Iran imposed severe restrictions on Hormuz. A June MOU produced a brief traffic rebound (peaking near half of pre-war levels in late June) before collapsing in July with renewed attacks, US strikes, and reimposition of the US blockade of Iranian ports.  

As of late August: commodity/energy transits remain ~4–15% of pre-war (recent daily counts often single digits to low teens; many dark/AIS-off; ~430–500 vessels and thousands of seafarers still affected). War-risk premiums remain extremely elevated (reported 15–40× peacetime). Brent trades ~$88–89. Iran and Oman have announced a framework for a temporary joint corridor and mine-clearing with revenue-sharing elements; Iran conditions full reopening on US acceptance of its terms (end blockade, compensation, etc.). The US opposes key elements that would restrict freedom of navigation or introduce fees/joint control favoring Iran. Qatar is mediating; direct US-Iran talks are not active and the US posture is “not in a hurry.” Intermittent vessel attacks continue. US forces report mine-clearing progress and maintain the blockade. Proxy activity persists at reduced but non-zero levels. **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
Relevant classes (3–6):

- **Tanker War (1984–88)**: Prolonged attacks on shipping in the Gulf/Hormuz approaches during the Iran-Iraq War. Strait never fully closed; traffic continued under elevated risk and insurance. Normalization tracked the broader ceasefire (years of elevated risk). Relevant for sustained asymmetric maritime pressure and insurance lag. Breaks down because 1980s traffic was less globalized and Iran retained stronger economic incentives to keep its own exports flowing.
- **Houthi Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb (late 2023–2025+)**: Attacks produced 50–90% traffic collapses on affected routes. Even after attack intensity fell, commercial recovery lagged months due to insurance, operator risk aversion, and behavioral change. Partial recovery visible within 6–12 months of sustained calm; fuller normalization slower. Highly relevant for insurance/operator lag and proxy dynamics. Breaks down in scale (Hormuz is a tighter, higher-volume energy chokepoint) and because the current case involves direct great-power/Iran kinetic exchange plus dual blockades.
- **2019 Gulf of Oman tanker attacks**: Brief spike in risk and insurance; traffic recovered relatively quickly once the campaign did not escalate into sustained control. Useful lower bound for short, contained episodes.
- **Suez Canal Ever Given (March 2021)**: Purely physical 6-day blockage. Backlog cleared in days to a couple of weeks; market/insurance effects largely short-lived. Relevant only as a contrast for pure physical vs. security/political disruption.
- **Broader post-conflict maritime/energy recovery patterns** (Iranian Revolution oil disruption, early Iran-Iraq War export effects, other chokepoint crises): Commercial confidence and insurance normalization typically lag military pauses by 3–12+ months; full functional recovery often requires durable political settlement.

Base-rate prior (before current-case evidence): Probability of meeting the full multi-domain definition is very low in the first 1–3 months of a sustained, high-intensity multi-domain disruption of this scale (<5–15%). Meaningful rise only after a durable political settlement plus several months of demonstrated calm for insurance and operator behavior. By 12–18 months, base rates from analogous prolonged disruptions support higher but still modest probabilities of full functional normality (roughly 25–45% range depending on settlement quality), with substantial residual risk of prolonged elevated conditions.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Durable US-Iran political settlement that restores unrestricted free navigation and lifts the dual blockade dynamic.  
2. Verified, sustained absence of attacks + independent mine clearance.  
3. Insurance market repricing after demonstrated calm (typically requires weeks to months).  
4. Operator behavioral return (fleet redeployment, contracts).  
5. Gulf-state mediation success and Iranian economic pressure for revenue restoration.  

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Core political disagreement over who controls/administers Hormuz and freedom-of-navigation principles.  
2. Iranian incentive to retain leverage via the strait.  
3. Insurance and commercial risk aversion lag (persistent even after kinetic pause).  
4. Residual proxy capabilities and escalation risk from other fronts.  
5. Mutual mistrust after repeated MOU breakdowns; US domestic/political incentives not to appear to concede control.  
6. Technical and verification challenges (mines, “dark” traffic, enforcement).  

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (approximate probabilities the sub-component is largely satisfied)  

- Major sustained reduction in direct strikes: 1w ~5–10%; 1m ~15–25%; 3m ~35–50%; 6m ~55–70%; 1y ~70–85%; 18m ~80–90%.  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (≥70% levels, free navigation): 1w <5%; 1m ~5–15%; 3m ~15–30%; 6m ~30–50%; 1y ~50–70%; 18m ~60–80%.  
- Meaningful insurance normalization (≤2–3×): lags transit/security by weeks–months; correspondingly lower at each horizon.  
- Oil/LNG export recovery to high levels: tracks transit closely but can partially use pipelines/STS; slightly higher than pure transit.  
- Proxy restraint: moderately higher than direct-strike reduction at longer horizons if core deal holds.  
- Force drawdown: lags political settlement.  
- No new major escalation trigger: declining probability of “clean” path as horizons lengthen due to residual risks.  

Gating factors are primarily the political settlement + sustained calm for commercial/insurance confidence. Transit and insurance are the hardest commercial gates.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**

- Current-evidence adjustment: Down (active constraints, no deal, high insurance).  
- Core rationale: Impossible to clear all operational criteria in one week.  
- Main gating factor: Political settlement + demonstrated calm.  
- What would raise: Immediate comprehensive deal + zero incidents + rapid insurance drop (extremely unlikely).  
- What would lower: Further attacks (already near floor).  
- Confidence: High.

- What would lower: Breakdown of Oman channel or new strikes.

- Main gating factor: Durable political settlement + initial calm.

- What would lower: Frozen conflict with permanent elevated risk premium and restricted transit.  
- Confidence: Medium (longer horizons inherently less precise).  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-level friction and diplomatic maneuvering around partial corridors. Traffic remains well below normal for months. A political deal eventually emerges under economic pressure, followed by a multi-month lag for insurance and operators. Full functional normality (per definition) is more likely than not only after 12–18 months, and even then may be imperfect.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Rapid US-Iran deal (via mediators) that restores unrestricted navigation, lifts blockades, includes verification, and produces an immediate sustained zero-attack environment. Insurance reprices within weeks; operators return aggressively. Probability of this path is low given current positions.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
No durable deal; intermittent attacks and enforcement continue; Iran retains effective leverage; insurance and operators treat the strait as permanently high-risk. Traffic stays structurally suppressed or moves only under special arrangements. This remains a material possibility through 18 months.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
The forecast may overestimate longer-horizon probabilities by assuming eventual political accommodation is more likely than a stable “managed tension” equilibrium in which markets partially adapt (pipelines, STS, dark fleet, alternative sources) without full free-navigation normality. It may underestimate the stickiness of insurance and operator risk aversion even after kinetic pauses (Red Sea precedent). Conversely, it may under-weight the intensity of Iranian economic pain and US/Gulf interest in cheaper energy, which could force faster compromise. Load-bearing assumption: the political control dispute over the strait is not easily papered over.

**14. Critical Signals to Monitor**  
- Concrete US-Iran or mediated text on free navigation vs. Iranian administration/fees.  
- Independent verification of mine clearance and zero successful attacks for 30+ days.  
- War-risk premium trajectory and JWC status.  
- Sustained daily transit counts ≥60–70.  
- Gulf producer export volumes and any production restart announcements.  
- Proxy attack frequency.  
- Force posture changes (US presence, Iranian coastal deployments).  
- Any new major kinetic events or nuclear-related triggers.
