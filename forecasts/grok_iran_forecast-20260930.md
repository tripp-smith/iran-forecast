I need current reporting on the 2026 Iran War and Gulf shipping conditions before I can define the target or assign probabilities. Starting with news, maritime, energy, and official sources.**1. Forecast Question**

Probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality after the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War, by: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

All of the following must hold concurrently for at least 14 consecutive days (a pause is not enough):

- **Direct military activity:** No US/Israeli/Iranian strikes on opposing military targets, ports, energy infrastructure, or naval assets in the Gulf/Hormuz/Gulf of Oman. No claimed or confirmed antiship missile, drone, mine, or small-boat attacks on commercial or naval vessels.
- **Proxy activity:** No Houthi, Iraqi militia, or Hezbollah attacks on Gulf energy facilities, pipelines, tankers, or US/partner bases that are credibly linked to the Iran war.
- **Shipping security:** Commercial vessels transit Hormuz on published international routes without mandatory IRGC/PGSA permits, without US night-escort as a precondition for movement, and without “non-compliant vessel” lists that threaten seizure.
- **Commercial transit levels:** AIS-visible + independently estimated dark/shuttle traffic ≥70% of pre-war daily crossings (~60+ merchant ships/day vs ~85 pre-crisis), sustained, not a one-week spike.
- **War-risk insurance:** Additional war-risk premium for a standard VLCC Hormuz transit ≤1.0% of hull value (vs pre-war ~0.2–0.25% and current 3–10%); major P&I clubs restore standard cover rather than voyage-by-voyage specials; JWC listed-area status relaxed or accompanied by commercially usable rates.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity:** Combined Gulf crude/condensate exports through Hormuz plus bypass pipelines ≥16 mb/d equivalent (pre-war Hormuz ~17–20 mb/d liquids + Qatari LNG); Qatar LNG loadings not structurally constrained by Ras Laffan damage or war-risk refusal.
- **Energy market stress:** Brent 20-day realized volatility consistent with a “risk-on Gulf” regime rather than a war premium (indicatively not persistently above ~$95–100 as a floor driven by physical disruption rather than OPEC policy); no repeated $5–10/day jumps on single-incident news.
- **Force posture:** No further large CENTCOM “mow the lawn” strike packages; Iranian coastal AShM/drone/mine infrastructure not being reconstituted under fire; US carrier/strike-group presence can decline from wartime surge without a new crisis.
- **No major new escalation trigger:** No nuclear-site strike, no mass-casualty attack on a Gulf capital or US base, no formal Iranian declaration of a fee/toll regime that is enforced by fire, no Houthi closure of Bab al-Mandab that re-imposes a dual-chokepoint shock.

Temporary ceasefire, lower oil prints after a speech, or a single high-escort night do **not** count.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)

1. Mutual economic exhaustion (Iran oil blockade + Gulf export losses + US midterm/political cost).
2. Escort + Omani-coast corridor already moving barrels—shows a technical path if politics stop shooting.
3. China/India/EU buyer pressure for predictable flows.
4. Precedent that both sides can sign paper (June MOU) even if they cannot keep it.
5. Adaptation: pipelines, dark fleet, night convoys reduce the value of total closure.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)

1. Nuclear file still tied to Hormuz by Washington; Hormuz control/tolls still tied to survival politics in Tehran.
2. Insurance and P&I hysteresis: underwriters need a long clean record, not a speech.
3. Proxy and second-front problem (Houthis / Iraq / Hezbollah) not party to any US–Iran memo.
4. Attritional “tanker-for-tanker” and coastal-battery reconstitution cycle.
5. Dual-chokepoint risk: even a quieter Hormuz fails the definition if Bab al-Mandab stays hot.
6. Force posture: wartime US naval concentration and IRGC remaining AShM/drone inventory.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Managed abnormality for many months: escorted and dark oil movements recover further; AIS hull counts stay depressed; war-risk stays expensive; talks stutter; periodic CENTCOM and IRGC strikes; Houthis keep a second tax on Saudi/Red Sea logistics. Functional normality arrives only if a broader bargain is struck and then survives 2–4 incident-free months—more likely after 12 than before 6.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Iran, facing oil-export strangulation and currency stress, accepts a no-fee, Oman-coordinated corridor and a nuclear freeze sufficient for Washington; US lifts the port blockade; Houthis are paid or coerced off Saudi infrastructure; underwriters see 60–90 clean days. That path could push 6-month probability toward ~40% and 12-month toward ~55%. It requires Tehran to surrender the Hormuz political lever it has treated as core leverage.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Nuclear talks fail; US treats periodic strikes as equilibrium; Iran enforces permits with occasional fire; Houthis hold Perim/Mokha; insurance stays voyage-special only; dual-chokepoint risk persists. That is a Tanker War / long Red Sea hybrid. 18-month probability of full normality could fall to the mid-20s.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

- **May overestimate later horizons:** Treating “adaptation” as progress toward the definition. Escorted 13 mb/d is not normality. I may be too impressed by barrel recovery.  
- **May underestimate later horizons:** Wars this costly often produce ugly bargains after the first winter/election cycle. If the operational definition were loosened to “oil moves and nobody is invading,” probabilities would be higher—but the assigned definition is deliberately tight.  
- Load-bearing assumptions: (a) insurers will not fully re-enter while IRGC claims residual control; (b) the nuclear file remains linked; (c) Houthis are not independently satiable. If any of those fail, the 12–18 month numbers move 10–15 points.
