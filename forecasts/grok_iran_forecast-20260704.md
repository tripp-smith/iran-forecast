**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain): 

Functional normality means a stable, low-risk operating environment where: 
- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes, mining, or major naval confrontations in/near the Gulf by state actors for sustained periods. 
- **Proxy activity**: Minimal or de-escalated attacks by Iran-aligned groups (e.g., Houthis, others) on shipping or Gulf infrastructure. 
- **Shipping security**: Safe commercial transit through Strait of Hormuz (and nearby) with <5% risk of attack/interference per transit; international routes (including Omani alternatives) accepted without coercion. 
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic and throughput recover to ≥80-90% of pre-war averages (~60+ ships/day, full oil/LNG export volumes). 
- **War-risk insurance**: Premiums return to near pre-war levels (e.g., <0.25-0.5% of hull value for standard voyages); full commercial coverage widely available without government backstops dominating. 
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Sustained flows at ≥85% pre-war capacity without force majeure declarations or major stranded cargoes; no selective tolls/fees disrupting neutral shipping. 
- **Energy market stress/volatility**: Brent volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained >$20-30/bbl spikes from Gulf risks); no ongoing supply shocks. 
- **Force posture**: Major actors (US, Iran, Gulf states) de-escalate presence; no routine escorts or heightened alerts. 
- **Absence of major escalation triggers**: No active disputes over sovereignty, fees, or routes leading to brinkmanship; stable ceasefire/MOU implementation.
