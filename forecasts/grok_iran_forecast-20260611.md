**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, multi-domain):

Functional normality means a sustained state where:
- **Direct military activity**: No kinetic strikes between US/Israel and Iran (or major proxies) for at least 30 consecutive days; no routine attacks on commercial or naval assets.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, PMF, etc., with no major cross-border attacks or maritime harassment attributable to Iran.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Consistent, low-risk transits (e.g., ≥50-70% of pre-war daily vessel traffic ~100-140 ships) without frequent attacks, mining, or credible threats; US/coalition escorts optional but not required for most operators.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Tanker and commercial traffic recovering to ≥70-80% of pre-war norms; minimal stranding or rerouting around Africa.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to near pre-war levels (e.g., <0.5% of hull value for 7-day policies, vs. current 1-5% or higher); standard coverage widely available without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (Saudi, UAE, Qatar, etc.) at ≥80% of pre-war volumes through Hormuz; Iranian exports normalized without ghost fleet reliance or major sanctions evasion risks.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility normalizes (e.g., no sustained spikes >$20-30/bbl from Gulf risks); no ongoing force majeure declarations or major supply shocks.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Israeli drawdown from heightened alert (e.g., reduced carrier presence, strike assets); Iranian naval/air forces not postured for immediate disruption; Gulf states resuming routine operations.
