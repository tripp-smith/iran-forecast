### 1. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”
Functional normality means observable, sustained restoration across multiple domains, not a temporary pause or partial de-escalation:

- **Direct military activity**: No US/Israeli strikes on Iranian targets or Iranian missile/drone attacks on Gulf shipping/bases for at least 30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, and other Iranian proxies (e.g., attacks reduced to pre-2026 baseline levels).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No attacks/mining incidents; freedom of navigation without Iranian permission, tolls, or selective approvals; UKMTO threat level drops from "critical."
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic through Hormuz returns to ≥80% of pre-war averages (typically ~20-25% of global oil trade); no widespread rerouting.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums fall to near pre-war levels (e.g., <0.5% of hull value per transit); full private coverage available without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, etc.) export at near-capacity; Iranian exports normalized without blockade; no forced production cuts due to storage issues.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalizes; no sustained premiums >$10-15/bbl over non-Gulf benchmarks; Asian/European buyers resume standard contracting.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval presence returns to routine Fifth Fleet levels; Iranian IRGC Navy de-escalates patrols; no heightened alerts.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No unresolved nuclear breakthroughs, major proxy flare-ups, or leadership crises triggering renewed brinkmanship.

A ceasefire alone does not qualify. Temporary insurance relief or lower oil prices from rerouting do not qualify.
