**Step 1: Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**<grok:render card_id="d6a94f" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="df21b4" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">5</argument></grok:render>

“Return to functional normality” means observable, sustained multi-domain conditions where:

- **Direct military activity**: No US/Israeli strikes on Iranian targets or Iranian missile/drone attacks on US/coalition forces or Gulf states for at least 30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Houthis, Hezbollah remnants, and other Iran-aligned groups (attacks on shipping or regional targets drop to near-zero sustained levels).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: No successful or attempted attacks on commercial vessels for 30+ days; US/coalition naval presence maintains open transit without active interdiction.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Daily vessel transits through Hormuz return to at least 70-80% of pre-war baseline (~50-60+ vessels/day consistently, per historical averages).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums fall below 0.5% of hull value for standard voyages (from current elevated 1%+ levels); major insurers resume routine coverage without government backstops or frequent cancellations.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Regional exports (Gulf producers + Iran) recover to within 15% of pre-war volumes; no sustained force majeure declarations; Iranian exports viable without heavy smuggling.
- **Energy market stress/volatility**: Brent crude volatility (e.g., 30-day implied or realized) normalizes to pre-crisis ranges; no repeated spikes >$10-15/bbl from Gulf-specific risks.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US/Israeli forces de-escalate to pre-crisis rotation levels; Iranian IRGC Navy reduces aggressive patrols; no reimposed blockades.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No major incidents (e.g., high-profile ship sinking, nuclear site strike, proxy barrage) for the period.

Temporary pauses, lower prices alone, or battlefield lulls without commercial/insurance normalization do not count. Full normality requires commercial confidence to drive self-sustaining transit and pricing.<grok:render card_id="800ae8" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">10</argument></grok:render>

**Step 2: Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant historical cases:

1. **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Prolonged low-level maritime attacks over years. US reflagging/escorts (Earnest Will) gradually improved security but full normalization took months-years post-ceasefire (1988). Relevant for chokepoint asymmetry and insurance sensitivity; breaks down due to lower tech/proxy density today. Suggests 6-18+ months for commercial recovery.<grok:render card_id="acc0e5" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">29</argument></grok:render>

2. **1991 Gulf War (Iraq invasion/liberation)**: Short intense conflict; shipping disruptions resolved relatively quickly post-ceasefire (weeks-months for full flows) with coalition dominance. Relevant for decisive military phase but less proxy entanglement. Suggests faster (1-6 months) normalization under strong US posture.<grok:render card_id="eef46d" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">30</argument></grok:render>

3. **Red Sea/Houthi Crisis (2023-ongoing)**: Persistent low-level attacks caused rerouting, elevated insurance, and partial flow reductions for months-years. Partial recovery slow despite naval ops. Highly relevant for asymmetric threats to shipping. Suggests prolonged (6-12+ months) for full insurance/transit normalization.<grok:render card_id="791bc3" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">17</argument></grok:render>

4. **Post-2019-2020 US-Iran tensions (Soleimani, tanker attacks)**: Brief spikes resolved in weeks-months with de-escalation. Limited relevance due to lower intensity.

5. **General chokepoint recoveries (e.g., Suez, Bab el-Mandeb)**: Insurance and traffic often lag military pauses by 1-3 months; full confidence takes 3-12 months.

**Base-rate prior** (pre-current evidence): Low short-term (1 week/1 month: ~5-20%); rising medium-term (3-6 months: 30-60%); higher long-term (1-18 months: 60-85%).
