**Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months.

**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of all the following for at least 14 consecutive days (with no major reversal):

- Direct military activity: No significant kinetic strikes by US/Israeli forces on Iranian territory or by Iranian forces on US, Israeli, or Gulf state targets (excluding isolated, immediately de-escalated incidents).
- Proxy activity: Houthi and other Iranian-aligned proxy attacks on shipping reduced to pre-February 2026 baseline levels (near-zero for commercial vessels in relevant waters).
- Shipping security: Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters free of active mining, missile, drone, or small-boat attacks on commercial vessels; transit possible without mandatory naval escort or special routing under Iranian control.
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible commercial transits ≥70% of pre-war average (~60+ ships/day, including substantial tanker traffic; pre-war ~85–100/day).
- War-risk insurance: Additional premiums for Hormuz transit returned to ≤1% of hull value (ideally approaching pre-war 0.15–0.25%), with capacity freely available at market rates without special government backstops dominating.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude/condensate exports (Hormuz + reliable bypass) ≥85% of pre-war volumes (~20 mb/d region) and Qatar/UAE LNG exports restored toward pre-war levels without ongoing force majeure on major volumes.
- Energy market stress: Oil and LNG prices no longer carrying a material war-risk premium (Brent not systematically elevated by double-digit % solely due to Hormuz risk); volatility normalized.
- Force posture: Material drawdown of US naval blockade/escort intensity and Iranian IRGC coastal offensive posture from wartime peaks.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No active preparations for nuclear breakout response, regime-change operations, or large-scale infrastructure targeting that would re-trigger the above.

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price drop, or partial flow recovery alone does not qualify.

**Current Situation Snapshot** (as of ~7 November 2026, day ~253)  
Hostilities continue at a reduced but persistent level. US forces have conducted strikes on Qeshm Island targeting Iranian missile/drone sites used against shipping; Iranian claims of drone shoot-downs and mine strikes on tankers continue (UKMTO/IMO data show ~11 attacks in the week to 4 Nov, some vessels sunk; cumulative IMO-reported incidents ~129 with 24 seafarer deaths). AIS-visible commercial transits are ~8–10/day (~10% of pre-war ~85–100), though some US-facilitated passages occur. Oil flows have partially recovered earlier via bypasses and limited Hormuz traffic (previously reaching ~70–80% of pre-war in some weeks) but remain volatile and disrupted; LNG is more severely constrained (Qatar capacity damage, force majeure). War-risk premiums are elevated at 6–10% of hull value. Diplomacy via Qatar (and Pakistan) is active, with Vance involved and post-midterm (Democrats controlling Congress) pressure on Trump; Iran continues to condition reopening on blockade lift, sanctions relief, and sequencing. Houthi activity persists in the Red Sea. Brent has fallen toward ~$98 after a post-midterm drop from higher levels but remains elevated. Data on exact daily oil volumes conflict slightly by source and lag; AIS undercounts dark activity.

**Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **Tanker War (1984–1988)**: Prolonged maritime attacks during Iran-Iraq War; traffic never fully stopped but was heavily disrupted. Relevant for mining/missile patterns and insurance spikes. Breakdown: Broader ground war context and different actor incentives. Suggested timeline: Partial stabilization within months of ceasefire; full commercial confidence 6–18+ months.  
- **2019 tanker incidents and Stena Impero**: Short-lived spikes. Relevant for rapid insurance/shipping reaction. Breakdown: Lower intensity and quicker de-escalation. Suggested: Weeks to low months for partial recovery.  
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–2024)**: Prolonged diversion and insurance elevation. Relevant for proxy persistence and slow traffic recovery. Breakdown: Different chokepoint and actor. Suggested: 3–12 months for partial normalization after threat reduction.  
- **Prior Hormuz threat episodes (e.g., 2011–12)**: Brief rhetorical/limited actions. Relevant for quick market rebounds. Breakdown: Far lower kinetic intensity. Suggested: Days to weeks.  
- **Post-ceasefire maritime recovery patterns (various Gulf/chokepoint cases)**: Insurance and shipowner confidence lag military pauses by weeks to months.

Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment, emphasizing that full multi-domain normality is demanding and rarely instantaneous after contested chokepoint wars):  
1 week: ~5%; 1 month: ~10%; 3 months: ~25%; 6 months: ~40%; 1 year: ~55%; 18 months: ~65%.

**Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Active mediation and post-midterm political incentives for de-escalation in the US (highest short-term weight).  
2. Mutual exhaustion and economic costs (Iranian revenue loss, US fuel-price politics, Gulf state pressure).  
3. Existing partial oil-flow adaptation and prior temporary MoU experience.

**Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Persistent kinetic activity and Iranian leverage via Hormuz attacks/mines (highest).  
2. Deep sequencing disagreements and Iranian demands for major US concessions first.  
3. Insurance lag, shipowner risk aversion, and physical damage (especially LNG).  
4. Proxy activity and risk of renewed US/Israeli escalation.  
5. Force-posture inertia and credibility concerns on both sides.

**Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-level hostilities and restricted Hormuz traffic through late 2026 into early 2027, with intermittent diplomacy. A fragile interim arrangement is possible within 1–3 months that reduces direct strikes and allows partial transit recovery, but full functional normality (especially insurance and LNG) remains unlikely before mid-to-late 2027. Markets and shippers remain risk-averse.

**Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm political pressure plus mediator success produces a sequenced deal within weeks: blockade eased, limited sanctions relief, nuclear steps, and verified demining. Traffic rebounds to 50%+ within a month and insurance falls sharply by month 3 as attacks cease. Probability of this path leading to definition satisfaction by 3–6 months is modest but non-zero.

**Prolonged Instability Case**  
Talks fail; Trump authorizes renewed strikes; Iran intensifies mining and proxy activity; traffic stays suppressed, premiums remain elevated, and LNG recovery is further delayed. Normality is pushed beyond 18 months. This remains a material risk given sequencing disputes and escalation incentives.

**Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that I overestimate normalization: Iranian incentives to retain Hormuz leverage, incomplete LNG reconstruction, and shipowner/insurer caution create longer lags than assumed; midterm pressure may not override Trump’s willingness to strike. Strongest case that I underestimate it: Mutual economic pain and mediator momentum could produce faster de-escalation than historical contested chokepoint cases, with oil adaptation already showing resilience. Load-bearing assumptions: that a military pause is necessary but not sufficient, and that insurance/transit are the true gates. Evidence that would prove the view wrong includes a sustained 14-day attack-free period with rising AIS counts and falling premiums (raises) or a major new US/Iranian strike campaign (lowers).
