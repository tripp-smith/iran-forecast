**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality means observable, sustained multi-domain recovery to pre-war baseline levels (or close functional equivalents) without reliance on temporary pauses. It requires **all** of the following for at least 4 consecutive weeks:  
- **Direct military activity**: No US/Israeli strikes on Iranian targets or Iranian direct attacks on US/Israeli forces/assets; no active naval blockade.  
- **Proxy activity**: Significant restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, PMF, etc. (attacks on shipping/energy down >80% from peak).  
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No mine incidents, attacks on commercial vessels, or credible threats forcing rerouting; US/Iranian naval forces in de-escalatory posture.  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Tanker and cargo transits through Hormuz at ≥80% of 2025 averages; no widespread avoidance by major operators.  
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to near pre-war levels (e.g., <0.5% of hull value per transit); full coverage available without special riders or government backstops.  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf exports (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Iran, Qatar) at ≥90% of pre-war volumes; no sustained shortages or rationing in Asia/Europe.  
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalized; no geopolitical risk premium dominating prices (> $10-15/bbl sustained add-on).  
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval presence reduced toward pre-crisis levels; Iranian IRGC Navy/IRGC not postured for closure/threats; Gulf states resuming normal operations.  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No leadership assassinations, major proxy breakthroughs, unresolved nuclear crises, or Iranian reconstitution sparking renewed strikes.  

A temporary ceasefire or partial reopening does not qualify. Lower oil prices alone do not qualify if driven by demand destruction.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Mutual exhaustion + economic pain (Gulf states, Asia buyers, Iran revenue loss).  
2. Diplomatic momentum (Pakistan/Qatar mediation, Trump deal interest).  
3. US/Gulf desire to stabilize energy.  
4. Iran's need for sanctions relief/reconstruction.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked higher impact)  
1. **Hormuz leverage dispute**: Iran views control as core deterrence; US demands unrestricted reopening. Gating.  
2. **Reconstitution + proxy restraint failure**: Iran rebuilding; proxies hard to fully control.  
3. **Israel/Lebanon spillover**: Netanyahu incentives to prolong pressure.  
4. **Trust deficit**: Violations erode confidence for insurers/shippers.  
5. **Nuclear/leadership uncertainty**: Lingering Iranian program and internal Iranian politics.
