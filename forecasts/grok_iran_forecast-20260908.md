**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by the horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from ~8 September 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality requires simultaneous satisfaction of all of the following observable conditions for a sustained period (not a single-day snapshot or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No sustained or recurrent US/coalition strikes on Iranian territory or Iranian strikes on US/coalition bases, warships, or Gulf-state territory at a tempo above pre-war background. Isolated, low-intensity incidents do not disqualify if they remain rare and non-escalatory.
- Proxy activity: No significant reactivation of Iranian-aligned proxies (Houthis, residual Hezbollah/Iraqi militias) conducting attacks on shipping, energy infrastructure, or US assets in the Gulf or Red Sea at levels materially above pre-war baselines.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial vessels transit without routine projectile/UAV/mine threats; UKMTO/JMUC/equivalent incident rates return near pre-war levels; no active exclusion zones or dual competing corridors enforced by force.
- Commercial transit levels: Commodity-vessel (especially tanker) daily averages recover to ≥80–90% of pre-war norms (~100–130+ vessels/day range historically; oil flows through Hormuz ≥15–18 mb/d range).
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional war-risk premiums for Hormuz transit fall to ≤1–2× pre-war levels (pre-war typically ~0.05–0.25% of hull value; current elevated multi-percent levels must normalize) and capacity is broadly available without US-government backstops as primary mechanism.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude/condensate and LNG loadings/exports recover to ≥85–90% of pre-war volumes on a sustained basis, without chronic force-majeure declarations or heavy reliance on alternative pipelines/dark shipping.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent/WTI volatility and risk premia attributable to Hormuz/Gulf risk return to pre-war ranges; no persistent multi-dollar-per-barrel war-risk add-on dominating pricing.
- Force posture of major actors: US/coalition forward posture and Iranian coastal/anti-access posture de-escalate from peak wartime levels toward sustainable peacetime or low-intensity deterrence postures (not permanent high-tempo “mow-the-lawn” operations or Iranian exclusion-zone enforcement).
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No unresolved nuclear enrichment reconstitution, no major leadership-targeting cycles, and no new territorial or exclusion-zone claims that re-ignite kinetic exchanges.

A temporary ceasefire, partial corridor under naval escort, reduced oil prices alone, or battlefield pause without commercial and insurance normalization does **not** meet the standard.

A June 2026 MoU/ceasefire framework collapsed in July after Iranian attacks on shipping and US responses. **4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant classes (selected for chokepoint, maritime, Gulf, or limited regional war parallels):

- **1980–88 Iran–Iraq War Tanker War phase**: Prolonged (years) attacks on shipping in Gulf/Hormuz approaches. Traffic never fully normalized during hostilities; insurance spiked and stayed elevated; full commercial recovery only after ceasefire + postwar settlement. Timeline for functional shipping recovery: multi-year. Breaks down: higher intensity and mutual attrition than 2026 so far; no US direct combat role of current scale.
- **2019–2021 Iran–US/Saudi tanker attacks & Soleimani aftermath**: Series of limpet/UAV/mine incidents + one major escalation. Shipping risk premium rose but traffic largely continued with adjustments; insurance normalized over 12–24 months once kinetic tempo fell. Partial recovery in months; fuller in 1–2 years. Breaks down: far lower intensity and no sustained state-on-state air campaign.
- **Red Sea/Houthi disruption 2023–2025**: Prolonged proxy attacks on shipping. Traffic diversion persistent; insurance elevated for extended periods; partial recovery only after sustained military pressure + political shifts, still incomplete years later. Suggests multi-quarter to multi-year for chokepoint normalization under proxy persistence.
- **1990–91 Gulf War / Kuwait liberation aftermath**: Rapid military victory produced relatively fast shipping/oil recovery (months) once Iraqi forces ejected and sanctions framework stabilized. Breaks down: decisive conventional outcome and clear victor; 2026 lacks this.
- **Suez crises / temporary closures (1956, 1967)**: Full closure produced multi-month to multi-year traffic/insurance effects even after physical reopening. Normalization lagged political settlement.
- **Post-2003 Iraq or post-2011 Libya energy/maritime recovery**: Political/military resolution incomplete → years of elevated risk, partial flows, insurance friction.

**Base-rate prior (before case-specific evidence, approximate cumulative probability of functional normality by horizon):**  
1 week: ~1–3%; 1 month: ~5–10%; 3 months: ~15–25%; 6 months: ~30–45%; 1 year: ~50–65%; 18 months: ~60–75%.  
These reflect that limited Gulf maritime disruptions historically resolve only after durable political-military de-escalation, not mere pauses, and commercial/insurance lag kinetic pauses by months to years.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**

1. Mutual exhaustion / political calendar incentives (US midterms, Iranian economic strain) favoring de-escalation deals.  
2. Existence of mediation channels and prior MoU text as potential restart point.  
3. Shared interest in oil revenue (Iran, Gulf exporters, consumers) and avoiding permanent diversion of Asian buyers.  
4. US ability to degrade Iranian anti-shipping systems faster than Iran can reconstitute under sustained pressure.  
5. Limited proxy activation to date (reduces second-order Red Sea/Gulf spillover).

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**

1. Absence of a durable political settlement on nuclear, sanctions, and security guarantees; “mow-the-lawn” can continue indefinitely.  
2. Iranian residual anti-access capability and willingness to contest corridors/exclusion zones.  
3. Commercial and insurance lag: even after kinetic reduction, owners/insurers require months of clean record before full return.  
4. Second-order effects: elevated force posture, residual mines/UAVs, buyer risk aversion in Asia, Gulf-state hedging.  
5. Escalation triggers still live (nuclear reconstitution attempts, leadership targeting, exclusion-zone enforcement, Israeli involvement).  
6. Historical pattern that Gulf chokepoint risk normalizes slowly after state-on-state fighting.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts**

Approximate probabilities that each major component is largely satisfied (sustained) by horizon (order-of-magnitude, correlated):

- Major reduction in direct strikes: 1w ~5%; 1m ~15%; 3m ~35%; 6m ~55%; 1y ~70%; 18m ~80%.  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (Hormuz): 1w ~2%; 1m ~8%; 3m ~25%; 6m ~45%; 1y ~60%; 18m ~70%.  
- Meaningful insurance normalization: 1w <1%; 1m ~5%; 3m ~15%; 6m ~35%; 1y ~55%; 18m ~65%.  
- Oil/LNG export recovery to high % of pre-war: 1w ~3%; 1m ~10%; 3m ~30%; 6m ~50%; 1y ~65%; 18m ~75%.  
- Proxy restraint sustained: higher baseline (~40–70% rising) given limited activation so far.  
- Force drawdown / posture reduction: lags kinetic reduction (lower than strike reduction).  
- No new major escalation trigger: declining with time as windows for provocation remain open (starts high short-term, falls).

Gating factors are primarily safe transit + insurance + absence of new triggers; oil recovery and strike reduction are necessary but not sufficient.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Continued low-to-medium intensity attrition with intermittent pauses and limited deals that reduce but do not eliminate risk. Traffic and exports recover partially under naval protection or dual arrangements; insurance remains elevated for many months; full multi-domain normality is delayed into 2027. “Mow-the-lawn” becomes a semi-permanent management tool rather than a path to rapid closure.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Strong mutual political incentives (US domestic calendar, Iranian economic distress) produce a verified comprehensive framework within 1–3 months, followed by rapid mine clearance, monitored corridor, insurance capacity return, and sustained low incident rates. Probability of this path materializing is modest (~15–25% overall weight).

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Core issues (nuclear, sanctions, security guarantees) remain unresolved; Iran retains enough anti-shipping capacity to contest corridors; insurance and commercial confidence stay impaired; occasional escalations or exclusion-zone enforcement keep risk elevated into 2027–28. This is the higher-weight path under current evidence.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Possible overestimation of longer-horizon probabilities: commercial and insurance markets have long memories; a “frozen” high-risk equilibrium (partial flows under escort, permanent elevated premiums) may persist even after kinetic reduction, keeping true functional normality below 50–60% at 18 months. Possible underestimation of short-to-medium horizons: if exhaustion is deeper than visible or a face-saving formula is found quickly, a cascade into safer transit could occur faster than base rates suggest. Load-bearing assumptions include continued limited proxy restraint and absence of a decisive new escalation trigger; both could break.
