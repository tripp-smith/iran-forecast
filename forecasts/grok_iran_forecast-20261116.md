**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon (1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained (at least 2–4 weeks of observed stability) satisfaction of the following observable thresholds:  
- Direct military activity: No significant kinetic strikes by US/Israel or Iran against each other’s territory, forces, or energy/infrastructure targets in the Gulf theater.  
- Proxy activity: Houthi, Iraqi militia, and other Iranian-aligned forces refrain from attacks on Saudi, UAE, Bahraini, or maritime targets at levels materially above the pre-February 2026 baseline.  
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters): No systematic mining, missile/drone, or small-boat attacks on commercial vessels; threat level reduced from “severe” to moderate or lower per UKMTO/JMIC assessments.  
- Commercial transit levels: AIS-visible + dark transit volumes (tankers, LNG, containers) recover to ≥70–80% of the late-2025/early-2026 baseline (~80–100 large commercial vessels/day equivalent), with free use of established traffic-separation schemes.  
- War-risk insurance: Additional premiums fall to ≤1–2% of hull value per voyage (from current 6–10% peaks), with broad market availability and cancellation clauses normalized.  
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf crude exports (including Iran) approach pre-war volumes (~20 mb/d regionally including Iran); LNG flows from Qatar/UAE uninterrupted; Iranian crude no longer subject to effective blockade.  
- Energy market stress: Brent volatility and absolute premium over non-war fundamentals compress to near-normal levels; no sustained supply-shock pricing.  
- Force posture: US carrier/naval presence returns toward pre-war norms (1–2 carriers rather than 3); Iranian IRGC naval posture de-escalates from active interdiction.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No large-scale Israeli or US strikes, no successful mass-casualty attacks on Gulf hosts or US assets, and no collapse of mediation channels.  

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price decline, or partial transit recovery alone does not meet the definition. Commercial and insurance normalization are gating.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of 16 November 2026, ~Day 262)**  
War began 28 February 2026 with joint US-Israeli strikes. A June 2026 Islamabad MOU for 60-day talks collapsed in July; hostilities and blockade resumed. As of mid-November:  
- Hormuz is severely restricted. UKMTO counts place recent weekly transits ~65% below pre-conflict levels; AIS-visible traffic is far lower (some trackers show ~8% or single-digit daily crossings, with widespread dark shipping). Ongoing incidents include projectile strikes and Iranian claims of mine hits on tankers (some unverified). JMIC threat level remains severe. IMO has logged 130+ incidents and ~25 seafarer deaths.  
- Oil: IEA November report shows October Gulf oil exports (ex-Oman) at 18.4 mb/d (26% below pre-war), crude at 15 mb/d (82% of pre-war). Iranian crude exports near zero under US naval blockade. Global supply has returned to surplus; Brent recently ~$93–96 after peaking above $130. LNG flows from Qatar/UAE have recovered relatively better.  
- Insurance: War-risk additional premiums quoted at 6–10% of hull value (up to 40× peacetime ~0.25%), with some earlier peaks higher.  
- Military: Recent IRGC drone attacks on Bahrain (intercepted, no major damage reported); Houthi missile/drone activity against Saudi targets continues. Three US carriers in theater (temporary rotation). Trump has signaled possible restraint absent new provocations; Rubio has downplayed need for major combat given economic pressure.  
- Diplomacy: Stalled. Iran conditions reopening on seven demands (end blockade, sanctions relief, asset release, regional de-escalation, compensation, formal end to war, etc.). Iranian officials (Ghalibaf, Rezaei) state Hormuz will not return to pre-war status and that the ball is in the US court. Mediation channels (Qatar, Oman) remain open but unproductive.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, 1984–88)**: Sustained attacks on shipping for years; US escorts (Earnest Will) from 1987; major incidents (Bridgeton mine, Samuel B. Roberts, Vincennes). Ended only with full ceasefire August 1988. Relevant for mine/small-boat tactics and escort limits. Breaks down: longer war, lower modern precision, different great-power dynamics. Suggests 6–18+ months of residual risk after de-escalation.  
- **Red Sea/Houthi disruptions (2023–2025)**: Prolonged diversion, elevated insurance, partial traffic recovery only after sustained military pressure and political shifts. Relevant for proxy maritime denial. Breaks down: narrower geography, less existential stakes. Suggests 6–12 months for partial commercial normalization.  
- **Prior Hormuz threat episodes (e.g., 2019, 2020s signaling)**: Usually short-lived spikes in insurance and temporary slowdowns without full closure. Relevant for signaling. Breaks down: no full-scale war or blockade. Suggests rapid (weeks–months) recovery once signaling ends.  
- **Historical chokepoint closures (Suez 1956/1967–75)**: Multi-year effects when political settlement lags. Relevant for second-order trade/insurance lags. Breaks down: different geography and technology. Suggests longer tails.  
- **Post-ceasefire energy/shipping recoveries (e.g., after major Gulf flare-ups)**: Oil flows often rebound faster than full insurance and transit normalization.  

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment)**: 1 week ~1%; 1 month ~5%; 3 months ~15%; 6 months ~30%; 1 year ~50%; 18 months ~65%. These reflect that commercial/insurance normalization lags military pauses and requires durable political resolution in high-stakes chokepoint cases.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- US economic/blockade pressure is severe; Iranian crude revenue collapsed.  
- Partial Gulf oil recovery already achieved via larger tankers, dark shipping, and alternative routing.  
- Trump administration has repeatedly signaled preference for restraint if provocations remain limited.  
- Open mediation channels (Qatar, Oman) and prior MOU precedent.  
- Market rebalancing reduces immediate price spike incentives for further disruption.  

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Iran’s explicit linkage of Hormuz reopening to maximalist conditions (blockade end, sanctions relief, regional de-escalation, compensation).  
- Active proxy (Houthi) and direct (drone/mine claims) pressure continuing.  
- High US and Iranian force postures; three-carrier presence.  
- Insurance markets reprice quickly on incidents and lag political signals.  
- Iranian statements rejecting return to pre-war free-transit status.  
- Risk of miscalculation (successful hit on US/Gulf assets or large Israeli strike).  

Ranked importance: Political settlement and Iranian willingness to accept less-than-maximal outcomes > sustained reduction in attacks > insurance normalization > pure oil-flow recovery.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Managed tension with episodic incidents through early 2027. Partial oil-flow recovery continues via dark shipping and larger tankers. A limited or sequenced deal (blockade relief in exchange for reduced attacks) becomes more likely by mid-2027, producing gradual insurance and transit improvement. Full multi-domain normality remains incomplete at 12 months in the central case; residual elevated risk premiums and occasional incidents persist.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Trump prioritizes deal-making after Asia trip; Iran accepts sequenced concessions (blockade lift + limited sanctions relief). Attacks drop sharply. Insurance markets respond within weeks of verified stability. Probability of normality rises 15–25 points at 3–6 month horizons under this path.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Iran holds maximalist line; a successful attack on US/Gulf assets or large Israeli strike restarts major combat. Houthi campaign intensifies. Insurance stays elevated; transit remains dark/limited. Probability falls 15–25 points across longer horizons.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case for faster: Economic pressure on Iran is acute; partial oil recovery already shows commercial adaptation; US political incentives favor a deal before further costs. I may underweight these.  
Strongest case for slower: Iranian leadership has repeatedly rejected pre-war free transit and tied reopening to hard conditions; proxy networks and mine stocks provide persistent leverage; insurance markets are sticky. I may overstate the speed of commercial response to any military pause. Load-bearing assumptions: that the US blockade remains effective and that neither side seeks full-scale escalation in the next 3–6 months.
