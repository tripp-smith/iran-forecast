**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War (began 28 February 2026) by these horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months. Current date is 16 September 2026 (~Day 200).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

A multi-domain, observable state lasting at least 30 consecutive days in which:

- Direct military activity: No kinetic exchanges between major state actors (US/Israel–Iran) involving strikes on Iranian territory, US/allied bases in the Gulf, or Iranian coastal/missile infrastructure at a rate exceeding pre-war baseline harassment. No sustained naval combat or blockade enforcement actions beyond routine presence.
- Proxy activity: No sustained attacks by Iran-aligned groups (Houthis, residual Hezbollah, Iraqi militias) on Gulf shipping, energy infrastructure, or Saudi/UAE targets that materially disrupt operations.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Free, unimpeded transit passage without Iranian “permission” regimes, mine threats requiring active clearance, or regular anti-ship attacks. AIS-visible commercial transits average ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (~85–130 vessels/day depending on source; use ~100 as midpoint).
- Commercial transit levels: Sustained two-way commercial traffic (tankers, LNG, dry bulk, containers) at ≥70% of pre-war volumes without reliance on dark transits, shuttle STS transfers as primary mode, or systemic avoidance by major Western/Asian owners.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Additional war-risk premiums for Hormuz/Gulf voyages ≤2–3× pre-war baseline (pre-war ~0.1–0.25% of hull value; current multiples 15–40× reported). Lloyd’s Joint War Committee listing risk reduced or premiums competitive enough that most major operators resume regular scheduling.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Persian Gulf crude + products + LNG exports sustained at ≥85–90% of pre-war levels (~21–23 mb/d crude/products equivalent + Qatar LNG) without force majeure declarations, pipeline shutdowns as primary workaround, or floating storage backlogs as structural feature. Iranian exports not zeroed by blockade.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent volatility and risk premium no longer dominated by Hormuz-specific risk; prices and spreads reflect global balances rather than chokepoint disruption (current Brent ~$105–108 with elevated diesel cracks).
- Force posture of major actors: No active US naval blockade of Iranian ports; Iranian forces not enforcing a transit regime or mining lanes; US/allied force levels and alert status consistent with post-conflict residual presence rather than combat operations.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No IAEA referral outcomes, leadership succession crises, or proxy territorial gains that restart tit-for-tat cycles.

Temporary ceasefires, price drops from demand destruction, or battlefield pauses without commercial recovery do **not** count. Partial recovery via bypass pipelines (East-West, Habshan-Fujairah) alone is insufficient.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant classes (3–6):

1. **1984–1988 Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Systematic attacks on shipping in Gulf; ~500 vessels hit; US reflagging/escorts (Earnest Will); mines. Shipping never fully stopped but risk elevated for years. Normalization of commercial behavior and insurance took the 1988 ceasefire + demining + time (months to 1–2 years post-ceasefire for confidence). Relevance: same geography, IRGC tactics, oil chokepoint. Breaks down: bilateral attrition war without nuclear dimension, superpower patronage of one side, no full “closure” claim or modern precision munitions/proxies. Timeline suggestion: partial stabilization 6–18 months after decisive ceasefire.

2. **2023–2025 Red Sea/Houthi disruptions**: Sustained attacks; traffic collapse via Suez/Bab el-Mandeb; insurance spikes; partial recovery with escorts/diplomacy but elevated risk persisted >12–18 months. Relevance: Iran-linked proxies, insurance/shipping behavior, chokepoint. Breaks down: less central to global oil, no direct state-on-state war with US. Timeline: meaningful commercial recovery 6–12+ months after peak intensity drop.

3. **Post-1991 Gulf War / Kuwait liberation maritime recovery**: Rapid physical reopening after decisive coalition victory, but residual mines, insurance caution, and force posture adjustments lasted months. Relevance: Gulf energy infrastructure + US-led ops. Breaks down: clear military victory vs. current inconclusive attrition.

4. **2019–2020 Gulf tanker attacks / Abqaiq**: Limited Iranian/proxy attacks; short insurance spike and traffic caution; rapid de-escalation after US response/diplomacy. Relevance: recent IRGC maritime playbook. Breaks down: far smaller scale, no multi-month war or leadership decapitation.

5. **Broader post-major-war maritime chokepoint recovery** (e.g., post-Suez 1956/1967/1973 patterns, Black Sea post-2022 partial): Commercial confidence lags physical access by 3–12 months; insurance and owner risk appetite slower than naval claims of “open.”

**Base-rate prior (pre-current-evidence adjustment, approximate cumulative probability of meeting full operational definition by horizon, assuming inconclusive war continues without decisive victory or collapse):**  
1 week: ~1–3%; 1 month: ~5–10%; 3 months: ~15–25%; 6 months: ~30–45%; 1 year: ~45–60%; 18 months: ~55–70%.  
(Derived from Tanker War/Red Sea lag of commercial normalization after intensity drop; higher for longer horizons as attrition and economic pain create incentives, lower near-term due to demonstrated fragility of MOUs.)

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)

1. Economic pain on all sides (Iran oil revenue collapse; Gulf states export losses; US inflation/diesel; global buyer pressure) creating incentives for durable deal.  
2. US political calendar (midterms) and munitions costs potentially favoring de-escalation.  
3. Bypass infrastructure and dark shipping already limiting worst-case supply loss.  
4. Prior MOU precedent showing both sides can pause when costs high.  
5. China/India/Asia demand for volume over pure risk aversion.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)

1. Core unresolved issues (nuclear stockpile, Hormuz control claims, leadership legitimacy post-Khamenei, proxy networks).  
2. Demonstrated MOU fragility and mutual distrust; hardliner sabotage risk.  
3. Second-order cascades (Houthi expansion, pipeline vulnerability, insurance inertia).  
4. Force posture lock-in (US blockade elements vs. Iranian coastal/missile residual capability).  
5. Commercial risk aversion lags military claims of control (owners/insurers require sustained quiet).  
6. No decisive military outcome; attrition favors prolonged contestation of the strait.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts**

Approximate probabilities of key components holding for sufficient duration to support overall normality (independent estimates, then gated):

- Major reduction in direct strikes: 1w ~5–10%; 1m ~15–25%; 3m ~35–50%; 6m ~50–65%; 1y ~60–75%; 18m ~70–80%.  
- Sustained safe commercial transit (≥70% volumes): 1w <5%; 1m ~10%; 3m ~25–35%; 6m ~40–55%; 1y ~55–70%; 18m ~65–75%.  
- Meaningful insurance normalization (≤2–3×): lags transit by 1–3 months; lower than transit probs by ~10–20 pts near-term.  
- Oil/LNG export recovery to ≥85–90%: similar to transit but slightly higher due to bypasses (current ~2/3 already).  
- Proxy restraint: lower than direct strikes due to Houthi autonomy (especially 3–12m).  
- Force drawdown / no blockade: tightly coupled to deal; low near-term.  
- No new major escalation trigger: ~60–70% short horizons declining modestly with time as new risks (succession, IAEA, Yemen) accumulate.

Gating factors: commercial transit + insurance are the binding constraints after any kinetic pause. Military pause alone is insufficient (June example).

**10. Most Likely Path**

Continued low-to-medium intensity contestation of the strait with intermittent pauses. Partial commercial recovery via bypasses, dark shipping, and selective risk-tolerant operators keeps Gulf exports in the 60–80% range. Full functional normality (per definition) remains elusive until a more durable political-military arrangement addresses Hormuz control claims and residual capabilities. Insurance and owner risk appetite recover slowly even after kinetic quiet. Probability rises with time mainly because prolonged pain increases deal incentives, not because resolution is inevitable.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

A comprehensive package (nuclear limits, Hormuz transit protocol with verification, proxy restraint mechanisms, sanctions relief elements) brokered under economic/political pressure, held for 60+ days with rapid demining/escorts and insurer response. Traffic and premiums normalize within 2–4 months of signing. Probability of this path: low-to-moderate (15–25% within 6 months).

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

MOUs repeatedly fail; Iran maintains residual anti-access capability and proxy leverage; US maintains pressure; Houthi/Bab el-Mandeb linkage persists; insurance stays elevated; commercial traffic structurally lower. Gulf states deepen hedging. Normality deferred beyond 18 months. Credible given current evidence of fragility.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Overestimation risk: Commercial lag may be longer than modeled (owners remember June collapse); second-order effects (Yemen, diesel markets, munitions) create more escalation triggers than assumed; base rates from Tanker War underweight modern precision and information effects. Underestimation risk: Economic costs (Iran revenue, US political, Asian demand) could force faster accommodation than historical inconclusive wars; bypass infrastructure already softens the worst case, reducing incentives for total contestation. Load-bearing assumptions: commercial/insurance behavior remains risk-averse; no sudden decisive military or political collapse on either side. Strongest critique of own numbers: 6–18 month figures may still be too high given the operational definition’s stringency and the demonstrated ability of hardliners to spoil.
