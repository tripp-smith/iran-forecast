**1. Forecast Question**  
Probability that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War, by horizons of 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from 8 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
All of the following must hold simultaneously for at least 14 consecutive days (to exclude temporary pauses):

- Direct military activity: No US/Israeli or Iranian strikes on military targets, bases, ports, or energy infrastructure inside Iran, the Gulf states, or on shipping; no Iranian ballistic-missile/drone salvos against GCC states or US forces.
- Proxy activity: No Houthi attacks on Red Sea/Gulf of Aden shipping or Saudi infrastructure linked to the Iran conflict; no significant Iraqi militia or Hezbollah kinetic operations tied to the war.
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and approaches): Zero confirmed attacks, boardings, mine incidents, or projectile strikes on commercial vessels for 14 days; UKMTO/JMIC report “normal” risk; AIS tracks show free choice of northern or southern corridors without IRGC coercion.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily two-way commercial transits ≥100 vessels (pre-war baseline ~130–140; recent 7-day averages ~4–8); tanker throughput ≥12–15 mb/d oil equivalent.
- War-risk insurance: Hull war-risk premiums ≤0.5% of hull value (pre-war ~0.1–0.25%; current estimates 7.5–30× baseline, often $3–10 m per VLCC transit); cargo war risk near pre-war levels; major clubs writing cover without exclusion clauses for the Gulf.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf oil exports via Hormuz ≥15 mb/d sustained; Qatari/UAE LNG loadings and transits restored to ≥80% of pre-war capacity (currently near-zero LNG crossings since mid-July in many reports); no widespread force-majeure declarations.
- Energy market stress: Brent ≤$75 sustained (current ~$82–84 range with spikes higher); realized volatility back to pre-war norms; no persistent $20+ war premium.
- Force posture: US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted; significant drawdown of surge forces; Iranian “Persian Gulf Strait Authority” or equivalent not enforcing exclusive control/tolls; no new major minefields.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No assassination of remaining leadership, no nuclear-site strikes that restart cycles, no declaration of formal war expansion, no large-scale proxy mobilization.

A temporary ceasefire, partial route agreement, lower oil prices alone, or battlefield pause without commercial and insurance recovery does **not** qualify.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  

1. **1980–88 Tanker War (Iran-Iraq)**: Closest maritime parallel. Hundreds of attacks; shipping volume fell ~25% at peaks but never near-zero for prolonged periods; oil continued to flow under escorts (Earnest Will). Full commercial/insurance normalization only after 1988 ceasefire (~4 years of elevated risk). Breaks down: bilateral conventional war, lower dependence on LNG, no nuclear dimension, different superpower role. Suggests multi-year elevated risk even after major combat ends.  
2. **2019–20 Iran-US tanker/drone crisis + Soleimani**: Short kinetic spikes; Hormuz traffic recovered in weeks to months once escalation paused; insurance spiked then normalized within 3–6 months. Breaks down: no full-scale war, leadership intact, no prolonged blockade. Suggests 1–6 months for commercial recovery after clear de-escalation.  
3. **2023–25 Houthi Red Sea campaign**: Sustained asymmetric attacks; major carriers diverted for >12–18 months; insurance elevated; partial recovery only after sustained military pressure and reduced attacks. Breaks down: secondary theater, not primary oil chokepoint. Suggests 6–18+ months for full commercial confidence.  
4. **Gulf War 1990–91 / post-ceasefire**: Rapid military end; shipping and oil recovery in weeks-months under coalition control, but residual risk and force posture lingered. Breaks down: clear conventional defeat of one side.  
5. **Post-MoU June–July 2026 partial reopening (internal reference)**: Traffic rose then collapsed within weeks on first major incident. Shows commercial recovery is fragile and gated by sustained security + insurance.  

**Base-rate prior (before current evidence, probability of meeting the full operational definition):**  
- 1 week: 1–3%  
- 1 month: 5–10%  
- 3 months: 15–25%  
- 6 months: 30–45%  
- 1 year: 50–65%  
- 18 months: 60–75%  

(These reflect that pure military pauses occur faster than multi-domain commercial/insurance/force-posture normalization.)

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Mutual economic exhaustion + domestic US political pressure on costs/casualties.  
2. Successful Iran-Oman technical deal that US and major shippers accept as non-coercive.  
3. Sustained 14+ day period without kinetic incidents, allowing insurance to reprice downward.  
4. Explicit US lift of blockade and reduction in force posture.  
5. Iranian regime calculation that controlled transit revenue > continued confrontation.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)  
1. Iranian core objective of recognized control/deterrence via the strait (hardliners/IRGC).  
2. Fragility of any deal: first major incident collapses confidence and insurance.  
3. Proxy autonomy (Houthis) and residual Axis capacity.  
4. US political/military incentives to maintain leverage; Israeli preferences.  
5. Structural insurance and charterer risk aversion that lags kinetic calm by months.  
6. Second-order damage to LNG infrastructure and buyer confidence.

- What would lower: Any new strike or shipping incident.

- Main gating factor: Insurance market and charterer confidence lag.

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-intensity exchanges and suppressed traffic for weeks to a few months while Iran-Oman talks produce a limited technical arrangement. Partial traffic recovery under constrained routes or informal understandings. Insurance remains elevated for 3–9 months. Full multi-domain normality (if achieved) most likely between 9–18 months via economic attrition rather than a clean comprehensive peace. Residual Iranian influence over the strait persists in some form.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Clean, verifiable Iran-Oman-US understanding announced within days, immediate kinetic halt, rapid demining/escort success, insurance premiums fall >50% in 30 days, traffic returns to 80+ ships within 6 weeks. Probability of this path: low (~10–15%).

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Deal fails or is broken by first incident; IRGC consolidates de-facto control; proxies escalate; US maintains blockade/pressure; insurance and charterers treat Gulf as semi-permanent high-risk zone for 12–24+ months. Probability of this path: meaningful (~25–35%).

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Possible over-estimation of normalization: The operational definition is deliberately strict; commercial and insurance recovery historically lags kinetic pauses more than the base rates assume, especially after a full-scale war with leadership decapitation and LNG infrastructure hits. Iranian hardliners may view strait control as non-negotiable regime survival asset.  

Possible under-estimation: US domestic politics, Gulf state economic pain, and Iranian oil/revenue needs could force faster pragmatic arrangements than historical analogies suggest. Markets adapt (discounts, dark shipping, alternative routes) faster than pure security metrics imply.

Load-bearing assumptions: (1) Iranian regime prioritizes survival/revenue over maximalist control if costs rise further; (2) insurance markets will reprice downward after ~60–90 days of proven calm; (3) no major new nuclear or leadership trigger.
