**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (as of 18 November 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of the following observable thresholds for at least 30 consecutive days (not a single data point or temporary pause):

- **Direct military activity**: No sustained kinetic exchanges between US/Iran forces or attacks on Gulf-state territory/assets involving the principal belligerents. Isolated incidents below a low threshold do not disqualify if they do not trigger response cycles.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthis and other Iran-linked proxies restrained from major attacks on commercial shipping or critical Gulf infrastructure (near-zero successful major incidents).
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and approaches)**: Strait open to unescorted commercial transit; mine risk assessed as negligible by UKMTO/IMO standards; incident rate near pre-war baseline.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Combined AIS-visible + estimated dark/ship-to-ship flows ≥70–80% of pre-war volumes across oil, products, LNG, and containers (pre-war Hormuz commercial crossings ~85/day AIS baseline; oil ~17 mbd).
- **War-risk insurance**: Premiums ≤1% of hull value for routine transits (approaching or below the pre-war ~0.25% range), with broad market capacity and no routine cancellations or extreme surcharges.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Non-Iranian Gulf oil/product exports ≥90% of pre-war levels (including pipelines); Qatar/UAE LNG restored to ≥80% of pre-war throughput. Iranian exports under blockade do not need full recovery if the overall system is otherwise normalized under agreed terms.
- **Energy market stress**: Hormuz-related risk premium in Brent not systematically >$5–10/bbl; volatility not driven by daily shipping disruption fears; inventories and flows stable.
- **Force posture**: Material US drawdown (carrier presence reduced to ≤1 strike group; naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted or converted to monitoring).
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No large-scale Israeli operations against Iran, no nuclear-related claims triggering strikes, no mass proxy activation.

A temporary ceasefire, oil-price decline, or battlefield pause alone does not qualify. Partial recovery (e.g., high oil flows via escorts and pipelines with still-elevated insurance and low LNG) is insufficient.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **Tanker War (1984–88)**: Multi-year maritime attacks within the Iran-Iraq War. Shipping continued at reduced levels with escorts; full commercial normalization required the broader ceasefire. Relevant for prolonged chokepoint disruption under active hostilities. Breakdown: different political context (no US blockade of the same intensity; no nuclear overlay). Suggests multi-year recovery if war persists; post-ceasefire stabilization in months.  
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Persistent proxy attacks leading to diversion, high insurance, and incomplete recovery despite naval responses. Relevant for proxy-driven chokepoint risk. Breakdown: narrower geography and less direct great-power involvement. Suggests multi-year incomplete normalization without political settlement.  
- **2019–20 Gulf tanker incidents**: Short spikes after JCPOA tensions; resolved or faded within weeks–months via diplomacy and deterrence. Relevant for limited kinetic maritime friction. Breakdown: lower intensity and no open war. Suggests faster recovery after de-escalation.  
- **1990–91 Gulf War aftermath**: Brief high-intensity disruption followed by rapid shipping recovery post-ceasefire. Relevant for post-conflict rebound once political control is settled. Breakdown: shorter duration and clearer outcome. Suggests months after durable end to hostilities.  
- **Broader Iran-Iraq War maritime component**: Ended with the land-war ceasefire (UNSCR 598). Relevant for linkage between battlefield and sea. Suggests normality tracks the political settlement, not isolated maritime pauses.

**Base-rate prior (outside-in, before case-specific evidence)**:  
1 week: ~5%; 1 month: ~10%; 3 months: ~20%; 6 months: ~35%; 1 year: ~55%; 18 months: ~70%.  
These reflect the historical pattern that functional multi-domain recovery (insurance + unescorted high-volume transit + force drawdown) rarely occurs while active hostilities or unresolved control disputes persist, and typically requires 6–18 months after a durable political resolution.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- Political incentives on both sides (US domestic costs; Iranian economic pressure from blockade). Rank: medium.  
- Existing mediation channels (Oman, Qatar) and occasional statements about possible near-term de-escalation. Rank: low-medium.  
- Partial adaptation (US escorts, bypass pipelines, STS) already supporting significant oil volumes. Rank: low (adaptation ≠ normality).  
- Market mechanisms (insurance capacity remains; some owners accept high rates). Rank: low.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Unresolved core issues (Hormuz control, blockade, sanctions, nuclear inspections/access). Rank: highest.  
- Ongoing kinetic exchanges and Iranian demonstrated ability/willingness to threaten shipping and Gulf hosts. Rank: high.  
- Elevated insurance costs and owner risk aversion keeping commercial volumes low. Rank: high.  
- Elevated US force posture and blockade as both cause and symptom of unresolved conflict. Rank: high.  
- Proxy channels (Houthis) and risk of escalation triggers. Rank: medium-high.  
- Historical pattern that commercial normality lags military pauses by months and requires political settlement. Rank: high (structural).

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

**• 1 Week**  
- Probability: 3%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward (active exchanges this week; insurance and transit still highly abnormal).  
- Core rationale: Immediate cessation of the multi-domain criteria is incompatible with ongoing strikes, high premiums, and low traffic.  
- Main gating factor: Cessation of kinetic activity and insurance/transit response (neither is imminent).  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Immediate durable ceasefire announcement with verified halt to attacks and rapid insurance quote collapse.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Major new strike cycle or confirmed mine campaign.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**• 1 Month**  
- Probability: 7%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~10%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward (stalled talks, recent attack uptick, no force drawdown signal).  
- Core rationale: Even a temporary pause would not produce insurance normalization or unescorted high-volume transit within 30 days; commercial lags are structural.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance and commercial confidence.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Binding ceasefire plus visible US force reduction and insurance rates falling below 1%.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Escalation involving Gulf states or sustained tanker campaign.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-High  

**• 3 Months**  
- Probability: 15%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~20%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward (core political issues unresolved; adaptation does not equal normality).  
- Core rationale: Possible limited de-escalation, but insurance, unescorted transit, and force posture thresholds are unlikely without a political package.  
- Main gating factor: Political settlement enabling force drawdown and insurer confidence.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Framework agreement on Hormuz management + blockade relief + verified quiet.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Collapse of mediation and renewed large-scale strikes.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**• 6 Months**  
- Probability: 28%

- Current-evidence adjustment: Modest downward (stalemate incentives exist but high bar remains).  
- Core rationale: Window for a limited deal opens; historical post-pause recovery timelines become more relevant, but insurance and commercial thresholds still demanding.  
- Main gating factor: Durability of any de-escalation and insurer response.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Signed interim agreement with implementation on shipping security and force posture.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Proxy activation or nuclear-related escalation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium

- Base-rate anchor: ~55%

- Main gating factor: Existence and durability of a political framework that permits force drawdown.

- What would lower this forecast materially: Frozen conflict with continued low-level attacks and high insurance.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium

- Probability: 60%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~70%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Downward (structural issues and historical lags justify skepticism of high certainty).  
- Core rationale: By 18 months, either a settlement or a more stable “managed abnormality” is likely; full functional normality (especially insurance ≤1% and unescorted volumes) has a majority but not dominant probability. Long-horizon uncertainty remains material.  
- Main gating factor: Whether a durable political resolution emerges versus prolonged frozen conflict.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Comprehensive agreement and verified multi-month quiet with force reductions.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Persistent low-level conflict or new major trigger.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Low-Medium  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-level stalemate through early 2027 with partial oil recovery via escorts/pipelines/STS, elevated but not peak insurance, and intermittent kinetic activity. Mediation continues without breakthrough. Full multi-domain normality is more likely after a limited political package (or fatigue-driven quiet) in the 9–15 month window than in the near term. LNG and unescorted high-volume commercial traffic lag oil. Probability of functional normality rises gradually rather than in a step function.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
A sequenced interim deal (Hormuz management formula + partial blockade relief + verified quiet) within 2–4 months, followed by rapid insurance-rate collapse and commercial return. US domestic politics and Iranian economic pressure align. Probability of this path: ~15–20% by 6 months, rising thereafter. Requires both sides accepting less than maximal demands.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Frozen conflict with continued Iranian ability to impose costs on shipping and Gulf hosts, US maintaining elevated posture and blockade, and insurance remaining structurally high. Proxies remain active. Full normality delayed beyond 18–24 months. Probability of this path dominating: ~35–40%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that the forecast overestimates normalization: Core issues (Hormuz control, nuclear, sanctions) are zero-sum enough that neither side can accept a package that enables the insurance and force-drawdown thresholds; low-level conflict can persist indefinitely as a “new normal,” keeping commercial metrics abnormal. Historical Tanker War and Red Sea cases support multi-year incomplete recovery. Strongest case that it underestimates normalization: Political costs on the US side and economic costs on Iran create stronger incentives for a limited deal than currently priced; partial oil recovery already shows adaptation capacity that could accelerate once kinetic activity drops. Load-bearing assumptions: that insurance and commercial behavior will lag any military pause by months, and that force posture will not draw down without a political framework. Evidence of rapid insurer response to a durable quiet or of quiet US force reductions would force upward revision.
