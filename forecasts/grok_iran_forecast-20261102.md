**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires a sustained multi-domain condition (observed for at least 2–4 consecutive weeks) meeting all of the following observable thresholds:  

- **Direct military activity**: No significant kinetic exchanges (airstrikes, missile/drone barrages, or naval engagements) between US/Israeli forces and Iranian forces or assets.  
- **Proxy activity**: Major Iranian proxies (especially Houthis, Iraqi PMF elements, residual Hezbollah capacity) not conducting large-scale attacks on commercial shipping, Gulf energy infrastructure, or US/Israeli targets that materially disrupt Gulf operations.  
- **Shipping security (Hormuz and nearby waters)**: Waters free of active mine-laying or effective IRGC interdiction; commercial vessels can transit without routine high-intensity military escort as the default operating mode.  
- **Commercial transit levels**: Combined AIS-visible and dark commercial traffic (particularly tankers) consistently at ≥70% of the pre-February 2026 baseline (roughly ≥60 equivalent daily commercial crossings).  
- **War-risk insurance**: Additional premiums for Hormuz/Gulf transits reduced to ≤1–2% of hull value (or broadly available at rates no more than 5–10× peacetime levels) with restored market capacity; not dependent on exceptional sovereign backstops as the primary mechanism.  
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Non-Iranian Gulf oil exports and Qatari LNG at ≥90% of pre-war levels without major force majeures or sustained port/export disruptions.  
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: War-risk premium largely priced out of crude (Brent not persistently elevated solely by Hormuz risk; material reduction from current ~$100–115 range toward levels consistent with other fundamentals).  
- **Force posture of major actors**: Significant reduction in dedicated US carrier/strike-group and escort/blockade presence; Iranian IRGC naval and coastal forces not in sustained high-alert denial posture.  
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No new high-profile attacks, leadership strikes, nuclear-related escalations, or proxy surges that restart the disruption cycle.  

A temporary ceasefire, battlefield pause, or lower oil prices alone does not meet the definition. Commercial and insurance normalization are gating requirements.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of 2 November 2026)**  
The conflict began 28 February 2026 with US-Israeli strikes (Operation Epic Fury / Roaring Lion); Ali Khamenei was killed early. It is now Day ~248 of the Hormuz crisis. An April ceasefire and June Islamabad Memorandum collapsed by early July; the dual-blockade dynamic (US blockade of Iranian ports + Iranian restrictions/attacks on shipping) has persisted with intermittent managed flows.  

AIS-visible commercial traffic is ~9–10/day versus ~85 pre-crisis (≈10% of normal). Hundreds of vessels remain waiting on both sides of the strait. Attacks (projectiles, claimed mines, hull explosives) continued through October and into early November; October was among the worst months for shipping incidents. Iran claims control via a Persian Gulf Strait Authority and requires coordination/permits; US CENTCOM coordinates some transits and maintains blockade pressure on Iranian exports.  

Total Middle East oil exports (including pipelines/bypasses and offshore transfers) have recovered to near or above pre-war levels (~24 mbd in mid-October Kpler data). Hormuz clearance itself remains lower and variable (roughly 8–14 mbd depending on the window and whether dark shipping is fully captured—40–80% of pre-war). War-risk premiums are elevated at 6–10% of hull value (roughly 30–60× peacetime); cumulative claims exceed $2 billion. A US DFC-linked reinsurance facility exists but has not fully normalized private capacity.  

Diplomacy continues via Qatar (Iran has floated a shortened two-day plan); Trump has publicly ruled out US strikes before the 3 November midterms. Preparations and messaging point to elevated risk of a short, intense US campaign afterward if talks do not progress. Proxies remain active: Houthis continue pressure on Saudi infrastructure and Red Sea/Bab al-Mandeb routes. Energy prices remain stressed (Brent ~$110 range). Sources include live AIS trackers (hormuz.now and equivalents), Kpler/Vortexa reporting, UKMTO/JMIC, insurer/broker commentary, and major news organizations. Some attack attributions and exact transit volumes remain disputed between CENTCOM and Iranian claims.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  

Relevant historical reference classes:  
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran–Iraq)**: Closest analog for sustained anti-shipping campaigns in the Gulf. Lasted years; US naval intervention degraded Iranian naval capacity (Praying Mantis) but shipping risk and insurance friction persisted until the broader war ended. Suggests 12–36+ months for functional commercial stabilization without political settlement. Breaks down due to different nuclear/proxy density, modern surveillance, and US precision-strike advantages.  
- **2019–2020 Iran–US maritime tensions (tanker attacks, Abqaiq)**: Short spikes in risk and insurance; partial recovery in weeks to a few months after de-escalation. Suggests 1–6 months if attacks cease and political signaling stabilizes. Breaks down because current case is a multi-month open war with leadership decapitation and dual blockades.  
- **Red Sea Houthi campaign (2023–present)**: Proxy denial of a chokepoint; insurance elevated and rerouting persistent for many months despite air campaigns and escorts. Partial recovery possible in 6–18 months; full commercial normality slower. Highly relevant for proxy effects and insurance stickiness.  
- **Black Sea shipping corridors (Russia–Ukraine)**: Severe initial disruption followed by managed corridors and partial recovery over several months with external protection. Suggests 3–12 months for functional flows under escort or temporary arrangements.  
- **Short intense Gulf wars (1990–91, 2003 Iraq)**: Rapid post-conflict commercial recovery for non-belligerent Gulf producers once major combat ended (weeks to a few months). Breaks down because Iran retains significant residual denial capacity and the conflict has not produced decisive regime change.  

**Base-rate prior (outside-in, before heavy case-specific adjustment)**:  
- 1 week: ~5%  
- 1 month: ~10–15%  
- 3 months: ~25%  
- 6 months: ~35–40%  
- 1 year: ~50–55%  
- 18 months: ~60–65%  

These reflect that chokepoint commercial and insurance normalization typically lags military pauses, with insurance and shipping behavior especially sticky.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
1. Physical adaptation already achieved (dark shipping, STS transfers, pipeline bypasses, US coordination) — reduces absolute dependence on a full political deal for oil continuity.  
2. Economic and political pressure on both sides (high energy costs, US midterms, Iranian export constraints and domestic strain).  
3. Active mediation (Qatar and others) and explicit short-horizon proposals.  
4. US capacity to degrade Iranian coastal denial assets if it chooses.  
5. Insurance markets’ historical ability to reprice downward once attack frequency drops for several weeks.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
1. Unresolved core issues (nuclear constraints, sanctions, blockade, Iranian claims of sovereignty over Hormuz).  
2. Insurance stickiness and capacity withdrawal — often the last domain to normalize.  
3. Residual Iranian denial capacity and demonstrated willingness to attack shipping even under escort pressure.  
4. Post-midterm escalation risk (short intense campaign possible).  
5. Proxy activity (Houthis especially) providing secondary pressure and complicating Gulf-state calculations.  
6. Eight months of established disruption already; confidence rebuilding requires sustained calm.

**9. Final Probability Forecasts by Horizon**  

**1 Week**  
- Probability: 5%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~5%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly downward (active attacks, midterm window, no sustained calm yet).  
- Core rationale: Too short for confidence rebuilding, insurance re-pricing, or force posture changes. Any quiet is likely tactical.  
- Main gating factor: Shipping security and insurance.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Immediate, verified multi-day halt in all attacks plus explicit dual announcements of transit coordination.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Fresh high-profile shipping incident or post-election strike announcement.  
- Confidence in this estimate: High  

**1 Month**  
- Probability: 12%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~10–15%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Roughly neutral to slightly downward.  
- Core rationale: Possible post-midterm quiet or limited deal window exists, but insurance and commercial behavior lag. Escalation risk remains material.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization and sustained transit security.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Successful implementation of a short coordinated reopening plan with no attacks for 2+ weeks and visible premium declines.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Resumption of large-scale strikes or continued weekly attack cadence.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-High  

**3 Months**  
- Probability: 25%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~25%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Roughly neutral.  
- Core rationale: Enough time for a managed de-escalation or limited agreement to produce measurable commercial recovery if attack frequency collapses. Still requires the political issues not to re-escalate.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance capacity and absence of new escalation.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Multi-week calm plus clear US force reduction signals and insurance quotes moving below 3–4%.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Post-midterm intense campaign or proxy surge that keeps premiums elevated.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**6 Months**  
- Probability: 38%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~35–40%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly upward (adaptation already demonstrated; economic pressure).  
- Core rationale: Managed or partial political settlement becomes more plausible; physical flows can support higher volumes. Insurance remains the slower variable.  
- Main gating factor: Insurance normalization and force posture.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Sustained 8–12 weeks of low attack rates and visible market capacity returning.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Renewed high-intensity phase or breakdown of mediation.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium  

**1 Year**  
- Probability: 52%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~50–55%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Roughly neutral.  
- Core rationale: Sufficient time for either a political arrangement or durable managed normalization under residual risk. Long-horizon uncertainty on Iranian residual capabilities and US political will remains.  
- Main gating factor: Resolution or durable management of the core political disputes.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Formal or de-facto agreement that lifts blockade elements and produces multi-month calm.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Prolonged low-level denial campaign or new nuclear-related crisis.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-Low  

**18 Months**  
- Probability: 62%  
- Base-rate anchor: ~60–65%  
- Current-evidence adjustment: Slightly upward (historical tendency for commercial systems to adapt even without full political resolution).  
- Core rationale: By this horizon, either a settlement, regime fatigue, or structural adaptation (pipelines, insurance markets, escort regimes) is more likely to have produced functional normality under the definition. Still far from certain.  
- Main gating factor: Absence of a new major escalation cycle.  
- What would raise this forecast materially: Clear multi-quarter trend of declining incidents and premiums.  
- What would lower this forecast materially: Evidence of Iranian reconstitution of denial capabilities or renewed large-scale war.  
- Confidence in this estimate: Medium-Low  

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued managed but restricted operations through early 2027, with intermittent attacks, elevated insurance, and partial oil flows sustained by dark shipping, bypasses, and US coordination. A limited or fragile diplomatic arrangement is possible after the midterms, but full multi-domain normality (especially insurance and force posture) is more likely in the 6–18 month window than earlier. Physical export continuity improves faster than perceived security and insurance.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
Post-midterm deal or durable de-escalation within weeks; attack frequency collapses; insurers re-enter aggressively within 4–8 weeks; US begins visible drawdown. Probability of functional normality by 3 months rises to ~40–45% under this scenario. Load-bearing assumption: both sides prioritize economic relief over maximalist demands.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Post-midterm strikes restart a higher-intensity phase; Iran maintains low-cost denial; proxies keep secondary pressure; insurance remains elevated for 12+ months. Probability of functional normality stays below 30% even at 12 months. Load-bearing assumption: residual Iranian coastal and proxy capabilities plus unresolved nuclear/sanctions issues sustain the disruption.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that this forecast underestimates speed: Physical oil flows have already demonstrated surprising resilience; a political pause could produce faster commercial re-entry than insurance history suggests if the US backstop expands and attack rates drop sharply. Strongest case that it overestimates speed: Insurance markets and shipowner risk tolerance are stickier than military pauses; eight months of established disruption plus proxy activity and post-midterm optionality make early normalization harder than reference classes imply. Load-bearing assumptions include the continued efficacy of US escort/coordination and the absence of a major new escalation trigger after 3 November.
