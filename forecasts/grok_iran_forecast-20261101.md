**1. Forecast Question**  
Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**  
Functional normality requires simultaneous, sustained satisfaction of the following observable conditions for at least 30 consecutive days (or 60 days for insurance and transit metrics where specified). It is not a temporary ceasefire, a price dip, or a pause in major strikes:

- Direct military activity: No kinetic strikes by the US, Israel, or Iran on each other’s territory, forces, or assets.  
- Proxy activity: Houthi, Iraqi militia, and other proxy attacks on shipping, energy infrastructure, or bases at or below pre-February 2026 baseline frequency and severity.  
- Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and adjacent waters): No attacks, mines, or harassment for 60 days; free transit without mandatory Iranian permits, routine US escorts, or systematic AIS-dark operations as the commercial standard.  
- Commercial transit levels: Total (AIS + estimated dark) oil, product, LNG, and container volumes ≥90% of 2025 pre-war daily averages through Hormuz and Gulf terminals.  
- War-risk insurance: Additional premiums ≤1% of hull value (roughly 4× pre-war ~0.25%) and available on standard commercial terms without primary reliance on special government facilities.  
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf exports (including pipeline bypasses) at ≥95% of pre-war capacity with no widespread force-majeure declarations.  
- Energy market stress: Brent crude and related volatility driven by non-crisis fundamentals rather than sustained war-risk premia (no multi-month levels >$90 solely from Gulf disruption risk).  
- Force posture: US blockade and escort operations reduced to routine presence; Iranian naval/IRGC posture non-aggressive and without formal exclusion zones or Strait Authority control mechanisms.  
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new nuclear breakout moves, regime-targeting campaigns, or major proxy escalations that reintroduce systemic risk.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**  
- **Tanker War (1984–1988)**: Multi-year campaign of attacks on shipping during the Iran-Iraq War. Traffic continued but at reduced volumes; full commercial normalization required the broader ceasefire. Relevant because of Iranian maritime disruption capability and US escorts. Breaks down: different political end-state and no nuclear dimension. Suggests 12–36+ months for functional recovery once hostilities end.  
- **Houthi Red Sea disruption (2023–present)**: Persistent threat capability produced multi-year elevated insurance, rerouting, and incomplete volume recovery even during lulls. Relevant for insurance lag and commercial risk aversion. Breaks down: lower volume stakes and different geography. Suggests 12–24 months for partial normalization after sustained quiet.  
- **2019 Gulf/Hormuz incidents**: Brief attacks produced short insurance and price spikes that normalized within weeks once the immediate threat subsided. Relevant for rapid market response to discrete events. Breaks down: no sustained campaign or dual blockade dynamic. Suggests weeks to low months.  
- **Post-ceasefire phases of limited regional conflicts (e.g., various Gulf skirmishes)**: Insurance and transit often lag military pauses by 1–3 months. Relevant for second-order commercial effects.  

**Base-rate prior (before case-specific evidence)**:  
1 week: ~5%; 1 month: ~10%; 3 months: ~20%; 6 months: ~35%; 1 year: ~50%; 18 months: ~60%. These reflect the historical difficulty of simultaneous multi-domain recovery when threat capability and political incentives for disruption persist.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**  
- Mutual economic pain (Iranian export blockade + elevated global energy costs) creating incentives for de-escalation.  
- Active mediation and some shared interest in avoiding full collapse of flows.  
- Demonstrated ability to move significant volumes under protection, reducing absolute scarcity.  
- Historical pattern that insurance and transit recover once a credible multi-month quiet period is established.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**  
- Persistent Iranian capability and demonstrated willingness to impose costs on shipping to retain leverage.  
- High probability of renewed US/Israeli kinetic action after midterms if talks stall.  
- Insurance market lag and risk aversion (requires sustained quiet far longer than a ceasefire).  
- Unresolved core issues (nuclear, Strait control, sanctions) that make temporary pauses fragile.  
- Proxy residual capacity and second-order effects on Gulf state posture and buyer confidence.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts** (probabilities of each component being in place by horizon)

**10. Most Likely Path**  
Continued low-to-moderate intensity maritime pressure and intermittent kinetic exchanges through at least early 2027, with partial volume recovery under protection but persistently elevated insurance and non-normal operating practices. A durable political settlement remains possible but not the base case within 12 months.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**  
A post-midterm agreement that sequences blockade relief, limited nuclear steps, and verified Strait free-passage, followed by 60+ days of zero attacks. Insurance begins falling within weeks of clean data; volumes and posture normalize by 3–6 months. Probability of this path: ~15–20%.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**  
Renewed high-intensity campaign after midterms, Iranian retention of maritime disruption tools, and proxy activation. Insurance stays elevated, volumes remain dependent on special measures, and commercial normality is deferred beyond 18 months. Probability of this path: ~30–35%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**  
Strongest case that probabilities are too low: Economic pain and mediation could produce a faster political deal than historical reference classes suggest, and partial volume recovery already shows adaptation. Strongest case that probabilities are too high: Iranian strategic incentives to retain Strait leverage, US political dynamics, and the demonstrated multi-month lag between military quiet and insurance/commercial normalization. Load-bearing assumptions include the durability of any post-midterm pause and the continued relevance of the Tanker War/Houthi insurance lag. Evidence that would falsify the forecast: a verified 60-day attack-free period with insurance falling below 2% and free transit becoming the norm, or the opposite (major new campaign).
