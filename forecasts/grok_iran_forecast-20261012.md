**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months. (Current date baseline: ~12 October 2026, ~Day 227 of the Hormuz crisis.)

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Sustained conditions (≥30 consecutive days) meeting *all* of the following observable criteria simultaneously:

- **Direct military activity**: Major cross-border strikes (US/Israeli on Iranian territory/assets or Iranian on US/Israeli/Gulf targets) reduced to sporadic/isolated incidents (<1 significant event per week average). No active large-scale air/missile campaigns.
- **Proxy activity**: Sustained restraint by Iran-aligned proxies (especially Houthis) — no major attacks on Gulf shipping, energy infrastructure, or host-state targets.
- **Shipping security (Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters)**: No successful kinetic attacks on commercial vessels for the period; residual mine threat mitigated (cleared, marked, or operationally irrelevant under escort/coordination).
- **Commercial transit levels**: AIS-visible commercial traffic (cargo/tankers ≥80 m) ≥70% of pre-crisis baseline (~60 ships/day vs. ~85 pre-crisis).
- **War-risk insurance**: Premiums ≤3× peacetime baseline (~0.3% of hull value or lower), with broad market capacity and no widespread cancellations or special restrictions for the zone.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Aggregate Gulf exports (crude + products + LNG/LPG, including any resumed Iranian flows) ≥85% of pre-war volumes, primarily via normal maritime routes rather than emergency pipelines/STS workarounds. Refined products and LNG recovering toward baseline.
- **Energy market stress**: Brent not primarily driven by acute Hormuz risk premium; implied volatility and crack spreads normalized relative to pre-crisis ranges (not absolute price levels).
- **Force posture**: Active US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted or suspended; major naval concentrations drawn down from peak confrontation levels; no persistent high-intensity escort/blockade posture.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No fresh nuclear breakout signals, large-scale proxy activation, regime-collapse dynamics forcing re-escalation, or equivalent shocks.

Temporary ceasefires, price declines driven by global demand/supply elsewhere, or pure military pauses do *not* satisfy the definition. Commercial and insurance normalization are gating.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot** (as of ~12 October 2026)

- War ongoing since 28 February 2026. Hormuz AIS commercial traffic ~8–9 ships/day (~10% of ~85 pre-crisis). Status: severely restricted. Ships transit under US naval coordination; Iran claims control, requires permits via Persian Gulf Strait Authority, and has conducted recent spikes of attacks (12+ tankers in one week ending early October). Mines reported.
- US naval blockade of Iranian ports active (reimposed ~mid-July); Iranian crude exports ~zero.
- Gulf oil exports (ex-Iran): recovered to ~80–100% of pre-war levels via Saudi/UAE pipelines (~40% bypassing Hormuz), ship-to-ship transfers, night/escorted transits. Crude near pre-war; refined products and LNG lag. Total regional flows ~16.5–19.2 mbpd vs. higher pre-war baselines. Brent ~$100–105.
- War-risk insurance: elevated (reports of ~15× peacetime / 1.5% hull, or higher peaks of 5–10%).
- Diplomacy: Stalled. Iran’s seven conditions for reopening (end blockade, sanctions relief, frozen assets, etc.) met with US response deemed “insufficient.” Trump publicly rules out strikes before 3 November midterms. Third US carrier + ~9–10k additional troops deploying; two carriers already present.
- Proxies: Houthis active against Saudi targets and Bab el-Mandeb contested.
- Confirmed facts (AIS, Kpler/Vortexa, UKMTO, official statements) vs. claims (Iranian control narratives, attack attributions) are distinguished below. Evidence is reasonably dense on traffic and attacks; thinner on precise insurance quotes and private diplomatic content.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant classes:
- **1980s Tanker War (Iran-Iraq, ~1984–1988)**: Closest analog — sustained anti-shipping in Gulf/Hormuz, US escorts, mines, oil adaptation via price/insurance offsets. Shipping continued (disruption limited in aggregate volume); full normalization only after ceasefire ending the broader war. Suggests multi-year horizon for functional normality if political core unresolved. Breaks down: Iran now faces direct US/Israeli air campaign and blockade; modern sensors/mines/insurance markets differ.
- **Red Sea/Houthi campaign (2023–present)**: Prolonged chokepoint disruption, insurance elevation, partial rerouting, slow traffic recovery even after attack pauses (Suez still well below baseline months later). Suggests 12–24+ months for partial commercial normalization. Breaks down: Hormuz is narrower/more critical and involves state vs. non-state actor.
- **2019 Gulf tanker attacks**: Short spikes, insurance rises, rapid de-escalation and relative normalization within weeks–months once kinetic activity ceased. Suggests faster recovery if attacks stop. Breaks down: Current conflict is sustained multi-month war with blockade and mutual claims of control.
- **Ukraine Black Sea/grain corridor (2022–)**: Intermittent agreements, elevated insurance, partial recovery under security guarantees. Suggests months of managed but incomplete normality. Breaks down: Different geography and parties.
- **Historical Suez/Red Sea post-conflict recoveries**: Often 6–18 months for traffic and insurance to approach baseline after hostilities end.

**Base-rate prior** (before case-specific updating; rough central estimates for full functional normality by horizon):
- 1 week: ~2%
- 1 month: ~5%
- 3 months: ~12%
- 6 months: ~25%
- 1 year: ~40%
- 18 months: ~55%

These reflect slow commercial/insurance lags even after kinetic pauses, and the rarity of rapid full normalization when core political/military disputes remain.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)
1. Mutual interest in avoiding uncontrolled oil-price spikes and US midterm political costs (near-term restraint).
2. Demonstrated adaptation (pipelines, STS, escorted night transits) already restoring most non-Iranian crude volumes.
3. Existing mediation channels (Qatar, Oman, Pakistan) and residual interest in phased deals.
4. Iranian economic pressure (blockade, inflation) creating long-term incentives for relief.
5. US naval superiority and mine-clearance capacity enabling managed traffic if political conditions allow.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)
1. Unresolved core disputes (blockade, sanctions/frozen assets, Iranian control claims over Hormuz, nuclear file) — gating for sustained commercial confidence.
2. Ongoing kinetic shipping attacks and mine threats — directly suppress traffic and keep insurance elevated.
3. Force posture (US carrier buildup, blockade) and Iranian IRGC capacity for low-cost disruption.
4. Proxy activity (Houthis) and risk of broader regional spillover.
5. Precedent of collapsed June arrangements; mutual distrust and domestic political constraints (US midterms, Iranian hardline elements).
6. Insurance and commercial behavior lag: shipowners and underwriters require sustained security proof before volume and pricing normalize.

Military pauses or crude-volume adaptation alone are insufficient. Probabilities are updated upward modestly from base rates for demonstrated adaptation and near-term political restraint, but downward for the stalled diplomacy, active attacks, and historical lag in commercial normalization. Long-horizon figures remain below 80% because the core political settlement is unresolved and insurance/traffic require prolonged proof.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Protracted managed disruption through late 2026/early 2027: intermittent attack reductions, partial traffic under US coordination and pipelines/STS, elevated insurance, no full blockade lift. Post-midterm diplomacy produces limited phased arrangements that stabilize crude flows further but leave refined products, insurance, and residual threats imperfect. Functional normality (per definition) most probable in the 12–18 month window rather than sooner, via de facto coexistence more than comprehensive deal.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Post-midterm deal meets enough Iranian conditions for blockade suspension + Iranian attack halt within weeks; US escorts and mine mitigation enable rapid traffic rebound; insurers reprice downward within 30–60 days on evidence of calm; proxies restrained. Traffic and insurance hit thresholds by 3–6 months. Probability mass shifted left if this materializes (currently lower-probability given “insufficient” response and buildup).

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Talks remain deadlocked; post-midterm strikes resume; IRGC maintains low-cost shipping harassment and mines; insurance stays elevated; proxies intensify; traffic remains <30–40% with high variance. Functional normality delayed beyond 18 months or requires major regime/political change. Consistent with 1980s Tanker War duration and Red Sea lag.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Strongest case I overestimate normalization: Commercial actors (owners, insurers, charterers) require longer proof periods than military analysts assume; residual mine and IRGC asymmetric capacity can suppress traffic indefinitely at low cost; nuclear/proxy issues remain unresolved spoilers. Strongest case I underestimate: Adaptation is already more advanced than traffic numbers suggest (dark shipping, pipelines); mutual pain from $100+ oil and Iranian economic stress forces a durable understanding faster than reference classes imply; US naval dominance can impose sufficient security for insurance normalization even without full political settlement.

Load-bearing assumptions: (1) Insurance and traffic are true gating factors, not trailing indicators of military calm; (2) core political disputes will not be resolved quickly; (3) no black-swan regime change or external mediation breakthrough.

What would prove the view wrong: Sustained 60+ day attack-free period with traffic >60 ships/day and insurance ≤3× by early 2027 (faster than expected), or continued <20% traffic and active attacks into mid-2027 (slower).
