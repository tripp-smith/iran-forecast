**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):<grok:render card_id="d8c246" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">0</argument></grok:render><grok:render card_id="578d1f" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">1</argument></grok:render>

Functional normality means **all** of the following sustained for at least 30 consecutive days without major reversal:
- **Direct military activity**: No strikes/exchanges between US/Israel and Iran (or their forces); IRGC/USN/CENTCOM posture reverts toward pre-Feb 2026 levels.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant new Houthi/Hezbollah/PMF attacks tied to the Iran theater; restraint holds.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: No attacks, mining, or credible threats; US “Project Freedom” escorts unnecessary; AIS/transit data shows routine safe passage.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Vessel traffic through Hormuz returns to ≥80% of 2025 pre-war baseline (~20-21 million bpd oil equivalent + LNG).
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums fall to low single-digit add-ons (not 10x+ war rates); underwriters remove “war zone” exclusions or high deductibles.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian, Saudi, UAE, Iraqi, Qatari exports flow at near-capacity without forced curtailments; no major stranded tankers or force majeure declarations.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI within ~15% of pre-war trends with normal backwardation/contango; no sustained >$20-30/bbl risk premium from Gulf disruption.
- **Force posture of major actors**: USN drawdown from blockade/escort levels; Iranian naval/IRGC forces not on high alert for closure; Gulf states resume normal operations.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakout moves, leadership decapitation follow-ons, or proxy surges; credible diplomatic track (e.g., Hormuz guarantees + sanctions relief path) active.

A temporary pause, lowered (but still elevated) oil prices, or battlefield lull does **not** qualify. Commercial/insurance behavior is the decisive lagging indicator.<grok:render card_id="31ff1f" card_type="citation_card" type="render_inline_citation"><argument name="citation_id">16</argument></grok:render>

### Most Likely Path
Prolonged coercive negotiations with intermittent escort incidents; partial traffic recovery by 3-6 months if no major clash; fuller (but imperfect) normalization by 12-18 months via messy compromise on Hormuz guarantees + limited sanctions relief. Persistent elevated risk premium.

### Faster Normalization Case (~20-30% odds overall)
Quick mutual concessions (Iran lifts restrictions for blockade end + limited nuclear freeze); successful escorts build confidence fast; Gulf states mediate. Traffic/insurance rebound by 3 months. Strongest evidence: Iranian storage crisis forcing deal.

### Prolonged Instability Case
Stalemate persists; proxies activated; minor clashes during escorts; insurance stays punitive. Traffic <50% normal for 12+ months. Strongest evidence: Iranian “never pre-war” statements + reconstitution.
