**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain): 

Functional normality means the Persian Gulf operates as a low-risk commercial chokepoint with minimal disruption to global energy and trade flows. Observable criteria (all must be substantially met for "return"):

- **Direct military activity**: No strikes, seizures, or exchanges of fire between US/Israel/Iran or their forces in/near the Gulf for ≥30 consecutive days.
- **Proxy activity**: No significant attacks (e.g., Houthi/Hezbollah/PMF) on shipping, Gulf infrastructure, or US/Israeli assets attributable to Iran command.
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Safe commercial transit without routine threats, mines, or IRGC interference; daily transits ≥50-70% of pre-war baseline (~60-100+ vessels/day bidirectional).
- **Commercial transit levels**: Oil/LNG/product tanker flows restored to ≥70-80% of normal (~15-20 mb/d equivalent throughput); minimal stranded vessels.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums back to low single-digit multiples of pre-war (e.g., <0.5% Additional War Risk Premium per voyage vs. current elevated 0.8-2.5%+); broad private coverage available without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Qatar, Kuwait, Iran) exporting near pre-war volumes via Hormuz without major force majeure or discounts; no systemic storage overflows or production shut-ins >2-3 mb/d.
- **Energy market stress/volatility**: Brent/WTI spreads and implied volatility normalized (no sustained >$100-110/bbl risk premium tied to Gulf); Asian/European buyers resuming routine contracting.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval presence drawdown to routine levels; Iran IRGC Navy not actively enforcing "sovereignty" tolls or closures; Gulf states de-escalating alerts.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No unresolved nuclear/HEU disputes, leadership vacuums, or proxy flare-ups driving immediate risk.

A temporary ceasefire or lower prices alone does **not** count.
