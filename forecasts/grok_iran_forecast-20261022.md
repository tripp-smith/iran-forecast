**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each of these horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 18 months.

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

“Return to functional normality” requires sustained, multi-domain operational conditions meeting all of the following for a continuous period of at least two weeks (to distinguish from temporary pauses):

- **Direct military activity**: No confirmed major strikes (missiles, drones, mines, or other projectiles causing significant damage or casualties) by Iran, the IRGC, the US, Israel, or regional partners in the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, or immediate approaches. Minor harassment or unconfirmed claims do not count as major.
- **Proxy activity**: No major escalatory attacks by Iran-backed proxies (notably Houthis) on Gulf shipping, energy infrastructure, or territory of Gulf states that force significant diversions or military responses.
- **Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Sustained commercial transit without requirement for US or other heavy military escorts/convoys as the default mode; threat level assessed as low-to-moderate by UKMTO/JMIC or equivalent; no systematic mine threat or coordinated attacks.
- **Commercial transit levels**: AIS-visible and estimated total (including dark) commercial vessel transits averaging at least 70-80% of pre-crisis baseline (~85/day AIS-visible or equivalent total volume) for the period, with dry bulk, container, and tanker traffic recovering in parallel.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Additional war-risk premiums for a typical Hormuz transit at or below ~1% of hull value (roughly 4-6x peacetime baseline of ~0.15-0.25%), with capacity readily available and no widespread club cancellations or 72-hour notices.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Combined Middle East oil (crude + products) flows through Hormuz and immediate alternatives at or above ~85-90% of pre-war ~20-21 mb/d levels, with Iranian exports no longer fully blockaded, minimal reliance on large-scale dark STS shuttling or emergency stock releases, and LNG (primarily Qatari) flowing without major disruption.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent crude not systematically elevated above $90-95 solely due to Gulf risk premium (distinguishing from other fundamentals); implied volatility and freight rates returning toward pre-war ranges.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US carrier and major surface combatant presence in the Arabian Sea/Gulf of Oman reduced from wartime surge levels; Iranian coastal and IRGC naval forces not in active blockade or attack posture; no large-scale forward deployment by other actors.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakout indicators, no large-scale resumption of US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory or nuclear sites, no collapse of mediation channels, and no new proxy theater openings that re-escalate Gulf risk.

A temporary ceasefire, lower oil prices driven by demand destruction or inventory, or a battlefield pause without commercial and insurance normalization does **not** meet the standard.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot** (as of 22 October 2026, ~day 237 of Hormuz crisis; war began ~28 February 2026)

The Strait of Hormuz remains severely restricted. AIS-visible commercial transits are a small fraction of the pre-crisis ~85/day baseline (recent days often single digits to low teens; 7-day averages cited around 3-10). US-facilitated and dark/STS activity has allowed partial oil flow recovery (Kpler and others report Hormuz-area flows in the 9-14 mb/d range in recent weeks, ~60-80% of pre-war crude baselines depending on exact measure and inclusion of STS/bypasses; overall Middle East flows sometimes near or above pre-war when counting Red Sea/Yanbu and pipelines). Pre-war total oil through Hormuz was ~20-21 mb/d. Iranian oil terminals have been largely idle under the US blockade reimposed after the July breakdown of the June MoU. Iranian attacks on shipping have intensified in October (UKMTO and others record 12+ incidents in recent weeks; IRGC claims mines and strikes on unauthorized or dark vessels; at least two ships sunk or lost in recent incidents; some engine-room hits reported). War-risk premiums are elevated at 6-10% of hull value (or 15-32x peacetime in various estimates), rendering many voyages uneconomic. Brent is trading around $110-112. Indirect talks continue via Pakistan and Qatar; Iran insists Hormuz will not reopen to all until its terms (including recognition of control/authority and related demands) are met and has offered limited proposals; the US has stated it will not accept a deal giving Iran a nuclear path and has signaled possible post-midterm (3 November) strike options. Force postures remain high (carriers in the region). Houthis have been active against Saudi-related targets. Nuclear issue is unresolved: ~440 kg of 60% enriched uranium pre-war, significant damage to facilities but stockpile status and remaining capability unverified, no meaningful IAEA access to bombed sites, Iran rejects zero enrichment and removal of material. Confirmed facts are distinguished from IRGC claims (many unverified) and US statements. Sources include UKMTO/JMIC reporting, Kpler/Vortexa/Windward tracking, Reuters/Bloomberg/Al Jazeera/CNN/ISW/JINSA, Iranian state media, and insurer commentary.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant historical cases:

- **Tanker War (1984-1988, Iran-Iraq)**: Prolonged maritime attacks (Iraq 283, Iran 168) with mines, missiles, and speedboats; US escort operations (Earnest Will); traffic continued at reduced but functional levels rather than full closure. Ended with overall land-war ceasefire (UNSCR 598). Relevance: same geography and Iranian maritime tactics. Breakdown: did not involve full US-Iran war or nuclear stakes; attacks did not produce total commercial shutdown. Suggests multi-year maritime risk with partial functionality possible under escorts, full normalization only after political settlement (months to years post-ceasefire).
- **Red Sea/Houthi disruptions (2023-2025, with 2026 flare)**: Sustained attacks forced major diversions (Suez traffic down 50-60%+ for extended periods); insurance premiums spiked to 0.7-1%+ and remained elevated even after attack pauses or declared ends; full commercial recovery lagged. Relevance: proxy maritime chokepoint warfare with insurance lag. Breakdown: different geography and lower stakes than Hormuz. Suggests 6-18+ months of elevated risk and incomplete traffic recovery even after kinetic pauses.
- **Other Gulf/Hormuz tension episodes (e.g., 2019 tanker attacks, 2020s drone/missile exchanges)**: Short spikes in risk and premiums (weeks to months) followed by de-escalation when political incentives aligned. Relevance: insurance and shipper behavior. Breakdown: far lower intensity and duration than 2026. Suggests rapid partial recovery if kinetic activity stops, but commercial confidence lags weeks.
- **Post-conflict maritime recovery more broadly (e.g., after major naval or mine threats)**: Insurance and traffic typically require weeks to months of verified safety after military pause before premiums and volumes normalize. Relevance: second-order commercial effects. Breakdown: assumes underlying political settlement.

**Base-rate prior** (outside-in, before current evidence): Probability of functional normality by horizon roughly 5% (1 week), 15% (1 month), 30% (3 months), 45% (6 months), 60% (1 year), 70% (18 months). These reflect that full multi-domain commercial normalization in chokepoint wars is slow and gated by political settlement more than battlefield pause; pure kinetic pauses produce only partial recovery.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**

- Ongoing mediation (Pakistan, Qatar) and Iranian limited proposals create a diplomatic channel.
- Partial oil flow recovery demonstrates that volume can be restored under protection, reducing absolute economic pressure somewhat.
- US domestic calendar (midterms 3 Nov) has produced a temporary kinetic pause on US strikes.
- Mutual exhaustion and economic costs on both sides create incentives for de-escalation over longer horizons.
- Historical pattern that pure maritime pressure campaigns eventually yield to political deals when costs rise.

Ranked by importance: diplomatic channel existence > demonstrated flow recovery > calendar-driven pause > economic incentives.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**

- Core unresolved issues: Iranian demand for Hormuz authority/control recognition and nuclear enrichment rights vs US zero/meaningful reduction demands.
- Active Iranian maritime campaign (attacks, mines) that raises insurance and deters traffic independently of formal ceasefire.
- US blockade and Iranian export cutoff create asymmetric leverage and Iranian incentive to keep pressure high.
- Elevated force postures and post-midterm strike optionality.
- Proxy activity and risk of further Houthi or other escalation.
- Insurance and shipper confidence lag (second- and third-order effects) that persist after kinetic pauses.
- Nuclear stockpile uncertainty as a standing escalation trigger.

Ranked by importance: unresolved political/nuclear demands > ongoing maritime attacks and insurance lag > blockade dynamics > force posture and proxy risk.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Continued contested maritime environment through early 2027, with intermittent attack spikes, high insurance, partial oil flows via workarounds, and mediation continuing without breakthrough. Partial volume recovery continues, but full multi-domain normality does not occur until a political arrangement (or clear exhaustion) reduces the attack incentive and allows underwriters to reprice. Post-midterm US options and Iranian nuclear leverage remain standing risks.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

A post-midterm deal that trades limited, verifiable nuclear steps and free Hormuz transit (no Iranian toll/control recognition) for sanctions relief and blockade lift, followed by 4-6 weeks of verified low incidents. Insurance collapses and traffic recovers faster than historical averages. Probability of this path is low but non-zero if both sides prioritize economic relief.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Iran maintains attack tempo to keep prices high and leverage, US maintains blockade and post-election strike optionality, nuclear issue remains frozen, proxies stay active. Traffic and insurance stay impaired for 12-18+ months; oil flows rely on permanent workarounds. This is consistent with the lower end of reference classes.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Strongest case I am overestimating normalization: Iranian control demands and nuclear stockpile are more zero-sum than I weight; insurance markets may treat any residual IRGC capability as permanently elevated risk; US domestic politics could sustain pressure longer. Strongest case I am underestimating: Economic pain on Iran (blockade) and global oil consumers could force faster compromise than historical cases; demonstrated ability to move oil under protection shows commercial recovery can be rapid once attacks stop. Load-bearing assumptions: that attacks can be dialed down independently of nuclear deal, and that underwriters will reprice within months of verified safety. Evidence that would prove the view wrong: sustained multi-week attack cessation with premiums below 2% and traffic above 60% of baseline by early 2027 (upside), or nuclear breakout signals plus attack surge (downside).
