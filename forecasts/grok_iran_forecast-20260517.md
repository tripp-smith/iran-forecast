**Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”** (measurable, observable, multi-domain):

Functional normality requires **all** of the following to hold simultaneously and sustainably (not a temporary dip or pause):

- **Direct military activity**: No US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory or forces; no Iranian missile/drone strikes on Gulf states, Israel, or US assets exceeding sporadic low-level incidents.
- **Proxy activity**: Sustained restraint by Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi PMF, etc., with no major attacks on shipping, oil infrastructure, or allies (minor incidents possible but not escalating).
- **Shipping security in Strait of Hormuz/nearby waters**: Open transit without routine Iranian tolls/inspections/seizures or US blockade enforcement; mine clearance complete; US Navy escorts unnecessary for most traffic.
- **Commercial transit levels**: ≥70-80% of pre-war daily averages (~100-140 vessels/day through Hormuz); no widespread loitering or rerouting via overland pipelines.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Premiums return to near pre-war levels (e.g., <0.5% of hull value vs. current multi-percent spikes); full private market capacity restored without government backstops.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Iranian, Saudi, UAE, Qatari, etc., exports at ≥90% pre-war capacity; no force majeure declarations; reservoirs and infrastructure undamaged or quickly repaired.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent/WTI volatility index normalized; prices within ~15-20% of pre-war baselines without war premium; no sustained shortages or Asian/European buyer distress rerouting.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval presence reduced to routine; Iranian IRGC Navy not dominating chokepoint claims; Gulf states resuming normal operations.
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No nuclear breakthroughs, leadership assassinations, or proxy escalations that could reignite direct conflict.

A ceasefire or lower oil prices alone does **not** qualify.
