**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by the horizons: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from 22 August 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

Functional normality requires *simultaneous* satisfaction of all the following observable conditions for a sustained period (not a single-day spike or temporary pause):

- Direct military activity: No sustained US/Iranian or Iranian/Gulf-state kinetic exchanges targeting military sites, energy infrastructure, or commercial shipping in the Gulf/Hormuz approaches. Isolated residual incidents do not disqualify if they do not cascade.
- Proxy activity: No significant Houthi or other Iranian-aligned proxy attacks on Gulf shipping or energy infrastructure that materially affect transit.
- Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters: Commercial vessels transit without routine kinetic threat, boarding, seizure, or forced turn-backs; no active mining or credible mine threat requiring continuous clearance operations.
- Commercial transit levels: Daily AIS-visible (or equivalent tracked) commodity vessel transits sustainably ≥70–80% of pre-war baseline (~90–110+ of ~120–130 vessels/day), including meaningful volumes of non-Iranian/Chinese-linked VLCCs and LNG carriers on open routes.
- War-risk insurance conditions: Hull war-risk premiums sustainably ≤1–1.5% of hull value (vs pre-war ~0.25% and current elevated multiples of 7.5–40×); cover widely available without capacity withdrawal or voyage-by-voyage extreme pricing that prices most operators out.
- Oil and LNG export continuity: Gulf producers (Saudi, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Iraq) restore export volumes through Hormuz to ≥80–85% of pre-war levels without chronic backlog, dark sailing dominance, or permanent rerouting; Iranian exports no longer the primary residual flow under blockade constraints.
- Energy market stress and volatility: Brent risk premium attributable to Hormuz normalized (prices not structurally elevated solely by chokepoint risk); no repeated multi-dollar spikes on single transit incidents.
- Force posture of major actors: Material drawdown or stabilization of heightened US naval/air presence and Iranian coastal anti-shipping posture; no active US naval blockade of Iranian ports or Iranian assertion of exclusive administrative control requiring permits/tolls.
- Absence of major new escalation triggers: No new MoU collapse, major infrastructure strike, seizure campaign, or third-party (e.g., Houthi expansion) event that resets commercial confidence.

A temporary ceasefire, lower oil prices from strategic releases, or partial recovery under military escort does **not** meet the definition. Commercial normalization lags military pauses by weeks to months due to insurer, owner, and charterer risk aversion.

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant classes (with breakdowns):

1. **Tanker War phase of Iran-Iraq War (1984–1988)**: Prolonged low-intensity attacks on shipping; full commercial recovery only after ceasefire + sustained security. Suggests 12–24+ months post-major de-escalation for insurance/transit normalization. Breaks down: no modern precision drones/missiles, weaker global insurance markets, different US posture.
2. **2019 Gulf tanker attacks / IRGC seizures + Abqaiq**: Short spike; rapid partial recovery under heightened escort but elevated premiums lingered months. Suggests weeks–3 months for partial transit recovery if kinetic stops cleanly. Breaks down: far lower intensity, no full war or blockade.
3. **Houthi Red Sea campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Sustained disruption; insurance and routing adaptation persistent despite pauses. Suggests multi-month lag even after kinetic reduction; full normality hard without credible deterrence. Breaks down: longer alternative routes available; less central to global oil.
4. **1980s–1990s Hormuz closures/threats and post-1991 Gulf War**: Traffic recovered within months of clear military dominance, but war-risk and owner caution lasted longer. Suggests 3–9 months for commercial metrics after decisive security.
5. **Modern chokepoint disruptions (Suez 2021, Black Sea grain post-2022)**: Physical reopening ≠ commercial normality; insurance and confidence lag 1–6+ months.
6. **Broader post-major Middle East wars (e.g., post-2003 Iraq maritime aspects)**: Force posture and proxy residual risk slow full normalization to 12–18+ months.

**Base-rate prior (before case-specific evidence)**:  
1 week: ~1–3%  
1 month: ~5–10%  
3 months: ~15–25%  
6 months: ~30–40%  
1 year: ~45–60%

These reflect that military pauses are more common than full multi-domain commercial recovery within short horizons; long-horizon rates remain below 80% due to structural Iranian leverage incentives and insurance lag.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization** (ranked)

1. Mutual exhaustion + economic pressure (Iran inflation/sanctions; US domestic/energy costs) producing a durable deal that includes credible Hormuz security guarantees.  
2. Sustained US (or coalition) naval dominance that raises Iranian attack costs enough for commercial confidence without permanent blockade.  
3. Insurer capacity return and premium compression after 4–8+ weeks of zero kinetic incidents.  
4. Gulf state mediation (Oman) producing workable transit protocols without Iranian exclusive control.  
5. Strategic petroleum releases and alternative supply adaptation reducing market urgency.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization** (ranked)

1. Iranian strategic preference for Hormuz leverage as residual deterrent and bargaining chip post-war damage.  
2. Insurance market structural caution and capacity limits—premiums and availability lag kinetic reality by months.  
3. Tit-for-tat escalation traps and MoU fragility demonstrated in June–July.  
4. Proxy residual capacity (Houthis) and dark-fleet dynamics that keep risk elevated.  
5. Domestic politics on both sides (US election-cycle optics; Iranian hardliner/IRGC influence) that punish concessions.  
6. Physical residual threats (mines, coastal missiles/drones) requiring sustained clearance and deterrence.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Stalemate persists through autumn 2026 with sporadic incidents, single-digit to low-double-digit traffic, and elevated premiums. Economic pressure produces another limited understanding in late 2026/early 2027 that allows gradual traffic recovery under escorts or protocols. Insurance lags by 1–3 months. Full functional normality (all domains) arrives, if at all, in the 9–18-month window after a more durable arrangement, with residual risk premium higher than 2025 baseline.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Decisive US military degradation of Iranian coastal capabilities + Iranian economic collapse forcing acceptance of open transit without exclusive control. Traffic recovers to 80%+ within 6–8 weeks of a new deal; premiums fall below 2% inside 3 months. Probability of this path: ~15–20%.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Iran prioritizes Hormuz leverage indefinitely; low-level attacks and seizures continue; insurers permanently reprice the Gulf; US maintains blockade or high posture; proxies expand. Traffic stays <30% of baseline for 12+ months. Probability of this path: ~25–30%.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

Overestimation risk: Under-weighting Iranian domestic hardliner strength and the demonstrated June collapse; insurance markets may stay elevated longer than historical analogies due to modern capacity concentration and ESG/owner pressures.  
Underestimation risk: Under-weighting speed of commercial adaptation once kinetic risk falls (tankers moved quickly in prior Gulf crises) and the depth of mutual economic pain.  
Load-bearing assumptions: (1) Iranian regime values Hormuz control highly enough to delay deals; (2) insurers require multi-week clean record; (3) no sudden regime-threatening internal Iranian shock. Evidence that would prove the forecast wrong: sustained >80 transits + premiums <1.5% for 30 consecutive days without a new deal, or a verified comprehensive security agreement producing immediate traffic surge.
