**1. Forecast Question**

Estimate the probability that the Persian Gulf has returned to functional normality following the outbreak of the 2026 Iran War by each horizon: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months (from 26 October 2026).

**2. Operational Definition of “Return to Functional Normality”**

“Return to functional normality” requires concurrent, sustained (at least 30 consecutive days) satisfaction of all of the following observable conditions across military, maritime, commercial, and market domains. Partial or temporary improvements do not count.

- **Direct military activity**: No large-scale US/Israeli strikes on Iranian territory or forces, and no Iranian large-scale missile/drone barrages on US bases, Gulf states, or Israel. Sporadic isolated incidents (<2 per week with no casualties or infrastructure damage) are tolerable only if they do not trigger retaliation cycles.
- **Proxy activity**: Houthi and other Iran-aligned forces conducting no more than residual, low-intensity attacks (≤1 significant incident per week) on Saudi/UAE targets or Bab el-Mandeb shipping. No active maritime embargo declarations.
- **Shipping security in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters**: Commercial vessel attacks ≤1 per week for 30 days; no active mining or credible mine threats in main transit corridors; no Iranian requirement for permits, fees, or “authorized” routes enforced by fire.
- **Commercial transit levels**: Average daily transits (AIS-visible + dark/facilitated) ≥80–100 vessels (roughly 70–80% of pre-war ~120–140 baseline), with the majority broadcasting AIS normally and no large anchorage backlogs outside the strait.
- **War-risk insurance conditions**: Additional war-risk premiums for Gulf/Hormuz tankers ≤1% of hull value (near pre-war ~0.25%), with broad market availability and no reliance on special US/Saudi government backstops or 7-day cancellation clauses dominating pricing.
- **Oil and LNG export continuity**: Combined Gulf (Saudi, UAE, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, and residual Iranian) crude and LNG outflows at ≥85% of pre-war volumes without major reliance on high-cost “dark shuttle,” STS transfers, or pipeline bypasses as the primary mechanism. Iranian seaborne exports restored if sanctions are adjusted, or stably zero if under durable agreement.
- **Energy market stress and volatility**: Brent crude sustained in a $70–95 range (or equivalent real-term normal) with 30-day realized volatility near historical non-crisis levels; no persistent $20+ geopolitical risk premium.
- **Force posture of major actors**: US naval blockade of Iranian ports lifted or reduced to routine sanctions enforcement; no dedicated multi-carrier strike groups permanently postured solely for Hormuz/Gulf interdiction; Iranian IRGC naval forces not actively interdicting or declaring routes “illegal.”
- **Absence of major new escalation triggers**: No assassination of senior leadership, strikes on nuclear/missile production infrastructure that restart high-intensity air campaigns, or attacks that destroy major export terminals.

A temporary ceasefire, a battlefield pause, or lower oil prices alone do not meet the standard. Commercial and insurance normalization must be independently verified.

**3. Current Situation Snapshot (as of 26 October 2026, ~Day 241)**

The war began with US-Israeli strikes on 28 February 2026. An April ceasefire and a June Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding both collapsed. Major land/air campaigns have paused (Trump has publicly held off on strikes until after 3 November midterms), but the maritime domain remains active. Iran continues frequent attacks on commercial shipping (October has the highest monthly total since the opening phase—dozens of incidents, including drones, projectiles, and claimed mines). The US maintains a naval blockade of Iranian ports (reimposed mid-July). AIS-visible transit is severely reduced (~8% of pre-war in some trackers); US-facilitated “dark” transits and pipeline bypasses have kept non-Iranian Gulf crude flows closer to pre-war levels at high cost. Hundreds of vessels wait outside the strait. War-risk premiums stand at 6–10% of hull value (up from ~0.25%). Brent is ~$114–117. Diplomacy continues via Pakistan/Qatar mediators, but stalled on nuclear constraints, Hormuz control, and blockade terms. Houthi activity against Saudi targets persists. Force posture remains elevated (additional US carrier movements reported). Confirmed facts are distinguished from IRGC claims (many mine/tanker-hit assertions remain unverified or disputed by US officials).

**4. Reference Classes and Base-Rate Prior**

Relevant cases:

- **Tanker War (1984–1988)**: Sustained attacks on shipping during Iran-Iraq War; traffic continued under US escort (Operation Earnest Will) but with elevated risk and premiums. Full commercial normalization followed the August 1988 ceasefire and political end of hostilities. Suggests multi-year disruption possible while kinetic pressure and political disputes persist; stabilization within months after durable ceasefire.
- **2011–12 Hormuz threats**: Verbal Iranian closure threats and drills; no sustained kinetic closure or major insurance spike. Rapid return to baseline once threats receded. Suggests short timelines when kinetic action is absent.
- **Red Sea/Houthi campaign (2023–ongoing)**: Multi-month to multi-year partial disruption; rerouting, elevated premiums, and incomplete commercial normalization even after some tactical pauses. Suggests commercial adaptation lags military pauses by months.
- **Brief Iran-US tanker incidents (2019) and Soleimani aftermath (2020)**: Short spikes in risk premiums and force posture; return to functional normality within weeks to a few months once direct escalation stopped.
- **Post-ceasefire recovery after limited Gulf conflicts**: Insurance and traffic typically recover over 1–3 months once attacks cease and political signals stabilize, but slower when mines, residual proxy activity, or unresolved political claims remain.

Base-rate prior (before case-specific update): Low near-term probability of full functional normality in an active kinetic maritime campaign with unresolved political demands. Rough prior: 1 week <5%; 1 month ~10%; 3 months ~20–25%; 6 months ~35–40%; 1 year ~50–55%; 18 months ~60–65%. These are then adjusted upward or downward by current evidence.

**6. Key Drivers Toward Normalization**

Ranked: (1) US political calendar and post-midterm deal incentives (Trump statements leave open “nice big beautiful agreement”); (2) Sustained mediation channels and Gulf state interest in de-escalation; (3) Market and shipping adaptation already keeping partial oil flows moving; (4) Mutual interest in avoiding full resumption of high-intensity air campaign; (5) Potential for limited technical arrangements on transit lanes even short of full political settlement.

**7. Key Drivers Against Normalization**

Ranked: (1) Iranian demonstrated capacity and willingness to sustain shipping attacks as leverage (highest October rate); (2) Unresolved core disputes (nuclear constraints, blockade lift, Hormuz control/permits); (3) Elevated war-risk insurance pricing and commercial risk aversion that lag any military pause; (4) Residual proxy (Houthi) activity and mine threats; (5) Force posture and blockade continuity; (6) History of collapsed MOUs and ceasefires in this conflict.

**8. Decomposed Sub-Forecasts**

Approximate probabilities that each component reaches the operational threshold by horizon (not mechanical averages):

- Major reduction in direct strikes: High by 1 month if pause holds (~70%), but fragile thereafter.
- Sustained safe commercial transit through Hormuz: Very low near-term; rises meaningfully only after political signal + 1–3 months of low attacks.
- Meaningful insurance normalization: Lags transit recovery by weeks to months; low until attacks stay near zero.
- Oil/LNG export recovery (functional, not just partial): Partial already; full threshold requires transit + insurance + political clarity.
- Proxy restraint: Medium near-term, dependent on Iranian direction and Saudi responses.
- Force drawdown: Low until political settlement.
- No new major escalation trigger: Medium-high in next 1–2 weeks (election timing); lower thereafter.

Gating factors are shipping security + insurance + sustained low attacks. Partial oil flows and military pauses are insufficient.

**10. Most Likely Path**

Continued elevated maritime pressure and partial oil flows through protected/dark mechanisms through the US midterms and into early 2027. Limited diplomatic progress produces occasional tactical pauses but not durable multi-domain normality until a broader political trade (nuclear constraints vs. blockade/sanctions relief) is reached or one side’s costs become prohibitive. Full functional normality more likely in the 9–18 month window than sooner.

**11. Faster Normalization Case**

Post-election deal that includes Iranian restraint on shipping, US blockade adjustment, and rapid technical demining/transit protocols. Insurance markets reprice within weeks of verified attack cessation. Probability weighted lower because prior MOUs collapsed and core issues remain hard.

**12. Prolonged Instability Case**

Iran sustains intermittent attacks as leverage; US maintains blockade and periodic strikes; proxies remain active; insurance and commercial traffic stay suppressed. Partial oil flows continue at high cost indefinitely. Higher probability than the fastest case given current attack tempo and history.

**13. Red-Team Critique**

The forecast may overestimate near-term normalization by weighting the election pause and mediation more heavily than Iranian demonstrated capacity and the lag between military signals and commercial/insurance recovery. It may underestimate longer-horizon normality if a political settlement proves more durable than prior attempts or if markets fully adapt via bypasses and alternative routes. Load-bearing assumptions are that Iran retains effective maritime interdiction capacity and that commercial actors require sustained low-attack periods before premiums and volumes normalize.
