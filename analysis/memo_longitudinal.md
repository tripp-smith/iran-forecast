# Persian Gulf normality odds fall through mid-November 2026

> **18-month odds: 80% → 62% (−18 pts).** Every horizon ends lower. Spring diplomacy and a mid-year memorandum lifted the curve temporarily; neither stuck. Commercial normality is still gated by settlement quality and insurance—not by the next ceasefire headline.

From 18 March to 15 November 2026, daily Grok superforecasts of a return to **functional normality** in the Persian Gulf after the 2026 Iran War moved lower on every horizon. The 6-month and 1-year odds each fell **17 points** (45%→28% and 65%→48%). Near-term odds stayed near the floor: 1-week 2%→1%; 1-month 10%→4%.

Audience: policymakers, shipping-risk analysts, and energy traders reading the series cold. The operational bar is demanding—military pause alone does not count. Commercial transit, war-risk insurance, export continuity, force posture, and the absence of new escalation triggers must clear together.

## Long horizons absorbed most of the decline

The series is a **downward translation of the whole curve**. The largest absolute declines sit at 6–18 months: the model kept treating the next few weeks as almost closed, while revising down how far a durable commercial reset could get within a year to a year and a half.

Spring lifted odds into late April (still with a formal blockade and deeply depressed transit). The mid-year memorandum window raised medium-horizon odds again in June. By early September those gains had largely reversed. Mid-November leaves medium and long horizons **below** the March starting point.

Late-series day-to-day moves at the 6-month horizon were large (including a −43 point drop from 11 to 12 November). Treat those as **high day-to-day variance** around a lower regime, not as a new structural break on their own.

## Evidence shifted from early-war shock to failed settlement and commercial lag

Across 1,464 parsed evidence rows on 160 dates, the recurring nouns are transit, attacks/strikes, Hormuz, blockade, Houthi activity, oil, and memorandum/MOU language. Kinetic pauses appear; then **insurance, volume, and political terms** fail to clear the bar.

- **March — Opening shock.** Operation Epic Fury; active barrages; commercial and insurance normalization expected to trail any military pause by months.
- **Late April — Passive blockade.** Brief transit uptick around a 28 April meeting; still ~86% below baseline volumes; Ras Laffan / LNG damage a hard constraint in earlier runs.
- **June — Memorandum window.** Islamabad/MOU ceasefire narrative; forecasts briefly price incremental reopening if the memorandum holds.
- **September–November — Fragile pauses.** A June MOU that lapsed without a broader deal; US blockade plus selective facilitation; low-intensity harassment and elevated war-risk pricing as the working baseline.

## Drivers reordered from “war ending soon” to settlement quality and insurance lag

Early toward-drivers emphasize coalition escorts, inventory attrition, and Omani/Qatari diplomatic channels. Against-drivers emphasize proxy cycles, insurer aversion, and mine/coastal reactivation risk.

By late April the toward-list is dominated by **meeting outcomes and short attack-free windows**; the against-list is still the **formal blockade**, collapsed transit volumes, and Iranian “insufficient” framing of talks.

By mid-November, the narrative path is low-intensity attrition into 2027: partial oil at elevated cost, deniable harassment, and commercial catch-up only after a political settlement that removes the incentive to tax the Strait.

## Prolonged instability stays competitive where measured

Structured scenario weights parse cleanly for only the early window (about 45 of 242 days). Where both weights exist, prolonged instability often sits at or above the faster-normalization weight (for example ~33% prolonged vs ~22% faster on 19 March). Later prose continues to treat a messy modus vivendi and a Red Sea–like coercion equilibrium as live branches even when numeric weights are missing. Do not over-read the sparse numeric scenario file as a full time series.

## Insurance and transit bind the aggregate forecast

Where decomposed sub-forecasts parse (most early dates; thinner later), **meaningful insurance normalization** and **sustained safe commercial transit** sit well below “major reduction in direct strikes.” On 18 March, strike reduction at 6 months was already ~65% while insurance was ~35% and high-volume transit ~40%. Later text keeps the same hierarchy even when the table format drifts: a strike pause is necessary and not sufficient; insurers and carriers are the slow variables.

## Two calibration risks remain in the latest view

Relative to the evidence path, the **15 November** forecast (1% / 4% / 12% / 28% / 48% / 62%) looks coherent with eight months of incomplete settlements and depressed transit.

1. **Underestimation of workaround capacity** if dark/escorted flows and partial oil recovery already deliver more system function than AIS-visible “normality” admits.
2. **Overestimation of long-horizon recovery** if intermittent taxation of Hormuz becomes a durable operating system rather than a bridge to a deal.

## Bottom line

Over 242 daily runs from 18 March to 15 November 2026, Persian Gulf functional normality **fell on every horizon**, with the steepest cuts at 6–18 months. Temporary lifts from spring diplomacy and a mid-year memorandum did not stick. The latest path is low-intensity attrition and expensive partial flows—with commercial normality still gated by settlement quality and insurance, not by the next ceasefire headline alone.
