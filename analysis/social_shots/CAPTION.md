## Short post (about X / LinkedIn length)

I ran the same Grok prompt for 248 days on one question. The long horizons are still down from March; last week clawed back some of a mid-November trough.

Since 18 March 2026 I've asked Grok every morning for calibrated odds that the Persian Gulf returns to functional normality after the 2026 Iran War. I don't mean a ceasefire. I mean shipping, insurance, exports, force posture, and no new escalation triggers all clearing together.

Horizons run from 1 week to 18 months. Each run starts from reference classes, then updates on current evidence, then breaks the problem into gates.

Through 21 November (248 runs):
- 18-month odds: 80% to 68% (−12)
- 6-month odds: 45% to 38% (−7)
- 1-year: 65% to 55% (−10)
- 1-week: 2% to 3%

15 November was the softest multi-horizon day in the series (18-month 62%). By 21 November those odds were back to 68%. That is a rebound from the trough, not a return to March.

Spring talks and a mid-year memorandum pushed the curve up for a while. Both faded. Kinetic pauses show up in the forecasts; insurers and carriers usually don't follow. At the same horizon, strike-pause odds stay well above insurance and transit.

Same prompt every day, public dashboard, full series. Not a single-day take.

## Thread / longer caption

1/ Every day since mid-March I've sent Grok the same superforecasting prompt: probabilities that the Persian Gulf is back to functional normality after the 2026 Iran War, at 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, and 18 months.

2/ I made the bar hard on purpose. A ceasefire isn't enough. The prompt wants military pause, proxy restraint, Hormuz security, commercial volumes, war-risk insurance, oil/LNG continuity, market stress, force posture, and no new escalation trigger to clear at the same time.

3/ Structure is outside-in: reference-class base rates, then the evidence ledger, then drivers for and against, then sub-forecasts for strikes, transit, insurance, and so on, then the final horizon probabilities. I keep that structure fixed so day-to-day comparisons mean something.

4/ I've kept it going for 248 daily runs, from 18 March to 21 November 2026. About eight months of forecasting through an active conflict.

5/ Medium and long horizons are still below where they started in March. Most of the absolute drop is in the longer horizons. Near-term odds never really left the bottom. The late-April and June lifts reversed. Mid-November marked a trough; the following week partially reversed it.

6/ The story inside the runs changed too. Early ones leaned on war ending, escorts, and attrition. Later ones lean on failed settlement and commercial lag: partial oil at elevated cost, deniable harassment, and normality stuck behind deal quality and insurance stickiness rather than the next pause headline.

7/ The gates chart is the part I keep coming back to. At six months, strike-reduction odds stay high while insurance and transit stay low. A military pause is necessary. It isn't sufficient.
